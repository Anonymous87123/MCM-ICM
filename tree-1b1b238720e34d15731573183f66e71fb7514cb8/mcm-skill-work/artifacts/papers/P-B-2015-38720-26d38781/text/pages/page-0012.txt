      2.6 Search Planes and Sensors
The most ancient method for detection of lost objects is visual. Besides the obvious
inefficiency of searching thousands of square miles of ocean by vision alone, the
                                                                           [12]​
model for visual search accuracy is related to the inverse cube of distance​   . For this
reason, we will ignore visual search as both too ineffective and too complex to model.

The two most common types of search planes are military aircraft—the older P-3
                                 [13]​
Orion and the new P-8 Poseidon​      . The Orion carries revolving multi-mode radar for
broad sweeps to detect debris as well as infrared and acoustic sensors for smaller
        [13]​
searches​   . For the modern plane, data on its sensing capabilities is classified, but
we can find specifications for a much older model that the current sensors are based
  [14]​
on​   .

Usually, once the radar search discovers likely debris, a more focused infrared scan
will cover the surrounding area to spot the residual heat signature of the submerged
      [15]​
plane​    .​For the sake of simplification in our first model, we will focus on the
radar search and imagine that it is an ideal sensor with 100% effective
probability of detection and no range uncertainty.
