                                            III.   Assumptions

         A.    Model Assumptions
              • The signal is not attenuated by rain, fog, and interactions with molecules in the lower
                                                                                              8
                atmosphere. These losses are only significant for frequencies above 1 GHz.
              • Ground interference is not a significant because the signal is high above theEarth’s
                surface for most of its path and the interference from the ground is viewed as a
                                                      5
                minimal impairment until reflection.
              • The signal is not polarized and the frequency remains the same during travel. These
                                                                                                      5
                factors are not as useful for long-distance radio models compared to free space loss.
              • The ionosphere reaches from 80 km to 2000 km as a spherical shell above a spherical
                                                                     13
                Earth for increased accuracy compared to flat models. The signal only begins to
                be refracted from bottom of the ionosphere.

              • The ionosphere follows the Reference Standard Ionosphere data from NASA at a
                                                                                              13
                given epoch, so the global data provides information about both night and day.
              • When modeling the behavior of a radio wave bouncing off of water or land, every
                bounce will occur on that specified terrain. The coordinates that we use are
                only to determine the ionosphere data location and the distance travelled
                on the Earth.

              • The ocean surface is stationary compared to the speed of light. The signal reflects
                off of fixed wave shapes.

              • A calm ocean has wave heights of less than 1 m, while a rough ocean can have swells
                from 1 to 10 m. A super-turbulent ocean can have a peak swell of 15 m, based on
                                                                                   14
                National Weather Service wave forecasts for a fully-developed sea.
              • The land consists of topographical features without any surface coverings, to simplify
                computations.

              • A flat land surface is entirely smooth, while a mountainous region may have sharp
                altitude deviations in the tens of meters.

              • The transmitting and receiving antennae are dipole 1 4 wavelength antennae with
