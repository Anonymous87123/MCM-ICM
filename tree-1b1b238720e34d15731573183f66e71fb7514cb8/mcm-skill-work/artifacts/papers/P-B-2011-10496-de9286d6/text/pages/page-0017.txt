    That example was a little bit long, so let us review the core idea. The
location of each pair of repeaters is associated with two privacy lines, one
ingoing and one outgoing. Outgoing repeaters (A-B-C-D-E) accept the pri-
vacy line associated with their current location as well as the outgoing pri-
vacy lines associated with locations that they are “on the way” to, from the
perspective of the hub. Ingoing repeaters also accept the ingoing privacy
lines associated with repeaters that they are “on the way” to. This includes
locations on the way to the hub on their subnetwork as well as those re-
peaters on other subnetworks. Effectively, users are free to communicate
within their subnetworks without having to worry about affecting broad-
casts in other subnetworks. The net result is a reduction of interference and
an increase in the network’s overall capacity.



4.5   K-Means Clustering
One of the weaknesses of the naive algorithm is its failure to adapt to the
population distribution that it is presented with. Heuristically speaking,
the best way to cover as many users as possible with as few repeaters as
possible is to center repeaters in areas with high population density. This
approach is especially effective in realistic population distributions since
users are likely to be clumped together in regions of high density.
