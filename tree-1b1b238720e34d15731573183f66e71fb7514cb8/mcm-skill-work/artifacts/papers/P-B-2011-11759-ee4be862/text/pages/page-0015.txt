7     Case Studies

We developed two population distributions to test our models on. The two cases are: a city-
like user distribution and a rural distribution with small towns of users. When we discuss
“parity,” we refer to the ±600 kHz difference in the recieving and broadcast frequencies of
the repeaters. The graphs that show these assignments represent each open line repeater as a
node, each labeled with “+” or “-” accordingly. We assign parity to each repeater such that
no one repeater is connected to the rest of the network solely through another repeater with
identical parity. This prevents a signal being either stepped up or stepped down repeatedly
such that it eventually falls outside the available frequency range and cannot be recieved.

The parameters for these case studies were set to values we deemed reasonable based on our
research from our referenced sources.
