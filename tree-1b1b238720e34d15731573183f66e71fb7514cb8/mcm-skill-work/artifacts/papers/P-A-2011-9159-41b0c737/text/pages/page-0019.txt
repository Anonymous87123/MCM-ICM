    We claim that a similar hanging height above the deck on each side of the
halfpipe is a sign for good ϕ. The reason is as the follows. In a round of snowboard
contest, the player will perform 6-7 tricks on both sides of the halfpipe. Thus small
ϕ will result in the condition that the player never takes air. Also, too large ϕ
will lead to higher and higher hanging height, which is dangerous for the player.
Therefore, we look for ϕ∗ that makes the same hang height above the deck on both
sides. The initial hang height above the ground hi is still assumed to be 9.0, cause
the halfpipe is symmetric, ϕ∗ should make the final height above the ground hf
equals to 9.0m. We calculate the hf − ϕ relationship in Fig. 19, fit the data by
using cubic spline interpolation and finally get the point intersected by the hf − ϕ
curve and the line hf = 9.0m. Thus ϕ∗ is captured, which is 17.1o .
    The corresponding (a∗ , b∗ ) for ϕ∗ = 17.1o is (4.42m, 5.4m).


6     Tailors and Tradeoffs
    Above analysis is purely theoretical, based on some ideal assumptions. In the
real game, the player is not a mass point without rotation, and the goal is not
simply to reach the highest point. Practical situations are much more complicated
and more factors should be taken into consideration. So we have to tailor the
halfpipe to optimize various requirements.
