5     Application in 3D Case
    Now we move our study to a more reasonable and complex case involving the
third dimension of the halfpipe. In fact, the halfpipe does not lie horizontally, but
is oriented downhill with a slope angle ϕ. In this way, the sliding is not a pure
energy loss process, but can gain energy from the gravity. Therefore, the third
dimension will alter the velocity of the snowboarder, which will bring changes to
a∗ and b∗ .
    Fig. 16 is our sketch for a 3-dimensional halfpipe. For a standard 18 FT halfpipe,
the length of the halfpipe L is about 100-150m, and for professional snowboarders,
there will be 6-7 tricks in a round. Therefore, in a whole trick, the player will travel
about 20m to the downhill direction, which is defined as l.
