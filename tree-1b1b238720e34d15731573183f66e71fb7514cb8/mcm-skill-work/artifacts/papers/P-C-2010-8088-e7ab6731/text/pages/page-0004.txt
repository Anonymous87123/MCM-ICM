When polyethylene is exposed to ultraviolet light (UV) with a of 344 nm, C-C single bonds are
cleaved and free radicals are formed to react quickly with O2 to form peroxy radicals. The peroxy
radicals then either continue a chain reaction of radical formation or two free radicals can react to
terminate the chain reaction1,7,12 . The pathway of free radical chain reactions and termination
reactions can be seen in Figure 3.

An integral part of the model is the cleavage of C-C single bonds to break polyethylene into
fragments. This accounts for the loss of mass as the polyethylene is degraded over time. The rate of
degradation can be estimated by assuming that every time a C-C bond is cleaved by UV light, a
monomer is removed from the original mass of polyethylene. The rate at which the C-C bonds can
be cleaved is dependent on the amount of UV light emitted by the sun in watts/m2 which is a unit of
irradiance. See Figure 5 for unit conversion1,7,12.
