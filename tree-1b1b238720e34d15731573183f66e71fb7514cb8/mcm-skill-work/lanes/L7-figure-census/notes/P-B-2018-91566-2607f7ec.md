## Templates this paper hands you

**Which section takes which exhibit.** A model-construction section takes a schematic
(3.1.3 -> Figure 1's transfer network, 3.4 -> Figure 2's three-nation panel) and, at the
point where the parameters are fitted, a 3x3 numeric block (Table 1) with no headings.
The notations section takes the one 18x2 symbol list. Result sections take chart pairs
only: 4.2 gets the absolute stacked areas (3+4), 4.3 gets the proportion pair (5+6), the
rank pair (7+8) and the map pair (9+10) in that order - quantity, share, order, space.
Sensitivity gets one three-panel strip. The appendix is ANOVA output, nothing else.

**Three-dimensional condition.** It never fires, and neither does a colourbar. The
strongest case in the paper for one - the English 2018/2068 choropleths - prints no
legend, no scale bar and no country labels at all, so the shade is the whole encoding and
a reader cannot calibrate it. Copy the flatness, not that.

**Table type against data scale.** Three rows by three columns is a fitted-parameter
print: no header, no caption outside the image, read as one block. Two columns by
eighteen rows is the symbol list, and it is the only table in the paper typeset rather
than screenshotted. Twelve rows by three columns is an appendix ANOVA run that has
already lost its header to a page break. Two rows by three columns is the tail of such a
run. Four rows by three columns is the shortest appendix table and it is the only one
that looks like a reading rather than a dump.

**Caption template.** `Figure N: <what is counted> in <year>`, colon mandatory, and the
year is the only quantity anywhere near the artwork - no axis in the paper carries a
title or a unit, so the caption's year is load-bearing. Tables use `Table N: <noun
phrase>` and nothing else. When two figures share one pasted image, the two captions are
printed on one line with no separator (`Figure 3: ... 2068 Figure 4: ... 2068`), and the
authors will even let them collide (`...Proportions inFigure 6:`) and wrap a stray year
onto the next line - quote the line as printed, do not tidy it.

**Context-weld template.** Lead: `<exhibit number> and figure <n> indicates the <change>,
and Figure <n> and figure <n> indicates the <change>.` then the pair; the pointer names
both members of the pair in one sentence before the artwork. Trail: one sentence of
finding, `<Only/in this way> <the one thing that moved> is replaced by <the one thing that
arrived>`, and then the next pair is opened by `To see how <quantity> changes, we plot the
top 15 rank of ...`. Charts that get no trail are the ones the caption already settles.
