## Templates this paper hands you

**Which section takes which exhibit.** One chart per step, and the chart always sits
at the END of the paragraph that argues for it: the PSR schematic opens section 3.1
before any mathematics, the indicator hierarchy (drawn as a table) opens 3.2.1 before
the bullet list that repeats it, the causal graph closes section 3.3, the validation
scatter closes 3.4, then the two DRC curves close 4.1.1 and 4.1.2, the Bangladesh curve
4.2.1, the forecast 4.2.2, the continent transposition closes section 5, and the
sensitivity chart closes section 6. The reusable rule is that a picture is the last
sentence of an argument, never the first, and the two structural pictures (PSR, the
indicators) come before any numbers exist while every later picture shows a computed
quantity. The part not to copy: the two tables in 4.2.3 and 4.2.4 arrive without a
caption, a number or a title, so the paper's busiest section is its least legible one.

**When three dimensions appear.** Never, in 27 pages and nine charts - and the paper
substitutes a trick worth naming: it crops the y axis to a fraction of a unit. Four
charts of the same fragility index run on 0.30-0.36, 0.28-0.44, 0.32-0.42 and
0.35-0.65, so the DRC upturn that the argument rests on fills the frame while the same
change on the Bangladesh chart is a wiggle. No colourbar appears anywhere, so nothing
here needs the crop check that #164's 3-D scatter did. Copy the discipline (flat,
labelled axes, two series at most); do not copy the window, which is how a small change
is made to look like a trend - and if two charts show the same quantity, fix one scale
for both.

**Table type against data scale.** Three grades and no consistent mapping. Five or
fewer numbers go into a boxed or booktabs grid with no header unit - Table 2's five
regression lines, Table 4's three continent scores, the six-intervention map. Twenty
rows need a boxed frame with internal verticals and no caption at all (Appendix A). The
two largest grids, 11x13 and 22x5, are the ones that get rotated sub-columns, merged
super-headers, shading and a source line - and the ones mislabelled as figures. What is
worth copying: the super-header span for grouped years, the source line under the frame,
the unit printed once for the whole block. What is not: precision that drifts inside a
column (1735.9 to 8530.6122), decimal points printed as colons, the same stub label
'Rank' twice, and a country list in which one code appears twice.

**Caption template.** `<Label> <number>: <bare noun phrase>` - "Figure 2: Indicators",
"Figure 5: FI of Congo", "Table 4: FI of different continents" - 3 to 6 words, one
sentence, voice `none` on all eighteen rows, and 19 words only where the caption has to
carry a key the chart forgot ("Blue circles represent the results of PSA, and red
crosses rep-resent the results of FSI"). Figures always below, tables eight below and
one above. Three faults to remember rather than copy: the key sentence has no space
after its colon and keeps the hyphen of a line break; the sensitivity caption and its
legend both lost the Greek symbol, so the figure is captioned "Analysis of 1"; and the
two appendix grids are captioned "Figure 10" and "Figure 11" while their own pasted
titles read "Table 2.6" and "Table 2" - a caption that names the wrong object type is
worse than no caption, because the pointer layer then has nothing to point at.

**Context-weld template.** Seven of eighteen exhibits are pointed at by number, and the
pointers are the ones a reader can follow: the sentence states the finding, then names
the exhibit - "From Figure 4, we can clearly see that the result is relatively
consistent with the trend of the ranking", "As Figure 8 shows, BD will be stable in 60
years, namely 2076", "From Table 3,Our model's classification results are mostly
consistent with FSI". The other eleven are introduced only positionally ("shown in the
figure below", "as shown in the following table", "as followed:"), which works inside
one page and fails across a page turn. The one backward reference in the paper is also
its most useful habit: Table 3 is read on p16 although its grid opens on p15, and the
grid's continuation across the fold carries no repeated header, no "continued" mark and
no caption. If you break a table over a page, repeat the header; if you cite an exhibit
by position, put the number in as well.
