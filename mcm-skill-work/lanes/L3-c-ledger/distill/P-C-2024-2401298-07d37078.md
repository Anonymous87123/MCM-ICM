## 5. Reusable writing action

**Define a contested term by equivalence, then invert both sides to get the measurable.**

The chapter needs `momentum` to be a number, but the term is contested, so it never defines it directly. It writes
the negation as a statement the paper has already tested, sets the two against each other with a literal
double-arrow, and then takes the inverse of both sides in one sentence:

> `It's easy for us to observe the equivalence between no momentum statement and`
> `⇐⇒ memoryless statement. "Momentum doesn't exist"`

> `Now by inversing both sides, we can measure the momentum effect as: E := S − Ŝ (11)`

and then admits the move out loud, which is what makes it usable rather than merely clever:

> `Since no strict definition of momentum exists as a statistic. We now use formula (11) to define the value of momentum.`

Why it works: the left-hand side of the equivalence was already tested in section 4 (Ljung-Box and runs test), so
by the time the reader meets `E := S − Ŝ` the quantity has a null hypothesis behind it rather than a definition
in front of it. The cost of that move is one sentence of honesty - the paper says the definition is a choice - and
the payoff is that equation (11) inherits the section-4 result for free.
