**Choose a model by one named criterion, then spend one more sentence proving the chosen model passes a test it could have failed.** The chapter does it twice, in the same shape, and the shape is what transfers: candidates named with their orders, a single selection rule with its number, a verdict sentence, then a diagnostic whose failure would have overturned the verdict. Verbatim, blocks 76-78:

> "Test result shows that the p-values of the LB statistics are all above the threshold of 0.05, so the model passed the test and we can use ARIMA(0,1,1) to explain the variation of the number of reported results."

and the selection it ratifies, in the same unit: "so we can choose ARIMA(0,1,1)as the target model." Three candidate orders are on the table before that sentence - the order read from `Figure 6`, and the two `auto.arima` results ARIMA(1,1,1) and ARIMA(3,1,1) - and the rule is the AIC. The second use is the white-noise gate of §7.1, where the Box-Pierce test is reported the same way and the seven p-values all exceed 0.05.

Why it is worth copying: the pair of sentences does two different jobs, and most papers do only the first. The selection sentence is unfalsifiable on its own - any criterion can be declared decisive - so the check sentence is what makes the choice auditable, because it names a threshold (`0.05`), a quantity (`the p-values of the LB statistics`, plural, so the reader knows more than one lag was tested) and a direction of failure. The reader can therefore disagree with the conclusion without doubting the evidence.

Two limits of the pattern as this chapter uses it, worth fixing when copying: the criterion value is never printed - no AIC appears anywhere in the chapter, only the words "the AIC" - so a reader cannot recompute the ranking that selected ARIMA(0,1,1); and the check is applied only to the winner, so nothing rules out that a rejected candidate would also have passed its own LB test. Print the criterion table, and check all three, and the same two sentences become a complete audit trail.
