## 5. Reusable writing action

**Announce the whole pipeline as one sentence with a countable number of steps, then open each step with the same label - and make sure the label's counter is global, not per-section.**

The action is a single 118-word block on leaf 6 (block 93) that names the paper's entire method before any model appears, and then three labelled blocks that keep the promise:

> In order to predict the number of medals for each country at the 2028 Summer Olympics in Los Angeles, in particular the number of gold medals and the total number of medals, the first step is to construct a machine learning regression prediction model that can make predictions with full consideration of multiple features ... The second step is to obtain prediction intervals for medals through a sampling method, and the third step is to analyse the results to explore which countries are likely to improve or decline.

and the labels, each welded to the end of the block before it:

> `⋇Step 1: Principle of Stacking` ... `⋇Step 2: Base Models 1. LightGBM (LGBM)` ... `⋇Step 3: Final Stacking Model Integration`

Why it is reusable: a reader who has been told there are three steps, and who then meets a `⋇Step n` label at each one, always knows both where they are and how much is left - which is exactly the information a 25-page competition paper with four tasks and six sections cannot otherwise give cheaply. The sentence also does the load-bearing definitional work in the same breath (`the number of gold medals and the total number of medals`), so the labels never have to re-explain the target.

Why it fails here, in a way worth copying the fix of: the paper runs **three separate counters through one document**. The `⋇Step` series has three members (1, 2, 3) and belongs to the model-building section; the bootstrap section then restarts at `Step 1` and runs to `Step 4` in `results` blocks 156-162; the PSM-DID section restarts again at `Step 1` and runs to `Step 4` in the chapter's own blocks 230-262. So `Step 3` names three different things on three different pages, and `Step 4` two. A reusable version keeps the announcement exactly as written - one sentence, a number of steps, the target named - and then numbers the labels globally (`Step 1`-`Step 3` for the pipeline, `Step 3.1`-`Step 3.4` for the bootstrap inside it), so that the count the reader was promised is the count they can actually check.
