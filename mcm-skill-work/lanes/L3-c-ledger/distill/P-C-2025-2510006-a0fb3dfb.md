## 5. Reusable writing action

**State the branch condition with its number, run both branches on the same page, and defer the number's defence to the sensitivity section in the sentence that introduces it.**

The action has three moves and they sit in three consecutive sentences on printed page 7 (leaf 8, blocks 71-72 and 73-75):

> To explore whether the number of medals in consecutive Olympic Games is related to the year, we propose different prediction algorithms. If the medal count shows a strong correlation with the year, we use linear regression to predict. If the correlation is weak, we use the average of the data as the prediction.
>
> We use 0.5 as the threshold for determining strong or weak correlation (other thresholds will be considered in subsequent sensitivity analyses).

and then the rule itself is printed as one piecewise expression rather than as two models in two subsections:

> a-hat j = { sum of a jk over k = 1..6 / 6,  |r| < 0.5 ;  max{0, a-hat j},  otherwise }

Why it is reusable: the reader is told which of two algorithms will run, on what statistic, at what cut, and in what order - before either algorithm appears - so the piecewise expression is legible on first sight and the later `max{0, ...}` clamp reads as a consequence of the weak branch rather than as an unexplained repair. Putting the cut in the same parenthesis as the promise (`other thresholds will be considered in subsequent sensitivity analyses`) also converts the most obvious reviewer objection - why 0.5 and not 0.3 - into a scheduled section instead of a hole. The paper does keep the promise: printed page 22 (leaf 23, block 194) reopens with `we initially assumed a confidence interval of 90% and a value of 0.5 for parameter r, which is the threshold for determining strong or weak correlation`, and Table 9 then reports every country at `r = 0.3, 0.4, 0.5, 0.6, 0.7`.

What the same passage costs, and where a writer should not copy it:

- **The promise is kept twelve pages later and the number does not travel with it.** Print 10 builds the 2028 United States forecast and reports `We predict the United States will win 54.57 gold, 52.16 silver, and 32.37 bronze medals, totaling 139 medals` (block 104). Table 9 on print 22 reports the same country as `139` total but `55` gold at every `r`, and block 196 calls that `very stable`: the total agrees, the gold does not, and the 0.43 gap is never mentioned. A deferred defence is only worth writing if the deferred table reproduces every figure it is defending, not just the one in the headline.
- **The branch is visible in the sensitivity table and is reported as a finding instead of as the rule firing.** Table 9 prints `USA 139 139 139 139 139` and `RUS 60 60 60 60 60` flat across `r = 0.3 ... 0.7` against `CHN 96 94 87 83 77` falling across the same range, which is exactly what a `0.5` cut produces - a flat row means the average branch was taken at every `r`, a falling row means the regression branch was taken at every `r` - yet block 196 reads the flat rows as `very stable` and the falling row as a `trend of predicted Chinese medals`, so a table that displays the threshold's effect is never connected back to the rule it tests.
- **The branch is stated for one response and silently reused for four.** The rule is announced for `the number of medals`, then applied to gold, silver, bronze and total with the same `0.5`, and the `max{0, ...}` clamp is justified only as `Since this value may be negative` (block 75) - correct for a count, but the clamp is then carried into the new-event Monte Carlo weights without comment.

The move to copy is the ordered announcement: condition, statistic, cut, promise of a later defence, then the piecewise formula. The move to avoid is deferring the defence far enough that the defending table is computed by then-current code or data rather than by the run that produced the headline - here `136.63` and `53.64` on print 7 become `139` and `55` on print 22 with no note, so the section that answers the objection also silently contradicts the number that raised it.
