## 5. Reusable writing action

**Define one discretisation rule, then spend it twice: once to make the quantity testable and once to make it predictable.**

The chapter has a continuous momentum value `M` from equation (1) and needs it in two incompatible forms - a chi-square test wants categories, and a classifier wants a label. Instead of inventing two thresholds it writes a single comparison rule and then says, explicitly, that the second use is the first one re-used:

> Currently, we possess the initial momentum values of Player 1 and Player 2, denoted as Mp1i and Mp2i, correspondingly, which are related by the following transformation equation ...

> By employing this discretization technique, we can obtain a crude depiction of the game's fluctuation pattern. Thus, we can consider it the dependent variable in our forecast, referred to as MS ...

> The equation (7) can be used to express this technique.

Why it works: equation (7) compares the two players' momenta rather than thresholding either one (`MP′1i = 1 if MP1i > MP2i`), so the rule needs no units, no scale and no calibration - which is exactly why it survives the move from the test to the model with one cross-reference and no re-derivation. The writer then names the output twice, `MS` for the forecast and the categorical variable for the test, and the reader can see they are the same object.

How to reuse: when a chapter needs one quantity in both a statistical test and a learning model, write the discretisation as a comparison between two of your own quantities (not against a constant), cite it by number at the second use instead of restating it, and give the second use its own symbol while saying it is the same transformation.

What it costs, and the chapter does not say so: a relational label flips every time the two players' momenta cross, so the label changes `from 1 to 0 or from 0 to 1 in two consecutive moments` by construction. That makes the classifier's target a function of the model's own previous output, and the one sentence that mentions the flip - `The dependent variable can be explained as a dichotomous variable that changes from 1 to 0 or from 0 to 1 in two consecutive moments.
