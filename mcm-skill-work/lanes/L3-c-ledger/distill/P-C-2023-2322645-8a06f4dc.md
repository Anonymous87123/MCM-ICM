## 5. Reusable writing action

**When you invent a measure, let the property you want pick the average, then pick the transform that makes that average computable.**

Block 69 does it in four sentences, and every sentence is a reason rather than a definition:

> `The motivation for choosing this value as opposed to ft(g) directly is that Subset Entropy will aim to quantify the average information following the first guess when word t is the target, and hence will need to take an average over some quantification of information. As ft effectively measures a multiplicative factor of information (rather than additive), taking a geometric rather than arithmetic mean would be more appropriate. However, as the average of the log value of some terms exactly corresponds to the log of the geometric mean of these terms, we can effectively instead consider the expectation of It(g) over some distribution of guesses D as a measure of the geometric mean of the factors. With this justification in hand, we have the following formulation of Subset Entropy:`

Why it works. (1) The measure's job is stated before its formula (`quantify the average information following the first guess`), so the reader knows what the formula has to do. (2) The observable is named honest - `ft` is multiplicative, and the paper says so instead of averaging it arithmetically and hoping. (3) The switch from geometric mean to an expectation of logs is justified by an identity, not by convenience: the average of logs is the log of the geometric mean, so the awkward mean becomes an ordinary expectation and the measure becomes estimable by sampling. (4) The formula arrives last, as the conclusion of the argument, in the exact form the argument licensed - `e(t) = Eg∼D[It(g)]` - and the very next block says what `D` is.

What to copy hardest is the order: what the measure is for, what the quantity naturally is, which average that forces, which identity makes that average cheap, then the formula. Most papers print the formula first and defend the averaging choice, if at all, in a sentence the reader has already passed.

What not to copy: the measure is never validated. Nothing in the chapter correlates Subset Entropy with observed difficulty, compares it to Positional Entropy, or shows a single word's value of it - and its only downstream use is as one of four Lasso survivors whose coefficient is never printed. A chapter using this move should close it by showing the new measure doing something the old one does worse, because a derivation that ends at its own formula leaves the reader unable to tell an invention from a notation.
