## 5. Reusable writing action

**Action: open a method section by killing the default choice with one quantified fact, then naming the replacement and the two properties it needs - three sentences, in that order, before any mathematics.**

The chapter's whole model-selection argument is one block:

> Although neural network models have become increasingly popular in recent years, this model usually requires a large amount of data for training. A dataset with only 400 or so data is not a good place to consider a neural network model.

> On balance, we decided to use the Prophet model, an algorithm based on an additive model for predicting time series data with characteristics such as seasonality, trends, and holidays. Also, Prophet[4] has strong robustness to handle problems such as non-stationary time series and outliers. For this dataset, Prophet is a good choice.

Why it works: the first sentence names the reader's default and concedes its popularity, which stops the chapter looking uninformed; the second sentence converts the concession into a number - `400 or so data` - and lets the number do the rejecting. The replacement is then introduced by the frame `an algorithm based on an additive model for predicting time series data with characteristics such as seasonality, trends, and holidays`, which is a definition and a promise of fit in one clause, followed by the two properties the dataset actually needs (`non-stationary`, `outliers`) and a verdict sentence that names the dataset rather than the method. A reader who reads only these three sentences knows what was rejected, why, what replaced it, and on what grounds.

**The caveat, and it is the instructive part:** the rejection is by data volume alone, and the replacement is then fitted to the same 400 rows and asked for an interval `for seventy days after January 1, 2023` - five times the length of the segment it was fitted on after the May 5 screen. So the quantified rejection is what makes the paragraph persuasive and also what makes it hollow: `400 or so data is not a good place to consider a neural network model` is equally an argument against any model with free parameters, and this one carries nine hyperparameters and a default prior. Copy the three-sentence shape; check that the number you reject with is not also a number your own choice cannot survive.
