## 5. Reusable writing action

Make each step of a procedure end on the panel that belongs to it, in the order name-point-print, and start the next
step immediately after that panel's caption. The chapter does it three times: "Step 1: Decide what information can pass
through the cell state. This decision is controlled by the "forget gate" layer through sigmoid, it will pass or partially
pass according to the output of the previous moment. See Figure 5 for details:" - then the caption `Figure 5: Step 1 of
LSTM` - then "Step 2: Generate the new information we need to update." The step name, the imperative, the pointer and the
caption are adjacent, so a reader who is skimming can walk the three steps by their captions alone.

What the move buys is a locatable procedure: the reader always knows which picture he is on, the numbering of the steps
and of the figures advance together, and the whole explanation can be re-read as a strip of three panels. It also makes
the write-up cheap to extend - a fourth step is one paragraph plus one caption - which is why this pattern is everywhere
in student papers.

The failure is that the payload is left inside the panel. Each step's equation (`f_t` in Figure 5, `C_t` in Figure 6,
`o_t` and `h_t` in Figure 7) is drawn inside the graphic and never numbered, and the caption that would let a reader cite
it is welded to the tail of the preceding paragraph rather than standing alone. The result is a procedure that can be
followed visually but cannot be referenced, audited or transcribed: the two numbered displays in the chapter are the
ones nobody needs to look up, and the four that carry the model have no identity at all. Name-point-print works only if
the pointed-at payload also gets a name of its own.
