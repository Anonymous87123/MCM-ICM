## 5. Reusable writing action

Give every feature two paragraphs in a fixed order: first the sentence that says why the feature should matter, then the
sentence that says how it was measured, with the data source named in the second. The chapter does it four times -
word frequency, letter frequency, repetition, part of speech - and it is the whole of §5's method. Three of the eight
paragraphs, verbatim:

"When attempting Wordle puzzles, words that are more commonly used in everyday language are often easier for people to
recall, such as "study" and "train.""

"Letter frequency,fLetter is obtained by adding up the usage frequency of each letter in the words. The data on letter
frequency is obtained from the Google Books Ngram Viewer[4], which includes a corpus of books and other publications
from 1500 to 2008 with high reliability."

"We used the popular Python natural language processing toolkit Natural Language Toolkit[5], which contains various text
processing and language analysis tools, including Part-of-Speech Tagging."

The move earns its place for a reason that has nothing to do with elegance: a rationale paragraph and a measurement
paragraph can be written and checked independently, by different people, and the measurement paragraph is where the
source, the corpus window and the tool version live. It is also what makes a feature set auditable - the reader can
disagree with a rationale without having to suspect the data, and can check the data without having to believe the
rationale.

The failure is that the two paragraphs are never joined back up. Each rationale states a direction: common words are
recalled first, so the try distribution skews low; frequent letters are hit more often, so words with high letter
frequency take fewer tries; a repeated letter gives a clue, so repetition should lower the number of attempts. None of
the four measurements restates that direction, none of the four readings computes it, and by the time the numbers appear
- `-0.3165`, `-0.4238`, `+0.130037`, `+0.024991` - the direction is no longer in the sentence that quotes them, because
the tables print `Median difference1`, `Median difference2`, `Median difference3` without saying which group is
subtracted from which. A rationale is a prediction with a sign in it. Write the sign into the reading's own sentence
("words with repeated letters score 0.13 higher"), or the two-paragraph template produces four features nobody can say
they confirmed.
