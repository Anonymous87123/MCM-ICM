Arithmetic that closes:

- The no-trade window is consistent with the model's own look-back: `the ﬁrst 10 trading days of gold(that is, the
  ﬁrst 14 trading days of bitcoin)` from September 11 to September 24, 2016 is ten weekdays and fourteen calendar days,
  and the initial portfolio `[x_c, x_g, x_b] = [1000, 0, 0]` is stated at September 25, the first day after it.
- The headline `$14,325.13 on September 10, 2021` from `$1,000` is 14.3 times, which supports `more than 13 times of
  the initial investment` without overstating it.
- The risk denominator matches the sample formula: `S_g = √(Σ_{j=1}^{10}(r_{gj} − r̄_{gj})²/9)` with
  `r̄_{gj} = (1/10)Σ r_{gj}`, ten observations and `n − 1 = 9`.
- The commission treatment is at least self-consistent: buys scale by `(1 − α)` and sells by `1/(1 − α)` in all six
  programmes, which is the right direction for a fee on the amount transacted in either direction.
- Behaviour v and vi differ from i to iv exactly where the trading calendar differs: `Δx_g = 0` and a one-asset
  objective, which is what a non-gold-trading day allows.

Arithmetic and definition that do not close:

- The text promises five parameters and the table delivers three. Block 109 says `we adjust the values of these 5
  parameters separately`, naming `l`, `d`, `d′`, `λ`, `ϵ`; Table 3 has three columns headed `d`, `d′`, `λ`. Hidden size
  and epochs are never varied, so the claim that `changes in the hidden layer size l ... and the value of epoch ϵ will
  have an impact` is untested by the chapter's only experiment.
- The scan's minimum is the seed in every column: `0.007261552` appears at `d = 10`, at `d′ = 5` and at `λ = 0.001`,
  which is exactly the initial combination `[l = 100, d = 10, d′ = 5, λ = 0.001, ϵ = 50]`. Block 115 then reports that
  minimum as if it were a discovery and adopts the seed as the tuned setting, so the search costs a table and returns
  its own starting point.
- The MAPE is computed on gold only and transferred to bitcoin by assertion: `since we believe that both prediction of
  gold price and bitcoin price reach the best result under the same parameter combination`. No bitcoin column, no
  bitcoin sentence, and Figure 4(b) is the first time the bitcoin fit appears.
- The risk term is signed and can go negative. (10) is `R = (x′_g − x_g)S_g + (x′_b − x_b)S_b`, and for a sale
  `x′_g − x_g = Δx_g/(1 − α_g) < 0`, so the programme that minimises `R` is paid for selling. In behaviour iv
  (`Δx_g ≤ 0, Δx_b ≤ 0`) both terms are negative and `min R` pushes holdings to their constraint limits - the model
  treats liquidation as risk reduction below zero.
- The risk has the wrong dimensions for its job. `S_g` is the standard deviation of the gold price level over ten days,
  and (10) multiplies it by a change in dollar holdings; price weight times dollars is not a risk, and no scaling by
  holdings means a large portfolio and a small one carry the same `S`.
- (11) multiplies the predicted growth rate by the change in holdings, not the holdings: `P = (r̂_{g,t+1} −
  r_{g,t})/r_{g,t} · (x′_g − x_g) + ...`. A position carried over unchanged earns nothing in this measure, so `P`
  scores trades rather than a portfolio - and `max P` is therefore indifferent between holding a profitable position
  and selling it and re-buying.
- The guard (13) throws away the second objective. It compares projected total values,
  `x′_c + (r̂_{g,t+1}/r_{g,t})x′_g + (r̂_{b,t+1}/r_{b,t})x′_b < x_c + (r̂_{g,t+1}/r_{g,t})x_g + (r̂_{b,t+1}/r_{b,t})x_b`,
  and keeps the portfolio if the change loses money; `R` never enters. A behaviour chosen for its return cannot be
  vetoed for its risk at the last step, which is the step the paper actually runs.
- The two ranges in block 161 disagree: `model i-iv` for gold trading days is right, `model v-iv` for the others is
  not - the non-trading behaviours are v and vi, and `iv` is repeated from the previous range.
- Behaviours v and vi drop their own defining condition from the constraint set: the block says `Δx_g = 0,Δx_b > 0` but
  the `s.t.` rows read `Δx_b < x_c` and `Δx_b > 0` only, so nothing in the programme forbids trading gold.
- Both the guard and behaviour i use strict `<`: `Δx_g + Δx_b < x_c` and the (13) comparison exclude equality, so a
  portfolio that exactly spends its cash, or exactly ties after commission, is not representable - and a tie flips to
  "change", which is the expensive branch under a 1%/2% fee.
- `x′_c` is used in (12) and (13) and never defined in words, and `x′_g`, `x′_b` appear only as left-hand sides of the
  substitution braces; the notation paragraph defines `[x_c, x_g, x_b]` and stops.
- `MAPE` is introduced by the acronym only (`MAPE(Mean Absolute Percentage Error)`) and (8) prints the residual under
  norm bars, `‖r_{g,t} − r̂_{g,t}‖`, rather than an absolute-value pair - harmless for a scalar, but the symbol then does
  not tell a reader that (8) is a mean of absolute percentage errors, and no value of `n` is ever given.
- The acronym does not name the method: `MLP(Multi-objective Linear Programming)` is MOLP everywhere else, and `MLP`
  is the standard abbreviation for a multilayer perceptron - a model this paper does not use but a reader will supply.

## 5. Reusable writing action

State each case of a case-split as four fixed moves, and repeat the wording verbatim so the six cases can be diffed
instead of re-read. The chapter says, six times over, "This trading behavior can be noted as Δ푥푔> 0, Δ푥푏> 0", then
"Since the value of 푥′푔−푥푔and 푥′푏−푥푏can be substituted as", then "According to equation (10), (11) and (12), the MLP
model for this trading behavior can be built as:" and a bracketed programme. The condition line, the substitution line and
the citation line are word-for-word identical across i to vi; only the substitution and the two objective rows change.

What the move buys is comparability. A reader who has checked one case has checked the scaffolding of all six, and the
remaining work is exactly the two lines that differ: the sign of each Δ and the fee factor, `(1 − α)` for a purchase and
`1/(1 − α)` for a sale. That is the same reason a reviewer can audit a table of cases faster than six paragraphs of
prose - the invariant parts are stated once in the reader's head and only the deltas are new. It is also why the
chapter's real errors are all in the deltas: behaviour v and vi state `Δx_g = 0` in the condition line and lose it from
the `s.t.` rows, so the one line that is supposed to differ is the one that is missing.

The failure is that the invariant is stated but never used. Six identical "According to equation (10), (11) and (12)"
lines should end with a table comparing the six programmes - which constraint binds in which case, what each objective
degenerates to - and no such comparison is ever printed; the chapter closes by assigning models to days and never puts
the six side by side. Boilerplate without a summary makes a repeated structure look rigorous while leaving the reader to
do the comparison that the structure was built to make cheap.
