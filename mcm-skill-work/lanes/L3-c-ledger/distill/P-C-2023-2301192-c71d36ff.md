## 5. Reusable writing action

Write a hypothesis test as four numbered moves in about 120 words - the two hypotheses, the statistic with its degrees of freedom, the rejection region at a named α, and then one sentence that prints the number, the p-value, the comparison and the verdict in that order. §3.5.2 is the best-built passage in the chapter and it is the only passage in the chapter built this way:

"1. Hypothesis Formulation: Null hypothesis: H0 : β0 = β1 = · · · βm = 0;"

"F = (SSR/m)/(SSE/(n − m − 1)) ∼ F(m, n − m − 1)"

"The rejection region of the test is F > Fα(m, n − m − 1), based on the given significance level of α = 0.05."

"The F-statistic of the regression equation is 1.058 with a corresponding P-value of 0.379 (> α = 0.05), indicating that the regression equation does not exhibit statistical significance."

The move earns its place because the last of those four sentences is auditable on its own. A reader who never sees §3.5.1 can take `1.058`, `0.379`, `α = 0.05` and check the verdict in one step; a reader who disagrees with the model still cannot disagree with the arithmetic. That is rare enough in this genre to be worth stealing, and the template costs four lines: state what you are testing, state the statistic and its distribution, state where you will reject, then state what happened. The first three moves also force the author to have a degrees-of-freedom pair, which is exactly what a reader needs in order to disbelieve the result properly - `(m, n − m − 1)` names both the model size and the sample size in six characters.

The failure is that the verdict is the last thing the chapter does with the test. §3.5.2's `p = 0.379` says word attributes do not explain the hard-mode proportion; §4 changes task, §5.1 classifies difficulty from the try distribution alone, §5.2 goes back to the attributes, and no sentence anywhere in the chapter draws the consequence. Two smaller things go with it: the null hypothesis includes the intercept (`β0 = β1 = · · · βm = 0`, and the second equals sign is missing before `βm`), which tests more than the author means; and the verdict paragraph is not the chapter's - it is blocks 140-143, filed under `results`. A test is a decision, and a decision that the following twenty pages ignore is not a decision but a decoration. Print the template, then let the next section start from its verdict: if the attributes are insignificant, say what the rest of the paper is now allowed to claim about them - and if nothing, say so in the sentence after the p-value, because that sentence is the one a reader will quote.
