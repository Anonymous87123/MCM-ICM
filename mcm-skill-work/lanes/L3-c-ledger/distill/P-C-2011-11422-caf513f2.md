## 5. Reusable writing action

**Convert both sides of a comparison into the same unit, print the ratio as an English phrase, then name
the boundary the ratio hides.** Block 93 does all three in three sentences: "This translates to 1501.3 BTU
needed a mile in an electric vehicle. The average passenger car can travel 31 miles on a gallon of
gasoline. Since a gallon of gasoline contains roughly 116,090 BTU, the average gas car runs on 3744.84 BTU
per mile [1]." Then the verdict, quantified but not left as a number: "This is more than twice as much
energy required by electric cars, so switching to electric cars decreases the amount of energy required
worldwide for transportation purposes, but also requires a switch from the 100% gasoline power source for
gas cars, to the medley of power sources used for electricity." The reusable part is the third clause: the
same sentence that claims the win concedes that the win only moves the energy question from the tank to
the grid, so the comparison never has to be defended later. Two sentences of arithmetic, one sentence of
verdict plus caveat - a ratio a reader can check with a calculator and a limit a reader cannot accuse the
author of hiding.
