## 5. Reusable writing action

**Perform the substitution out loud before running the test.** The chapter has to test a quantity nobody can measure - the coach's claim that a match "swings" - and instead of arguing about it, it writes one sentence that does three things at once: it names who made the claim, it says the claim is being *replaced* by something measurable, and it hands the reader the observable stand-in together with the test that will judge it.

> With an aim to confirm or falsify the coach's conjecture, we have made a synonymous conversion, examining whether the generated time series of athlete performance scores, which could be approximately considered as explicit expression of implicit state, namely athlete's momentum, are random sequences.

Three moves in twenty-eight words: `confirm or falsify` fixes the claim as a hypothesis before any test is named, `synonymous conversion` declares that what follows is a substitute rather than the thing itself, and the apposition `explicit expression of implicit state, namely athlete's momentum` makes the substitution auditable - a reader can reject the equivalence without rejecting the test. The test then arrives as the object of `examining whether`, not as a new topic, which is why the next paragraph can open on the white-noise definition instead of on a transition.

Copy the shape for any task phrase that is unobservable or undefined (`momentum`, `team chemistry`, `market sentiment`, `robustness`): one sentence, `confirm or falsify` + `synonymous conversion` + the observable series + `namely <the original word>` + `examining whether ...`. What the chapter then fails to do is the other half: it never revisits the conversion after the test, so the reader is left holding a rejection of white noise and no sentence that translates it back into a statement about momentum.
