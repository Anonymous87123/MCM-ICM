## 5. Reusable writing action

**Make the blend weight a function of the two models' own errors, then print enough for a reader to recompute the blend.** Verbatim, block 76:

> "In order to improve the accuracy and robustness of the model prediction, we derived a new combined ARIMA-BP prediction model based on ARIMA and BP neural network model by using the prediction errors to weight the two models."

and the weights it then prints: `w_A = PE_B²/(PE_A² + PE_B²) ≈ 0.406, w_B = PE_A²/(PE_A² + PE_B²) ≈ 0.594`, followed by `P_i = w_A P_Ai + w_B P_Bi` as equation (1).

Why it is worth copying: choosing a blend weight is normally where a paper resorts to a subjective number, and this chapter instead makes the weight an inverse-variance weight - the worse model gets the smaller share, automatically. That converts a judgement call into an arithmetic claim a reader can attack. It also leaves two free checks that the chapter happens to pass: the weights sum to 1.000, and the blend reproduces the published forecast, `0.406 × 20598.84 + 0.594 × 19496.05 = 19943.8`, which is the `approximately 19944` the next page reports. A reader who wants to doubt the combination has to doubt the printed inputs, not the author's taste.

Two limits of the pattern as this chapter uses it, worth fixing when copying: `PE_A` and `PE_B` are never printed anywhere, no table of prediction errors appears, and `N` is never given, so the two weights can be checked for consistency but cannot be recomputed from data; and the claim that the combination improves on either model rests on Figures 5 and 6, which print at the same scale with near-identical shapes, so the improvement the weights are supposed to buy is not visible in the evidence offered. Print the error table, and put the three forecasts on one axis, and the same two sentences become a complete audit trail.
