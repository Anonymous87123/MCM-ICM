## 5. Reusable writing action

**Before modelling an outcome, assign it a number and publish the scale.** The great-coach model's opening move is to stop talking about medals and start talking about a score: `We classify the winning capabilities into four levels: 0, Unable to win a medal / 1, Win a bronze medal / 2, Win a silver medal / 3, Win a gold medal`, with the domain declared one block earlier as `(Pn,y,e, ϵn,y,e ∈[0, 3])`. Once that mapping exists, every later sentence is arithmetic: the capability of a country is the sum of its athletes' capability, its coach's contribution and noise - `Pn,y,e = Pn,y,e|a + Pn,y,e|c + ϵn,y,e` - the athletes' part is the mean of the three previous Games - `Pn,y,e|a = (Pn−1,y,e|a + Pn−2,y,e|a + Pn−3,y,e|a) / 3` - and the coach's part is whatever is left, `Pn,y,e|c = Pn,y,e − Pn,y,e|a`. Four lines, one table, and the paper's vaguest phrase ("the great coach effect") has become a subtraction.

The action is worth stealing because it costs almost nothing and it makes the case study checkable. Because the scale is published, the Lang Ping example can be audited in the reader's head: the USA won nothing in 1996, 2000 and 2004 and silver in 2008, so the athletes' baseline for 2008 is 0 and the coach's contribution is 2; the baseline for 2012 is the mean of 2000, 2004 and 2008, which is 0.67, and the observed silver gives 1.33. The chapter prints exactly those two numbers, and a reader who disagrees can say which step is wrong.

**What it costs.** The scale is ordinal dressed as cardinal: gold = 3, silver = 2, bronze = 1 is a ranking, not a measurement, so a coach's contribution of 1.33 - the paper's own number - corresponds to no medal that exists, and the paper averages over Games and over events as if the units were comparable. The uncertainty term is deleted by assumption at the first convenient moment: `For convenience in analysis, we typically assume ϵn,y,e = 0`, which removes the only part of the model that could express a coach's effect varying by event, and the vocabulary then follows - `Pn,y,e|c` is called `the enhancement in winning capability provided by a great coach`, one number for a coach, and 121 has to add a separate rule for coaches who lead several events. The published scale also does not stop the example from failing its own arithmetic: the USA coach contribution is given as `2 and ... 1.33, indicating Lang Ping improved their medal-winning capability by an average of 1.16 across two Olympics`, where the mean of the two printed values is 1.67. A scale only helps if the totals on it are added up correctly, and this one is added up wrong in the sentence that is supposed to demonstrate the method.
