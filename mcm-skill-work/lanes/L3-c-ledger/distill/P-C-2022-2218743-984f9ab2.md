Record: `lanes/L3-c-ledger/records/P-C-2022-2218743-984f9ab2.json`, chapter `model-establishment`,
`pages [7, 16]`, 20 groups, 76 layout blocks.

A chapter that builds two models and finishes with neither. The first half forecasts gold and bitcoin with ARIMA and
then abandons the forecast to argue that trading should be scheduled by moving-average alignment; the second half
optimizes a daily portfolio with NSGA-II and then replaces its own risk measure with a second one. The build is real -
weights, means, a covariance matrix, a constraint system, a Pareto filter - and it is the only long stretch of
mathematics in the paper, 445 words in a single unit. What it never does is hand a number to anything: 23 displayed
equations, none numbered; the market-state indicator `M_t` introduced and then never used; no order, no significance
level, no slope window. Structurally it is the cleanest field in the lane - 76 blocks, contiguous 61-136, no island -
and the most chaotic in headings: four different conventions for storing a numbered title, and not one block of kind
`heading`.

Arithmetic that closes:

- The commission coefficients agree with the prose: `eI_t` charges `1%|y_t − y_{t−1}|G_t` and `2%|z_t − z_{t−1}|B_t`,
  which is the "1%-2% transaction commission" of the section that motivates the whole trading-day detour.
- The weight vector is a partition: all three shares divide by the same `x_t + y_tG_t + z_tB_t`, so `Σw = 1` by
  construction, and the portfolio CVaR then uses `W_t^T C_t W_t` on that simplex.
- `μ_{i,t}` averages `t − 1` returns and `σ²_{i,t}` divides by `t − 2`: a sample variance around an estimated mean,
  which is the right pairing.
- `σ²_{ij,t} = σ_{i,t}σ_{j,t}ρ_{ij}` is a covariance, and the three off-diagonal entries are symmetric with the
  printed `σ²_{21,t}`, `σ²_{31,t}`, `σ²_{32,t}` - the matrix is properly built.
- Ljung-Box is written in its standard form, `Q(m) = T(T + 2)Σ_{l=1}^{m} ρ̂_l²/(T − l)`.
- The ten periods are strictly increasing (5, 7, 10, 13, 16, 20, 25, 30, 60, 120), so the chain

## 5. Reusable writing action

Read a chart, then freeze the reading into a named variable before the model is built. The chapter does it in one
sentence and in one step: "Based on the results, we get indicators describing the state of the gold and bitcoin
markets: Mt and Nt." - the previous sentence has just read Figures 11 and 12, the next two displays define the two
indicators piecewise as markets "in a bull or bear market" or "Others", and the block after that states the decision
rule that follows, `Then when Mt = 0, our investment decision should not conduct gold transactions`. Read, name,
define, use: four consecutive steps, no repetition, and by the end of the page a reader who has not seen either chart
still knows what drives the next section.

What the move buys is a legible hand-off. Everything before it is exploratory - the two charts, the reading sentence
about "buying and selling nodes" - and everything after it is algebraic, and the indicator is the only object that
crosses the border. It also gives the later optimization an obvious place to put its conditional structure: with a
variable that says "this market is trending", a model can decide to trade or not trade without rewriting its
equations.

The failure is that the hand-off is never collected. `M_t` and `N_t` appear in the brace, in one decision sentence,
and then in nothing: not in `eI_t`, not in the constraint system, not in either CVaR form. Worse, the brace inverts
the sentence that introduces it - the prose says trade when the market trends, and the four rows then write the
holding unchanged (`y_t = y_t`) exactly in the rows where trading is allowed. So the reusable action is right and its
execution is not: freeze the reading into a variable only when the variable is going to appear in the next equation,
and check that its case split agrees with the sentence above it. A named indicator that no equation reads is not a
hand-off, it is a decoration.
