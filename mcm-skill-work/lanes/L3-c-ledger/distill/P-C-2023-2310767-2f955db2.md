## 5. Reusable writing action

**Make the first model's failure the second model's label.** After the residual gate fails, this chapter does not discard the ARIMA fit and start again - it keeps the fitted values as the linear part and hands the leftover to the second model as that model's target:

> `The prediction results of the ARIMA model are not very volatile due to the fact that the residual part of this result is not well predicted reasonably well, so this paper further improves the prediction model by combining ARIMA with LSTM neural network.`

and then, one unit later, names the label explicitly:

> `based on the ARIMA prediction results and the actual number of reported results on the residual series of number of reports, which is used as the expected output of the LSTM neural network`

The move is reusable in three parts, and the third is the one worth copying. First, the first model is allowed to fail on a stated, named diagnostic (Ljung-Box white noise) rather than on a hunch. Second, the failure is translated into an object - the residual series - that the next model can consume. Third, the two models are joined by an addition made explicit in prose, `the prediction results of ARIMA and LSTM neural network models are summed`, so the composition is checkable instead of implied by a diagram. What the chapter does not do is carry the same rigour into the numbers: the gate that licenses the whole handoff prints no statistic, the embedding order `18` appears with no search behind it, and the final interval `[9614,43109]` is printed without a nominal coverage level - three quantities that a reader would need in order to repeat the claim, all three left out.

A second, smaller action sits underneath it and is worth stealing on its own: **number the build steps, then unnumbered-list the pipeline.** The ARIMA half runs `Step 1. Sequence smoothing (determine the parameter d)`, `Step 2. determination of p and q order.`, `Step 3. residual test`, each step opening a paragraph and each closing on its own verdict (`d = 1`; `we perform parameter estimation and test that the data are found to be statistically significant`; `it is not white noise`). The hybrid half is a single `firstly ... secondly ... thirdly ... fourthly ... finally` paragraph. The contrast is deliberate in effect even if accidental: the half that has a textbook behind it is enumerated as steps, the half that is the authors' own construction is a run-on list - and that is exactly backwards from what a judge needs, which is the reason to take the first half's numbering and apply it to the second half's content.
