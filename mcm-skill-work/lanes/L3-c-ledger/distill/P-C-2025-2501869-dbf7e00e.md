## 5. Reusable writing action

**Number the modelling decisions as steps, so the equations arrive in the order a reader has to accept them.** The chapter does not dump its specification; it walks it: `Step 1: Variable definition and treatment.` (49), `Step 2: Define latent variable Xi,tΘ and potential medal abilityMapdisq∗ i,t.` (59), `Step 3: Construct Tobit model.` (67, welded onto the tail of the previous block), `Step 4: Construct Hurdle model.` (70, welded onto the block that defines the Tobit truncation), and then inside the fourth step two sub-decisions, `1) Calculate the probability of winning P i,t > 0 .` (71) and `2) Calculate the expected number of medals won by the winning countryMi,t.` (73). By the time (4), (5), (6), (7) and (8) print, each one has a slot to sit in, and a reader who loses the thread can recover it from the step label rather than from the prose.

The action is cheap - five short sentences - and it does something an equation dump cannot: it makes the *order* an argument. Step 1 finishes with the design decision that drives everything downstream (`we introduce Nai,t to discretize the number of participating athletes into four categories`), Step 2 states what the model is trying to estimate before stating the estimator, and Step 4's two sub-steps are exactly the two halves of a Hurdle model, so the model's structure is legible before any algebra is read.

**What it costs.** The steps are labels, not architecture: none of the four is ever cited again, none is set as a heading (all four are `body` blocks in the layout), and the numbering restarts inside Step 4 as `1)` and `2)`, so the chapter runs 1-4 and then 1-2 in the same sequence with nothing distinguishing the two levels. Because the labels are prose, three of them are welded onto the block they are supposed to introduce - `Step 3` shares a block with the end of the previous definition, `Step 4` with the Tobit piecewise display - and a reader of the text layer sees `...is a random error term. Step 3: Construct Tobit model.` as one paragraph.

**The same chapter shows what happens when the discipline stops.** The model comparison is handed to one table with one metric, `Table 2 Model Performance Comparison (MSE and R2) Hurdle Tobit XGBoost Ramdom Forest MSE 20.15 23.01 63.67 51.25 R2 0.8545 0.8026 0.7121 0.758`, and the prose then misreads its own table twice in one sentence: `As shown in the figure, Hurdle and Hurdle perform better` (block 83, naming Hurdle twice and Tobit never) and `The R2 of Hurdle and Tobit is significantly higher than that of the former two` (block 84, where the comparison is against the *latter* two). The typo `Ramdom Forest` is printed in the caption row as well as in the heading above it. A numbered step chain is only worth what the prose does with it afterwards, and here the numbers are right and the sentence that reads them is not.
