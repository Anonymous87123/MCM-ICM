## 5. Reusable writing action

**Put the plausible reading on the page, then refuse it in the next sentence and hand the decision to a criterion the reader can rerun.**

Blocks 100-105 do it in six moves, and every move is short:

> `For this figure, there are three interpretations:`
> `• ACF PACF are trailing, ARIMA(1,1,1) model can be considered.`
> `• ACF trailing, PACF first order truncated, consider ARIMA(1,1,0).`
> `• ACF third-order truncated, PACF trailing, consider ARIMA(0,1,3).`
> `The interpretation about the ACF and PACF plots is difficult and subjective. It is not sufficient to judge the order of ARIMA by this alone. The best approach to fitting ARIMA is to start with a low order model, and then try to add a parameter at a time to see if the results change.`
> `So, we implemented this through computer programming. An ARIMA model was obtained by minimizing the evaluation criterion AICc according to a variation of the Hyndman-Khandakar algorithm (Hyndman & Khandakar, 2008).`

Why it works. (1) The three bullets are the textbook reading, so the paper shows it knows the method. (2) They are mutually inconsistent - the same plot is read as trailing/trailing, trailing/truncated and truncated/trailing - and the paper does not hide that, it prints all three. (3) `difficult and subjective` is the verdict on the method the paper has just used, not on the reader. (4) The replacement is named and cited, and the criterion is one the reader can recompute (`AICc`), so the choice stops being an argument. (5) The escape hatch is procedural - `start with a low order model, and then try to add a parameter at a time` - which is the same thing the citation formalises.

What to copy hardest is the ordering: bullet list, then doubt, then criterion, then citation. Most papers write the criterion first and never show the reading they rejected, which leaves the reader unable to see why the search was necessary.

What not to copy: the three bullets are not equally supported - one of them (`ACF third-order truncated, PACF trailing, consider ARIMA(0,1,3)`) predicts a model the paper never fits, and after the pivot to AICc the paper never reports which candidate won or by how much. A chapter using this move should end it with the search's own output - the candidate list and the AICc of each - because that is the evidence the paragraph has just promised to trust instead of the plot.
