## model-establishment — 3.5 Charging model (3.5.1 basic idea, 3.5.2 model process, 3.5.3 adaptation for climate emergency) (pp. 14-17, 14 blocks)

## results — 4.4 Culture Impact: Nationalism and Globalism (pp. 20-21, 4 blocks)

`mechanism → handoff → handoff → handoff`

The cultural counterpart to 4.3, argued rather than modelled. 162-163 state the mechanism: nationalism makes assimilation the default fate of an incoming culture, assimilation is nationalism in action, over-action abuses refugee rights, and in the long run assimilation destroys the difference that made the culture worth preserving; the sentence is cut at 'in the long run, assimilation will eventually cause a ...' and continues in 163, which adds the counter-case (close contact lets both flourish, but the foreign culture may then grow fast enough to threaten the local one). 164 delivers the recommendation (the local government should designate a gathering place) and opens three scenarios, each one measured-block-chain: no continuous immigration -> the smaller group adapts and, with nothing arriving to refresh it, the culture risks extinction (164-168); continuous immigration -> survival and flourishing, evidenced by San Francisco's Chinatown after 'nearly two hundred years' (169-172); and cultural conflict -> the local majority demands assimilation, which 'is likely to infuriate immigrants and abuse their human rights' (173-177, whose tail glues '5 Model Extension / 5.1 Profile'). The Chinatown date is wrong (Chinese immigration to San Francisco began in the 1850s, so roughly 170 years, and the paper says nearly two hundred), and the chapter is the only place the mechanism behind 2.5's extinction clock is stated in words: a culture survives on a continuing inflow, which is exactly what a one-off resettlement of a whole island population cannot provide. That contradiction - the policy moves everyone at once, the model says culture needs arrivals forever - is never confronted.
