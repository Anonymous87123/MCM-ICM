## results — 4.4 Definition of global equity (4.4.1 GE coefficient, 4.4.2 relationship between RE, OE, FA, GE) (pp. 10-11, 5 blocks)

`read-figure → read-figure → interpret → interpret → interpret`

6 blocks, 297 words. 127-128 is one unit: the Figure 2 caption plus the fitting decision — the FA cumulative distribution is fitted as y = x^a, giving a = 0.7165 with R^2 = 0.8957, and GE is then defined as 1 minus twice the area between the fitted curve and the 45-degree line, “Where S1 = integral from 0 to 1 of (x - x^0.7165) dx”. 129 reports the resulting GE = 0.7265 and reads the histogram (“roughly normally distributed slightly to the left”), which is the only place the paper's headline equity number appears in the body. The construction is unusual and worth stating plainly: GE is not an aggregate of country scores at all, it is a measure of how evenly FA is distributed across countries, so a world where every country scores 1 and a world where every country scores 0.1 both yield GE = 1 — the definition rewards equality of the distribution, not its level, and the paper never says this. 130-132 then give the monotonicity claims (RE = OE = 1 gives GE = 1; FA rises in RE and in OE; GE rises as FA concentrates), all asserted without proof and consistent with the distribution-reading above only for 130.
