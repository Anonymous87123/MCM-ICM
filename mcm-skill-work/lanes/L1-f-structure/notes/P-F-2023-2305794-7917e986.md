## model-establishment — 3 The Calculation of GGDP / 3.1 The Calculation of GGDP (pp. 6-6, 5 blocks)

`route → derive → derive → derive → define`

7 blocks, 298 words. 60's tail carries the section heading; 61 states what green GDP is for (accounting for the negative externalities of growth). 62 makes the modelling choice explicit — “There is no single 'official' calculation method for Green GDP. We choose ANI as the basis … To avoid controversy, we revised GGDP into two parts” — and prints equation (1), GGDP = GDP - c(CO2) - c(El) - c(Wa) - c(RD). 63 reads the four deductions off the equation (carbon market price, energy pollution, opportunity cost of waste, natural-resource depletion). 64-65 expands it into equation (2): GDP - C1 K_tCO2 P_CDM - C2 (1-delta) E_total P_elect - C3 TW p - (GNI/100) %NRD, with every symbol defined, and 64's extraction is one mangled fragment of the display. 66 defines NRD as forest plus energy plus mineral depletion. ## results — 4.1.4 Evaluation results (impact of the shift on climate mitigation) (pp. 13-15, 13 blocks)

26 measured blocks, 626 words, 13 printed units. 153 is the identification strategy and it is the most methodologically interesting block in the paper: G-hat is the GGDP forecast with the shift (from the life-cycle model), G is a second GGDP forecast “directly based on historical data by ARIMA”, and the difference is a treatment effect, equation (11) Delta G = G-hat - G. 154 argues the policy will not change the GDP trend (using 70), so Delta G measures the environmental-damage effect. 155 splits Delta G evenly across the four channels — an equal split with no justification, and the single weakest step in the paper, since it is what converts one aggregate number into the four physical quantities that follow. 156 gives the result set: with the shift in 2019 (Delta G = 10.6 trillion dollars), by 2050 the world cuts CO2 by 4425 million tonnes, saves 2376.22 TWh, cuts waste by 7898 million tonnes, reduces mineral, metal, fossil and biomass consumption by 23 billion tonnes, and lifts the raw-material recycling rate from 8.6% to 19.7%. 157-176 then walks the six AHP factors, each bullet cut across blocks: greenhouse gases -27.61% and NOx -49.71% (158-161); a 300.00% additional price on polluting enterprises (162-164); citizens with high environmental quality rising from 6.23% to 45.11% (165-166); forest area +4.00% and ecosystem health +20.16% (167-169); developed-country technology gains with global waste disposal at 35.55% (170-172); building and industrial energy efficiency +10.13%, vehicle fuel efficiency +68.33%, fossil share of energy falling from 80.79% to 27.86% (173-176). 177-178 closes with “Figure 7: Prediction interval of temperature change with different carbon emissions” and the headline “a significant increase (44.18%) in the global Climate Mitigation Score”, tied to the 1.5-degree goal. This is the only place in the paper where the recommendation to a specific country is derived from a game rather than asserted, and the game's conclusion (do not shift yet) sits against 6.4's own admission that shifting “will bring great benefits”.
