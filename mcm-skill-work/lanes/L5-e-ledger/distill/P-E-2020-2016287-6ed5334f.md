Chapter answers "what is the maximum safe level" by building a carrying-capacity model: four waste
disposal rates, an environmental bearing quantity against an environmental bearing capacity with a
sigmoid damage function, three quantity metrics and three capacity metrics, and the level itself as
the point where the ratio reaches one. It then turns to reduction, scoring eleven indicators,
classifying countries three ways, and predicting with PCA plus a multivariate time-series model.

## 5. Reusable writing action

Define the safe level as a ratio reaching one, then enumerate the three regimes around it, so the
threshold is a looking-up exercise rather than a claim.

> "The EBR is defined as the ratio of EBQ and EBC, which is shown as Eq.3." … "To sum up, to safely mitigate the plastic waste without further environmental damage, the value of EBR must be controlled under 1. And the maximum levels of plastic waste refers to the situation when EBR equals 1."

Companion move from unit 22: make a scalar criterion out of a weighted sum over physical media, so
the same ratio works for water, air and soil — "the overall EBR is the weighted sum of the ratio,
which can be expressed as Eq.4."
