## When the paper goes three-dimensional

Never. All 22 rows are `three_d = no`. The nearest thing is Fig 9, the road bend drawn
in perspective to make the centripetal construction readable, but it is a flat 2-D
drawing with a single vanishing convention and no extruded geometry, so it is recorded
as a flat plan view. Condition observed: perspective cues appear only where a physical
configuration has to be shown spatially (an inclined plane, a bend, a drafting
formation), and those panels always carry axis-free annotation instead of a data grid.

- 3 rows x 11 cols: the checkpoint windows (Table 5) and its appendix reprint. One
  rotated stub label, ten numeric columns, no merged cells.
- Pattern: as a table grows sideways the stub column falls to one rotated label and the
  header row stops carrying units; as it grows downward the header row takes the units
  and the stub stays horizontal. Every table is booktabs, full width, portrait, no zebra,
  no diagonal header.

## Caption templates

- Numbered figure: `Figure N: <noun phrase>`, 4 to 12 words, never a full sentence and
  never a verb - which is why `caption.voice` is `none` for all 28 rows, the only paper so
  far with no verbal caption at all.
- Numbered table: `Table N: <noun phrase>`, 4 to 10 words, same colon-only form.
- The captions that get long are the ones that must name the process, not the picture:
  `Figure 8: Analysis of the rider's force during the uphill process` (11 words),
  `Figure 9: Analysis of the rider's force in the process of turning` (12 words).
- No caption states units, a condition or a parameter value, and none states a conclusion:
  `caption.units`, `caption.conditions` and `caption.concludes` are false everywhere. Units
  live on the axis labels; the one unit that reaches a table does it in a header cell.
- The appendix plate carries no `Figure N` caption at all: five sub-captions are set
  directly under their panels (`UCI Women's track map`, `Women's power output of
  Olympics`, `... of UCI`, `... of own design`) plus one bold numbered key for the stacked
  column, so those rows are `caption_source = typed-from-crop`.
- The appendix restarts numbering at 1, so `Figure 1`, `Figure 2`, `Figure 3` and
  `Table 1` each exist twice in the same paper and the section path is the only
  disambiguator.

## Connector-sentence templates

- Lead, dominant form (12 of 28): `..., as shown in Figure N:` or `... are shown in
  Figure N below:` - a clause lifted from the running sentence, colon at the end.
- Lead, direct numbered pointer (8 of 28): `Figure N shows ...` / `Table N below gives ...`
  / `... are listed in Table N.`
- Lead, deictic without a number (4 of 28, all tables and diagrams): `..., part of which
  are shown in the table below.` / `... as shown in below:` (sic) / `... as shown above,`.
- No lead at all (7 of 28): the structure diagram, the two fit curves that are cited only
  through their sibling's number, the three derivation diagrams, and the five appendix
  rows - reached from the section heading or from the plate's own `Introduce:` line.
- Trail: present on 11 of 28 rows and always reads the exhibit, never restates the
  caption - `As shown above, select FTP, restore factor`, `According to the graph above, in
  the tangential direction of the corner, the rider is exposed`, `From the data in the
  table, we can find that as the deviation value increases, ...`. Three openings cover all
  of them: `As shown above`, `According to the graph above`, `From the table`.
- Two mis-citations are recorded as found: the body calls the two fit curves `Figures 4
  and 5` while they are printed as Figures 3 and 4, and it calls the turning force
  diagram `Figure 8` while it is printed as Figure 9. The mention rows keep the printed
  number in `style` where it exists and drop it where the body only says `Figure 8`.
