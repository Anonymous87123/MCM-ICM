## Caption templates

- Numbered figure: `Figure N: <noun or gerund phrase>`, 5 to 9 words, one sentence on all sixteen
  rows, always below the artwork, always `caption.voice = none`, never naming a unit and never
  drawing a conclusion. Three captions stay lower case after the colon ('the impact of changes in
  demand', 'the impact of technological improvements', 'the results are as follows' style), which
  is the only variation in a template the paper otherwise applies mechanically.
- Numbered table: `Table N: <noun phrase>`, 2 to 12 words, always ABOVE the grid, and the word
  count is the mirror image of the figure captions - the shortest is 'Table1: Notations' at two
  tokens and the longest is Table 2's twelve-token sentence, 'Population and water consumption
  statistics of five states in 2020'.
- Typography inside the caption is evidence and is kept verbatim: `Table1: Notations` has no space
  after the word, so its numbering reads `Table N:` only because the matcher ignores the gap;
  `Figure 6 : Model height diagram` and `Figure 9 :Real-time water level change in Lake Mead` each

  so any search for `Figure 10:` misses the exhibit.

  volume is created to represent the appearance of the two lakes, as shown in Figure 5.`
- `<...> from Figure N.` - used once, after the fact, for Figure 16: `The sensitivity of group a is
  better obtained from Figure 16.`
- `the figure below is ...` - the paper's opening deictic form, used for the uncaptioned map:
  `The figure below is a drought monitoring map from the official website of the U.S. Weather
  Bureau [1].`
- `is shown in the chart below` - the one phrase that names the medium rather than the number:
  `The visualization of water use data by state is shown in the chart below.`

  equation and again for the sensitivity grids: `... calculate the rate of change corresponding to
