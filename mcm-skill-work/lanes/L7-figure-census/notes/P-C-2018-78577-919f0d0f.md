## Templates this paper hands you

**Which section takes which exhibit.** The body runs definition, then states, then
method, then forecast, and each stop gets a fixed exhibit type: the notation glossary
in 1.3.2, the one definition grid (CO2 ratios) inside the index section 3.2, the
state chapter 3.3 as four strips and charts (consumption strip, production strip,
renewable/clean line, greenhouse-gas line, population bar), the model chapter 4 as
apparatus only (schematic in 4.1, one teaching curve in 4.2, the PCA grid in 4.3.1),
the result section 4.4 as a bar-plus-line pair, the forecast section 5.2 as a
correlogram pair plus a prediction chart plus a parameter grid, and the policy section
6 as one forecast chart (6.1) and two data grids (6.2.1). No exhibit appears in the
abstract, the assumptions or the evaluation section.

**Three-dimensional condition: never fires.** Not one exhibit in 36 measured regions
is 3-D and no exhibit carries a colourbar. Value is always encoded as height or
position against a printed axis, so the two charts whose art is missing (Figures 4 and
9) are still readable as empty because the frame and ticks print without the data.
The only encodings beyond the axes are the ACF confidence bounds and the two
reference lines on Figure 14, and both carry their numbers in the prose rather than
on the artwork.

**Table type vs data scale.** Two columns is a glossary or a definition and prints
full-measure as booktabs - the glossary because wrapped meanings need the width, the
definition because the single numeric column wants a header unit. Three printed rows
over five or six state columns is the paper's result shape: booktabs, unit inside the
stub cell, decimals centred. Four to six rows is where the internal vertical appears
(Table 3's two blocks, Tables 6 and 7's stub divider), because the stub must not blur
into the numbers it labels. Decimal alignment is used only when every cell in the
column measures the same thing - PCA loadings and the two-year prediction grid - which
is 2 of 7 grids.

**Caption template.** `Figure N: <noun phrase naming the quantity>` and
`Table N: <noun phrase naming the content>`, 4-8 words, colon attached to the number,
one sentence, no unit, no condition, no conclusion - and the side is kind-driven
without exception: below for every figure, above for every table. A composite strip
gets ONE caption printed outside the whole row, so the template never scales down to
the panel.

**Context-weld template.** Pointer sentence naming the exhibit, then the exhibit, then
a reading sentence that converts it into a ranking or a verdict: `Figure 2 shows X.` ->
strip -> `Arizona and California lack natural resources while New Mexico and Texas are
rich.` For grids the pointer closes the paragraph above and the reading opens the one
below (`The parameters are shown in Table 6.` -> grid -> `These results show the
feasibility of the model.`). Two exhibits are reached from inside a subordinate clause
(`according to Figure 11`, `using the data in Table 2`) and never get a sentence of
their own; both are the paper's least legible exhibits.
