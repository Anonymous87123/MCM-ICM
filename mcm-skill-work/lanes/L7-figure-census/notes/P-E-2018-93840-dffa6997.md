## Templates this paper hands you

**Which section takes which exhibit.** This is the chart-only shape: seven exhibits, no
tables, and the allocation is strictly by stage of argument - the equilibrium section
takes everything analytical (Figures 1, 2 and 3 under 5 Equilibrium and Tipping Points, in
the order the argument needs them: one map of the fold, one map of the three statuses, one
map of the parameter plane), and the case studies take one exhibit each (Syria 6.1, Cuba
6.2, New Zealand 6.3), with a fourth dropped in where it fits (Figure 6, the bifurcation
diagram with the three countries planted in it, filed under 6.2 because that is where the
text reaches it). What to copy: a paper whose whole model is one equation emits three
analytical charts and then ONE exhibit per application, never a table, and never a chart
in the derivation. What not to copy: leaving the introduction, the model derivation and
the intervention section with no exhibit at all - four pages of the model and five of the
intervention are pure prose, which is why the census counts thirteen measured regions on
eleven of twenty-four pages.

**When three dimensions appear.** Never, and the reason is instructive: the model's state
space IS two-dimensional (the (s, r) parameter plane), so the paper's one ambition beyond
flat lines is to draw REGION BOUNDARIES on a plane and then drop labelled dots inside
them (Figures 3 and 6). No multipanel, no shared axes, no colourbar, no surface. The
dimensional trick that a 3-D plot would buy - a third axis for the third state - is bought
instead with text annotation: the three state regions are named in place ('Stable State',
'Vulnerable State', 'Fragile State') and one point is annotated 's=5.2, r=0.65' rather
than being readable off a grid. Copy that: when the deliverable is a classification, a
flat map with in-place labels survives a printout and a projector, a 3-D box does not.

**Table type against data scale.** Not applicable - the paper prints no grid at all, which
is itself the finding. Its numbers live in two other places and both are worth naming for
the lane: the parameters sit inside the equation images (excluded as not-an-exhibit) and
the country values sit inside the prose ('New Zealand's fragile state index ranks 169th
out of 178 countries'). So a paper can carry quantitative results with zero tables, and a
census that equated 'exhibit count' with 'figure count plus table count' would still be
right here, while a census that assumed results imply tables would manufacture a deficit.
The reusable rule: table absence is only a defect when the numbers have nowhere else to
go; here they have the prose and the axes, and the axes lose (see the next paragraph).

**Caption template.** `Figure <n>: <full sentence>`, colon, terminal period, sentence
case, nineteen to thirty-four words, two sentences for six of the seven. Three slots
vary. (1) Whether the caption names what is plotted: the first three do ('r and k',
'dMdt = dMdt and M', '(s; r) ... as a function of t') and the four case-study captions
name nothing. (2) Whether it concludes: three of the seven end on the model's verdict
('The model predicts that Syria would have a negative growth rate and become more
fragile') - a caption that states the finding rather than the content, which is fast for
a skimmer and fatal for a reader who wants the axes. (3) Where it sits: below, always, in
this paper. Copy the below-placement and the one-sentence form; do not copy the copy-paste
seam: 'Figure 2: This gure depicts the relationship between dMdt = dMdt and M', where the
symbol is pasted onto itself, and Figure 1's caption, which exists only as ink on p8 and
vanishes completely from the text layer.

**Context-linking sentence template.** Here the paper gives you the NEGATIVE template, and
it is the most useful thing in the document. Two of seven exhibits are ever numbered in
the prose: 'In Figure 1, we see that for a xed k, shifts in r returns di erent types of
solution curves' and '... corresponding to di erent (r; x) values in Figure 2'. The other
five are pointed at by deixis - 'the resulting graph is as shown below', 'As can be seen
from the graph below', 'this bifurcation plot' - and three of them (Figures 4, 5, 7) are
never pointed at at all. That is what a reader experiences on a paginated printout: the
pointer names no exhibit, so the exhibit cannot be found. Copy the two-beat rhythm that
DOES work - a locating clause that names the exhibit, then one reading clause with a count
or a direction in it ('When r equals r1, the equation returns three roots, which means the
function has three xed points') - and treat "as shown below" as a defect to rewrite. Note
also that every caption here is below its art and every graph claim is unreadable off the
axes: six of seven figures print tick marks with no numerals, so the prose carries the
magnitudes and the exhibit carries only the shape.
