## Templates this paper hands you

**Which section takes which exhibit.** One construction picture per section head and
one computed picture per section tail, and the division is clean enough to copy
wholesale: the study flowchart closes 1 Introduction (it is the last thing on the page,
one page after the prose that promises it), the framework pyramid opens 3.1, the impact
taxonomy closes 3.2.1, then every exhibit after that is a number the paper computed -
the radar pair and the before/after weights table close 4.1, the sensitivity pair
closes 4.2, the tipping-point gauges close 4.3, the budget curves and the allocation
bars sit inside 5.2, the after-intervention radar closes 5.3, the optimised sensitivity
pair closes 5.4, and the model comparison closes 6.3. What to copy: a definition drawn
once at the head of the section that needs it, and everything else placed after the
paragraph that reads it. What not to copy: both of the backward placements (Figure 7
named on p19 and printed on p21, Figure 10 named on p22 and printed on p23), because a
pointer that arrives two pages after the sentence is a pointer the reader has to hold in
memory.

**When three dimensions appear.** Once, in eleven figures, and it is decorative: the
framework pyramid of Figure 2 is three isometric slabs with visible side faces and a
white pin-dot at each front corner, used to rank the three need groups - physiology at
the base, society at the apex - an order the prose never states and the weights then
contradict, since the groups are never weighted between themselves. Copy the flatness of
the other ten; do not copy the extrusion, because a depth cue that carries an unstated
ordering is worse than a flat picture with no ordering at all. Note also what this paper
proves about colour: eleven figures, zero colourbars, and colour is keyed only by a
legend - which is why the near-white 'before' polygon of Figure 4 and Figure 9 vanishes
into the page and the comparison the figure exists to make becomes invisible at print.

**Table type against data scale.** Two grades, and the boundary is arithmetic. A weight
sheet with no numbers to compare is not a grid at all here: Table 1 and Table 3 are
drawings of three stacked blocks - band title spanning the columns, a header row of
named factors with their per-cent, then two cells of welded prose ('Price 26%, Import
10%'), heavy full-width separators and short column stubs, no closed box, no shading,
no unit in any header because the per-cent is inside every cell. They are pretty, they
are photographed by any detector, and they cannot be sorted, compared or summed by a
reader, which is how Table 3 gets away with printing Stability 101, Governance 99 and
Economics 111 while the text says its weights are 'still similar to Table 1'. The one
real grid is Table 2, and everything about it is honest: five columns, two header rows,
country names merged over their condition columns, a double vertical rule between the
countries, three-heavy-rule booktabs with open outer edges, three-decimal alignment on
all thirty-six values, and a heavier rule setting off the summary row F that the prose
actually reads. If a table carries numbers the reader must compare, copy Table 2; if it
carries a hierarchy, use a real grid anyway and leave the drawing to the figure.
