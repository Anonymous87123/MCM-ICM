## When the paper goes three-dimensional

Three rows carry `three_d = isometric`, and all three are coordinate frames rather than
surfaces: Figure 8 (three route views, `panel_count = 3`, in one row), Figure 11 (three state
planes displaced along a distance axis) and the leaflet route on p25. No row anywhere has
`colorbar = true`, no row draws a mesh floor or a shaded ramp, and the two flat-mapped courses
(Figures 6, 7) stay two-dimensional even though the data behind them carries altitude. Carrying
rule: this paper uses 3-D to show a route that has height in it, never to show a distribution,
and it drops back to a stack of 2-D panels the moment it has to compare riders.

  never continues one across a page break, and never shades alternate rows. Only the numbered
  table moves its units into the header.
