## Templates this paper hands you

## What this paper is

2019 ICM D, team 1908810: "Getting a Move On When the Louvre's Bombed". Three models on one
graph of the Louvre - spectral partitioning of the floor-plan graph, an agent-based
simulation built on queuing theory with an explicit blocking mechanic, and a
differential-flow model over the same adjacency matrix - then sensitivity runs on structural
damage, density distribution and exit count. 20 pages at 612 x 792 pt, 19 printed pages,
18 measured regions, 9 numbered figures and 9 refusals. **No table, no appendix, no deficit.**

## Which section takes which exhibit

- 3.1 opens with **the graph itself** (Figure 1, a force-directed network of ~150 rooms with
  a viridis fill keyed to distance-from-exit).
- 3.4 carries **the partition pair**: a two-panel spectral drawing (Figure 2) and the same
  network re-drawn in 3-D with node size carrying the cut (Figure 3).
- 4.3 carries **the blocking schematic** (Figure 4) - a timing diagram, not a chart - and
  4.4 carries the response-delay chart (Figure 5).
- 5.2 carries **the equilibrium scatter** (Figure 6): one point per room, two reference
  rules at 0.1 and 0.9.
- 6.2 carries **the density pair**: the plan view with the Mona Lisa hotspot (Figure 7) and
  the 2x2 congestion comparison (Figure 8).
- 6.3 carries **the results chart** (Figure 9): five setups, evacuation time against
  density.

So the paper puts a picture next to every modelling claim it makes and a chart next to every
result it reports. The model half (Figures 1-6) holds four drawings and two charts; the
results half (Figures 7-9) holds one drawing and two charts.

## When it goes 3-D

Once, and for one job: Figure 3 is a 3-D projection of the same node-link layout of
Figure 1, used only because a Fiedler cut has to be seen as two SEPARABLE pieces with node
size carrying the split. Nothing else in the paper uses depth - not the timing schematic,
not the 40-bar response-delay chart (flat rectangles at 8x magnification), not the 2x2 heat
quad, not the area chart. Depth here means "this is the same object, seen from above", and
the axes are removed so no scale is claimed for the third dimension.

## Layer defects found in the measured layout

- **Every exhibit is welded to the wrong section.** The layout pass files a region under the
  deepest heading printed BELOW it, and this paper prints 7 of its 9 figures at the top of
  the page (y = 108 of 792). So Figure 1 lands in 3.2 (it introduces 3.1), Figure 4 in 4.4.1
  (it belongs to 4.3), Figure 5 in 5.2 (it is the last exhibit of 4.4), Figure 6 in 6.1 (it
  closes 5.2), Figure 8 in 6.3 (it belongs to 6.2) and Figure 9 in 7.3 (it belongs to 6.3).
  Only Figures 2, 3 and 7 land in the right section, and only because their section happens
  to be the next heading on the page. All nine records were re-assigned by the rule "the
  section is the one whose prose introduces or reads the exhibit".
- **Algorithm 1 is welded FORWARD to §4** (it prints in §3.5), and Algorithm 2 to §4.4.1 (it
  prints in §4.3): the same forward-walk, applied to a non-exhibit.
- **Algorithm 3 is measured as two regions** with the header line of the listing appearing
  in both crops; the frame verticals run unbroken across the gap, so the listing is one
  object that the region pass cut. Filed as two refusals, not two exhibits.
- **Figure 8 is measured as four regions**, one per panel, with no region over the whole 2x2
  block. The record anchors on panel 1 and files r2-r4 as fragments.
- **The caption detector truncates Figure 7**: the layer reports "highest around the" and
  drops "Mona Lisa room." because the art's own annotation interrupts the run. The caption
  is quoted from the text layer by slicing to the printed full stop.
- **The footer counts 19 pages while the file has 20** ("Page 4 of 19" prints on PDF page 5);
  two headings repeat in the TOC rows and are dropped from every section path.
