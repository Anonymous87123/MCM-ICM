## When the paper goes three-dimensional, and what the plates do instead

Never. No panel prints a perspective, a mesh or an isometric frame. The paper's only
value-carrying colour is Figure 7's correlation heatmap, which is printed flat with a
colour bar at its right - one colour bar in twenty-five rows [geometry.three_d]
[geometry.colorbar].

What varies instead is how much each plate holds: one panel for a single comparison
(Figures 5, 6, 10, 11, 12, 13), two panels when two views of the same question share a
caption (Figures 1, 2, 9, 16, 18), three panels for the ACF trio (3) and the sensitivity
triple (19), and a 3x3 cell grid for the scatter matrix (8). `shared_axes` is false on
every figure row - each panel prints its own ticks and, in Figure 8, its own histogram
diagonal [geometry.panel_count] [geometry.panel_layout] [geometry.shared_axes].

Legend placement follows the same economy: a single-series result plot is printed with no
key at all (Figures 3, 12, 13, 17), a comparative plot puts the key outside at the right
under the axis (Figures 5, 6, 10, 15 - Figure 15's key carries ten commission levels

puts the key above (Figure 11) or inside every cell (Figure 8) [geometry.legend].
