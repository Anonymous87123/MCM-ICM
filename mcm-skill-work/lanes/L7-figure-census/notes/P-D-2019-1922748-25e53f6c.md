## Templates this paper hands you

## What this paper is

2019 ICM D, team 1922748: "A Systematic Dynamic Route Planning Model". An N&M/SES microscopic
speed model feeds a node graph and a matrix model of the venue, a Ford-Fulkerson-based dynamic
system planning algorithm solves it, and the whole thing is validated on a six-room
hypothetical building and then run on the five floors of the Louvre. A 30-page file, 17 of
whose pages carry art, 42 measured regions - **all 42 are exhibits**: 18 printed figures
(15 numbered + 3 lettered appendix ones) and 2 tables, giving 40 figure rows and 2 table rows.
No refusals, no deficits. Every figure row is a RASTER paste; the only vector art in the file
is the two tables (21 and 14 paths).

## Which section takes which exhibit

- 1.3 "Overview of our work" takes the two orientation exhibits: **Figure 1** (the mind map)
  whose pointer is the last line of 1.2, and **Figure 2** (the method flow chart).
- Section 3 takes **Table 1**, the symbol table - the paper's only symbol table.
- 4.2 takes **Table 2** (the speed coefficient k, two header rows), pointed at from 4.1.
- 5.1 takes **Figure 3** (the six-room teaching graph); 5.3.1 takes **Figure 4** (the block
  matrix picture) whose lead sentence is the last line of 5.2.
- 6.1 takes **Figure 5** (model initialization, every weight printed); 6.2 takes **Figure 6**
  (the three-panel flow strip); 6.3.1 takes **Figure 7** (the bottleneck pair) and 6.3.2
  **Figure 8** (remaining people against time); 6.4 takes **Figure 9** (the four-state
  adaptive strip).
- 7.1 takes **Figures 10 and 11** (the real 1F plan beside its node graph, then the five
  stacked floors); 7.2.1 takes **Figure 12** (the four-stage flow strip); 7.2.2 takes
  **Figures 13 and 14** (the export flow curve, then the margin strip).
- 9.1 takes **Figure 15** (the sensitivity pair).
- The appendix's "Other figures" page carries **Figures A-1, A-2 and A-3** - the algorithm
  flow chart and the ten real/simulation floor pairs.

So the shape is: one drawing per question in the model half, and then the result half gives
every finding a (curve, node graph) pair, with the algorithm chart demoted to the appendix.

## When it goes 3-D

For the Louvre only, and only one way. **Figures 11, 12(a-d) and 14(a-d)** - nine rows out of
forty - are the same five floor plates (2F, 1F, 0F, -1F, -2F) drawn as stacked parallelograms
in an oblique projection, joined by vertical stair edges. The projection is used for one
reason: it is the only way to show inter-floor edges without drawing five separate graphs.
Everything else - the six-room model, the four chart figures, the appendix pairs - is flat,
and the paper never uses a colourbar, a surface mesh or a photograph.

## What the tables do that the figures cannot

The two tables carry constants, not results: Table 1 defines the symbols, Table 2 gives the
speed coefficient k by channel type. Neither is a data table and neither is ever read back -
no prose sentence quotes a cell. Every number the paper argues about lives in a figure's axis
or edge label instead.

Table shapes worth reusing:

| table | shape | trick |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Table 1 symbols | 3 cols, 9 rows, thin horizontals + full-height verticals | units are their own column, '\\' marks the dimensionless ones |
| Table 2 speed coefficient | 4 cols, 7 rows, two header rows | the bottom row merges three cells to say 'this row is one channel type' |
