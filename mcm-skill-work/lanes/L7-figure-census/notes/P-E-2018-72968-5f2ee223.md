## Templates this paper hands you

**Which section takes which exhibit.** One exhibit per modelling step, and none for the
steps that have no numbers yet. Section 4 opens with the four-field disc (Figure 1) before a
single equation is written; the notation table holds the 25 symbols of everything that
follows; the AHP weight grid closes the calculation section. Task 1 gets the world map,
Task 2 the Yemen treemap, Task 3 the tree plus the 3-D scatter, Task 3's application the
India treemap, Task 5's re-weighting the stacked bar, and the sensitivity section the decay
chart - one exhibit per task, placed at the end of the paragraph that argues for it. The
reusable rule: the picture that states the model's structure comes first and gets no
numbers, and every later picture shows a computed quantity. The part not to copy: Tasks 3
and 5 get two exhibits each while Task 4, the cost intervention, gets a table with no
region at all, so the paper's own emphasis and its exhibit count do not agree.

**When three dimensions appear.** Exactly once, and it is the paper's central evidence.
Figure 5 is a perspective scatter of all 178 countries on extreme-climate factor,
self-regulatory factor and SPEC score, drawn in MATLAB's default view with a back-face
grid, and colour re-encodes the vertical axis on a ramp whose colourbar is labelled 10-60
while the axis runs 0-80. The relation it is used to prove - the linear relation that
motivates the whole tipping model - is a two-variable claim, and the paper never draws it in
two dimensions, where it would be readable. Copy the placement, not the chart: a 3-D scatter
for a 3-variable claim is defensible; a 3-D scatter for a 2-variable claim, with a
mislabelled redundant colour key, is how the paper loses its own argument.

**Table type against data scale.** Three grades, and only the top two measure. The notation
grid is 26 rows by 2 columns with top, header and bottom rules and nothing else, and it
carries the caption inside the top rule so it reads as a header cell. The AHP grid is 18 by
2 with the unit inside the header ('weight (%)'), two-decimal values aligned on the point,
and four field blocks divided by extra horizontal rules instead of a group column. The two
grids that carry no rules at all - Fragility classification, three score bands; the
intervention costs, eight rows - are exactly the two the detector cannot see, because a
booktabs grid with three thin rules has no area to cluster. Worth copying: unit in the
header, decimals aligned, caption inside the top rule when the table is a legend for its own
column heads. Not worth copying: field blocks with no field labels.

**Caption template.** `<Label> <number>: <bare noun phrase>`, 3 to 10 words, one sentence,
voice `none` on all ten rows - "Figure 3: yemen", "Figure 6: India", "Table 1: Notation",
"Figure 8: Weight variation with time span for different a." Figures take their caption
below, tables above, without exception. Two faults are worth remembering rather than
copying: the two treemaps of the same template are captioned 'yemen' lower case and 'India'
capitalised, and the world map's caption runs to two sentences and prints a reading rule -
"Higher score means means a state less fragile, and lower score means a state more fragile."
- the only caption in the document that tells the reader how to decode the scale, and it
repeats the word 'means' while doing it.

**Context-weld template.** Every pointer in the paper is parenthetical and none matches its
own caption: 'Table (1)', 'Table (2)', 'Figure (2)', 'Figure (7)', 'Figure (8)' against
captions that print 'Figure N:' with a colon. Six pointer lines cover four distinct numbers,
two of them name the wrong object - both print 'Figure (7)', one introducing Figure 5's
scatter and one introducing Figure 6's treemap - and four measured exhibits are never
pointed at at all. The habit worth taking: the pointer sits in the sentence that states what
the figure establishes ('We conclude the results in Figure (2).'), so the exhibit is
introduced by its finding rather than by its existence. The habit to avoid: numbering the
pointer from memory. A reader who trusts the pointers will look for a Figure 7 that the
paper never shows them.
