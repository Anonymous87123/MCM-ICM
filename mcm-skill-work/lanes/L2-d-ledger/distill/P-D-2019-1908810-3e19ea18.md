## 2. Paragraphs, what each does, and the handoff sentence

62 units over 10 pages, 40 words each. The ladder's joins carry the argument:

- 1 → 2 from structure to a first measurement: "At the most basic level, we can analyze the graph as follows: considering only the estimated time to travel from one room to another, the maximum time for an unimpeded museum-goer to exit the building is simply the maximum time over the shortest paths to the exit from each room."
- 2 → 3 to the responder case, and its flaw: "Note that in this case, security personnel would be recommended to take the same path as bystanders moving in the opposite direction. This situation is unrealistic since the shortest path to a target location may pass through heavily congested rooms."
- 6 → 7 from definition to tractability: "This is very computationally complex to solve, therefore we bound this constant instead."
- 9 → 11 from a number to its picture: "To further understand connectivity, we look at a spectral graph drawing of the ﬂoor plan."
- 14 → 15 from the partition to the hidden exits: "A number of emergency exits from the Louvre are kept from public knowledge; they are not on any publicly available ﬂoor plan."
- 16 → 19 closes the first model: "We address these issues with the subsequent model."
- 20 → 21 opens the second by restating the first's limits: "while the connectivity model gives us useful insights, it doesn't take into consideration many other factors at play."
- 29 → 30 from the speed law to its constants: "Here, t0 is the average time for a person to pass through room u to room v with zero impedance (no other people around; the base rate)."
- 36 → 39 from the mechanism to the responder tweak: "Indeed, the emergency personnel follow the same behaviors and interactions as normal evacuees outlined in Section 4.3, but have priority in movement over others."
- 42 → 49 opens the third with the analogy: "Crowds traveling through the Louvre during an evacuation could be modeled in aggregate as something similar to a viscous non-Newtonian ﬂuid percolating through a porous substrate."
- 51 → 52 from the functions to the system: "To actually analyze the densities in particular rooms we use linear algebra and our graph structure to solve for the change in density of each room at 1 second intervals until the evacuation is complete."
- 61 → 62 from thresholds to result: "a room is either bottlenecked, when ρ = 1, unoccupied, when ρ = 0, or at equilibrium."
