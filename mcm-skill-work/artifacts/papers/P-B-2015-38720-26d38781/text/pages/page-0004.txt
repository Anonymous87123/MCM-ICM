1     Introduction

Despite modern design and tracking capabilities, airplanes such as Malaysian flight
MH370 occasionally disappear over swathes of open ocean. With no communication
from the downed plane, searchers must determine the optimal strategy for recovery.
Meanwhile, family members of passengers wait anxiously for news of their loved ones’
fates.

      The Dilemma:
         ● The primary objective is to find the missing plane.

         ● Our second consideration is to reduce the time it takes to find the plane
           and any surviving passengers, improving the odds of rescuing them alive.

         ● In addition, we will take into account the different types of downed
           planes, search planes, and sensors.

      The Approach:
         ● First we examine historic plane crashes to build context—their locations,
           causes, and the potential downed planes involved.

         ● Taking into account various communication methods and FAA
           regulations, we determine what constitutes a plane “lost” and the time
           it takes to reach that conclusion.

         ● We learn about the origin and evolution of ocean search technique.

         ● We research various search planes and sensors to find their speed, sight,
           and accuracy.

         ● We define all the variables we will use throughout the development of
           our model.

         ● Using our chosen downed plane specifications, we calculate the
           maximum search area that we need to consider.

         ● Based on our crash research, we determine the probability distribution
           of downed planes within the search area.
