5.3.3 Established Genres over Time

྘ Initially, we plotted individual sonic variables as functions of time for different genres. We quickly realized

these plots, while suggestive of the dynamic nature of genres, are not very meaningful on their own.
Therefore, we sought ways to analyze the evolution of genres that include many different sonic variables.

ଆ One method is to measure the sonic similarity between each genre and the mainstream. As genres change

over time, we can use musical similarity deﬁned in Eq. 5, to measure the inﬂuence of sound of certain
genres on the mainstream. To analyze this data, we deﬁne the centroid CN of any network by taking an

࿐ average of all associated songs weighted by popularity. We may then calculate the similarity between the

mainstream and each of the top 10 established genres using centroids. To smooth the graph and remove
discontinuities, we calculate this value for each grouping of ﬁve neighboring years. These values are plotted
in Fig. 6. This graph illustrates genre trends, such as the rise in popularity of folk music in the 1960s and
1970s. We explore the changes in this graph as part of Sec. 5.5.
