4.1.2   Energy Reserve Model




                   Figure 3: Schematic diagram of the capacity storage model

    We know that the body’s blood sugar levels fluctuate within a certain range during human
exercise. Blood sugar is the main source of energy for aerobic exercise, such as cycling. As
blood sugar is consumed, liver glycogen is broken down into blood sugar to increase blood sugar
levels. At the same time, it is common sense that a cycling race will not deplete the athlete’s
liver glycogen. Thus, the blood glucose concentration can be stabilized within a certain range
during the race. As a result, we designed the following energy reserve model:
