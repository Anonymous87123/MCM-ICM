                                                    Summary

    Momentum, elucidating the phenomenon wherein a player‚Äôs real-time performance surpasses

‡æò pre-match expectations, holds paramount significance in tennis matches. This paper introduces the

Real-Time Momentum Quantification (RTMQ) model, which quantifies momentum by assessing
the disparity between a player‚Äôs pre-match win probability and the real-time win probability. The
study establishes the importance of momentum by comparing our model‚Äôs results with an alternative
model that neglects in-match performance. Notably, specialized indicators are employed to predict

‡¨Ü momentum swings, and game theory is applied to strategize against different opponents.

    First, we use the independent and identically distributed (IID) assumption to reverse calculate the

‡øê server‚Äôs probability of winning the first point (Ì†µÌ±ù0), base on the Elo rating system. IID assumption

is not applied to calculate any other value, including real-time win probability. Then, RTMQ
model is constructed by integrating Ì†µÌ±ù0 and the Real-Time Win Probability (RTWP) model for

‡∂î calculating subsequent point win probabilities during a match, that also eliminates the effect of

the serve side advantage. The resulting momentum difference aligns seamlessly with real-world

ƒü ls observations. The significance of momentum is confirmed through a K-S test, rejecting the

hypothesis that in-match performance is inconsequential. Further analysis involves applying the

e Fourier Series Fitting (FSF) model to track momentum trend.
‚Ä´ ›º‚Ä¨d To forecast large momentum shifts, a Swing Prediction (SP) model is introduced. The model
o operates on a per-set basis, as a set provides a more comprehensive dataset for analysis compared to
·áô individual games. We sample the momentum trend value (MMTTV) from the momentum trend at
m the end of each set. This is because the frequent in-set momentum shifts can render predictions based

on real-time momentum data at the end of each set unreliable. At the same time, key technical

‚Ä´ ‹Ñ‚Ä¨H performance factors are distilled from specialized indicators through factor analysis. We then

employ a Multivariate Linear Regression model to predict the swings. Our model demonstrates

‡æê T an impressive prediction accuracy of 90.44% on our test problem, with specific factors such as
A 2nd_serve_direct_won and Net_approach_point_won identified as crucial contributors to player

1‚Äôs momentum.

‡∫± M The paper extends its analysis to dynamic strategic choices made by tennis players during

matches, employing the Mixed-Strategy Nash Equilibrium model. K-means clustering is

ƒü utilized to determine strategic clusters. The Nash equilibrium is calculated, offering insights into

optimal strategy combinations against different opponents.

‚Ä´ ›º‚Ä¨The efficacy of the proposed models is validated through testing on notable tennis matches,
‡æê including the extraordinarily tight 2023 Wimbledon Gentlemen‚Äôs Final between Carlos Alcaraz and

Novak Djokovic, and the one-sided 2017 French Open match between Andy Murray and Andrey

‡∫± Kuznetsov. The SP model exhibits high accuracy (85.32% for the Alcaraz vs. Djokovic match,

and 88.34% for the Murray vs. Kuznetsov match), while the FSF model accurately cap-
tures momentum trends, taking into account the score situation and historical commentary.
Eventually, we pinpoint crucial factors for inclusion in future models.

Keywords: tennis; momentum; real-time win probability; Elo rating system; mixed-strategy Nash
equilibrium; quantification model

1 Introduction

1.1 Background Information

    In the 2023 Wimbledon final, Carlos Alcaraz overcame Novak Djokovic‚Äôs initial dominance to
win, demonstrating a thrilling momentum shift. This echoed the 2008 final where Rafael Nadal
triumphed over Roger Federer, highlighting the unpredictable and captivating nature of tennis.

‡æò Research has shown that athletes, especially tennis players, can experience a psychological or

physiological boost known as momentum. This concept was first explored in a study by Gilovich,

‡¨Ü Vallone, and Tversky (1985), who identified a ‚Äòhot hand‚Äô phenomenon in basketball where
‡øê a player‚Äôs real-time performance significantly exceeds pre-match expectations based on his

overall record. However, momentum can be temporary and may shift to the opponent, leading to
swings where potential winners lose several points or games.

    This paper explores the dynamic performance changes of players throughout a game and offers
in-depth insights into how players‚Äô techniques and strategies should adapt in response to their

‡∂î opponents‚Äô key factors.

‡∂î or swings, in tennis matches. Our model operates on a per-set basis, as a set provides a more

‚Ä´ ‹Ñ‚Ä¨H probability during a match to maximize their chances of winning. The choice of strategy, whether

‡æê T of decisions. To analyze this intricate web of strategic choices, we have adopted a Mixed-Strategy

Nash Equilibrium model. This model serves as a tool for predicting the most common combinations

‡∫± M pursue to gain an upper hand. Through our model, we aim to unravel the probabilistic nature of
ƒü strategic decision-making in tennis, paving the way for more informed and effective strategies.

·áô‚Ä´ ›º‚Ä¨od 8.3 Contextual Limitations in SP Model
m When applying SP model to conduct MMTTV predictions and dynamic game strategy selec-
‚Ä´ ‹Ñ‚Ä¨H tions, we observed that players balance their tactical strategies based on their own abilities and

those of their opponents. Within a single match context (e.g., all matches in the 2023 Wimbledon

‡æê T Championships can be considered as having a consistent background), the model‚Äôs predictions

are accurate. However, when generalizing the model to different match contexts, various factors

A such as the court‚Äôs impact on tennis ball bounce, climatic conditions influenced by the time of

the season, and other contextual elements prevent us from directly unifying datasets from different

‡∫± M matches to make sufficiently accurate predictions for a player facing a new opponent in a different

match context. For instance, knowing Roman Safiullin‚Äôs performance data only from grass courts

ƒü at Wimbledon makes it challenging to predict his strategic choices when playing on clay courts.
‚Ä´ ›º‚Ä¨8.4 Key Indicators to be Included in Future Models

    Beyond the generalization verification with the French Open dataset, we further discuss the
model‚Äôs generalization potential. Given the rule differences between men‚Äôs and women‚Äôs singles

‡æê tennis, extending our model to women‚Äôs singles or tournaments would require reconsidering the

change from best-of-five to best-of-three sets. This necessitates rederiving the initial value cali-

‡∫± bration‚Äôs independent and identically distributed assumption. Extending the model to other racket

sports with a net, such as table tennis, would involve redefining technical indicators based on rule
variations and performing common factor analysis across different sports, incorporating relevant
technical analyses.
