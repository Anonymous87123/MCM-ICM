      Foot Partition-based Travel Direction Model (FPTDM) The proposed FPTDM relies
on the following observation: when walking upstairs, the primary load-bearing and contact area of
the foot is in the front two-thirds, whereas when walking downstairs, the main load-bearing region
shifts to the rear two-thirds. By extracting the contact patterns from these two binary images—one
representing the “front two-thirds” of the foot and the other the “rear two-thirds”—and incorporating
flow rates of people going upstairs and downstairs, we can formulate a partial differential equation that
describes staircase wear.

be the wear depth (or height loss) at time t. Suppose Nup (t) and Ndown (t) are the numbers of people
going upstairs and downstairs, respectively (they may vary over time or be approximated as constants).
