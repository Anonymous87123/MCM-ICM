1.1 Problem Background

The culture and art industry has witnessed an unprecedented boom recently. Delighted

with an ample supply of excellent masterpieces of art, audiences around the world are grateful

for the effort made by those great artists. Among these popular productions, musical pieces
are the most inﬂuential.

྘ With a burning desire to understand the role of music in the collective human experience,

we try to develop a method to quantify musical evolution. There are many factors that can in-

ଆ ﬂuence artists when they create a new piece of music, including their innate ingenuity, current

Our goal is to comprehend and measure the inﬂuence of previous music productions.

࿐ Many musicians admit that there are lots of predecessors who inﬂuence their works pro-

foundly. It has also been suggested that inﬂuence can be measured by the degree of similarity
between song characteristics, such as structure, rhythm, or lyrics. There are sometimes revolu-

ඔ tionary shifts in music, offering new sounds or tempos, such as when a new genre emerges, or

there is a reinvention of an existing genre (e.g. classical, pop/rock, jazz, etc.). This can result

ğ ls from a sequence of small changes, a cooperative effort of artists, a series of inﬂuential artists,

or a shift within society.

e There are many artists who have contributed to the great shifts of music genres. Sometimes
‫ ݼ‬d these shifts are due to one artist inﬂuencing another. Sometimes it is a change that emerges

ğ ls 3 Problem 2: Music Similarity Metric
‫ ݼ‬de In the provided dataset, a music masterpiece is recorded as a group of numerical features,

including danceability, valence, acousticness, etc. These features characterize the music from

o certain perspectives, such as emotion, rhythm and melody, while some of them might be
ᇙ strongly correlated. For example, we learn from common sense that positive emotions are
m often conveyed by faster tempo in music, which indicates that valence and tempo might have

positive correlation. To form an intuition about this, we conduct a correlation analysis on all

‫ ܄‬H informative features and the result is shown in Figure 3.
ྐ T From Figure 3, it is clear that some features might have strong correlation with others, and

thus the Euclidean distance in original feature space is not a reasonable metric for music simi-

A larity. To address this problem, we adopt Factor Analysis (FA), which views original features as

linear combinations of latent variables, to form a lower dimension space, where the Euclidean

ັ M distance has much better performance as the measurement for music similarity.

    FA is a widely-used method for mining latent variables, which is also called components

ğ sometimes. For a dataset X ∈ Rm×n, we assume X is produced by some invisible random

variables H ∈ Rm×d in a latent space with lower dimension d < n. The relationship between

‫ ݼ‬H and X has the form of Equation (3)
                ྐ X = HW + M + E                                                      (3)

where W ∈ Rd×n is weight matrix of latent variables, M is the empirical mean of each feature

ັ estimated from the dataset, and E is the stochastic error term with zero mean.
    Equation (3) indicates that we might conduct a linear transform, which turns X into H, and
utilize the distance metric of H space to measure the similarity of music. We then use the stan-

dard Factor Analysis function in the scikit-learn python package to obtain the representations of

X in a 3-D latent space by setting dimension d = 3. The result is shown as Figure 4.
