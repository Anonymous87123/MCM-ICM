1	We aim to devise a model evaluating the performance of an ODR (Orbital Debris Removal) system and propose the best alternative.
2	Conclusion is drawn about whether there exist profitable business chances illustrated in a time-dependent mode.
3	Further analysis is conducted by AHP to assess the overall performance of ODR.
4	We are also required to come up with a new method implementing collision avoidance activities.
5	The first model simulating the debris removal process is built on ordinary differential equations.
6	This model serves as a basis for the profits model which generates the net gains as the difference of the ODR cost and total revenue.
7	We conclude that it takes a private firm more than 50 years to benefit economically from this industry.
8	Currently there is no profits to be made.
9	We extend the profits model to include the recycling activities of debris.
10	Results show that it has smaller benefits than the non-recycled mode due to high recycling cost.
11	Further extensions take varying sizes of debris and different types of cleaners into account.
12	We conclude that using one type of cleaner makes more profits than using different types.
13	The third model utilizes AHP to choose the best alternatives in terms of overall performance based on five factors.
14	The SpaDE ODR system is evaluated to be the most favorable one.Our sensitivity analysis demonstrates that our first debris removal model is robust to parameters changes.
15	The AHP model is sensitive to the relative weights value of the five factors.
16	We additionally contrive a new method of collision avoidance.
17	The advan- tages of traditional ”box” method and the ”collisional probability” method are incorporated in our new method.
