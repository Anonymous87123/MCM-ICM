1	In order to determine the optimal donation strategy, this paper proposes a data- motivated model based on an original definition of return on investment (ROI) appropriate for charitable organizations.
2	First, after addressing missing data, we develop a composite index, called the performance index, to quantify students’ educational performance.
3	The perfor- mance index is a linear composition of several commonly used performance indi- cators, like graduation rate and graduates’ earnings.
4	And their weights are deter- mined by principal component analysis.
5	Next, to deal with problems caused by high-dimensional data, we employ a lin- ear model and a selection method called post-LASSO to select variables that statis- tically significantly affect the performance index and determine their effects (coef- ficients).
6	We call them performance contributing variables.
7	In this case, 5 variables are selected.
8	Among them, tuition & fees in 2010 and Carnegie High-Research- Activity classification are insusceptible to donation amount.
9	Thus we only con- sider percentage of students who receive a Pell Grant, share of students who are part-time and student-to-faculty ratio.
10	Then, a generalized adaptive model is adopted to estimate the relation between these 3 variables and donation amount.
11	We fit the relation across all institutions and get a fitted function from donation amount to values of performance contributing variables.
12	Then we divide the impact of donation amount into 2 parts: homogenous and heterogenous one.
13	The homogenous influence is modeled as the change in fit- ted values of performance contributing variables over increase in donation amount, which can be predicted from the fitted curve.
14	The heterogenous one is modeled as a tuning parameter which adjusts the homogenous influence based on deviation from the fitted curve.
15	And their product is increase in true values of performance over increase in donation amount.
16	Finally, we calculate ROI, defined as increase in performance index over in- crease in donation amount.
17	This ROI is institution-specific and dependent on in- crease in donation amount.
18	By adopting a two-step ROI maximization algorithm, we determine the optimal investment strategy.
19	Also, we propose an extended model to handle problems caused by time dura- tion and geographical distribution of donations.
