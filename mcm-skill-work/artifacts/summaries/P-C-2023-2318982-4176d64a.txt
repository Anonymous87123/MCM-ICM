1	Since its launch in early 2022, Wordle has sparked a wave of sharing yellow, green and grey squares on social media.
2	Wordle has simple but challenging rules that requiring only a short attention span.
3	Based on the Wordle dataset, we dig into the information hidden behind the number and the percentage of reported results.
4	First, we focus on the number of reported results that varies over time.
5	We try to build an ARIMA model providing us with a prediction interval for the number of reported results on March 1, 2023.
6	It indicates that the Wordle still maintains a high level of enthusiasm one year after its release.
7	Then, we explore the factors influencing the percentage of Hard Mode.
8	By fitting a multiple linear regression model, the results show that the number of repeated letters and the frequency of words are correlated with the difficulty of the game.
9	The difficulty information that players obtained from the community in advance may influence their choice of game mode.
10	Next, we are curious how the distribution of the reported results would change in the future.
11	To simplify the model, we generalize the player’s game states to their known number of squares of each color.
12	Wordle can then be modeled as a Markov chain, and the problem is transformed into solving the first-arrival distribution of it.
13	This requires knowledge of the initial distribution and transfer probabilities relying on the strategies chosen by players.
14	In addition, the transfer probability is assumed to depend on the difference in the amount of information between states.
15	So we propose a method to measure the current amount of information in the states.
16	Based on this, we model the entire Markov chain and solve the first reach-time distribution under different strategies.
17	To make the model more reasonable, it is assumed that the proportion of people choosing the above two strategies varies with time.
18	Accordingly, a method based on historical data is proposed to estimate this proportion.
19	Finally, we combine the estimated proportion with a Gaussian process regression model to predict the future proportion of player strategy choices.
20	This is then combined with the Markov chains model to predict the distribution of future reported results.
21	We finally obtain the distribution of EERIE, which is (0.00, 0.15, 11.05, 28.44, 35.46, 21.16, 3.76).
22	Finally, we want to classify words according to their difficulty.
23	Since word difficulty is only related to the word itself, it is believed that clustering according to word attributes can reflect the difficulty level of words.
24	For this idea, K-Prototypes clustering is performed and reasonable word difficulty index is set.
25	Then, we extract the difficulty information of each category, and then plot the density function and calculate Kullback-Leibler divergence.
26	Both of results show that words with different attributes have different difficulty levels.
27	It proves that our idea is reasonable and the classification model is accurate.
28	Further, we classify the EERIE into “hard” class by its attributes, which is consistent with the percentage distribution obtained above.
29	In addition, we discuss other information about the dataset, such as the difficult words, the easy words and the unexpected words.
30	Finally, the sensitivity analysis of the model shows the good robustness of our model.
