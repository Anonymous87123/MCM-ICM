3	Teamwork Analysis of Huskies Based on Passing Networks Summary We create undirected weighted passing networks to describe team behavior in soccer matches.
4	In passing networks, the nodes are players participating in the match and the weight of an edge equals to the number of passes between these two players.
5	Based on the constructed networks, we successfully identify some network patterns.
6	Besides, we consider some quantitative metrics to characterize the networks.
7	To capture short-term properties, we modify the basic model to l-pass networks and investigate the evolution of structural indicators.
8	To capture long-term characteristics during the entire season, we propose a Markov Chain model, which can be regarded directed weighted network model.
9	To evaluate the success of teamworks, we devise a performance indicator.
10	The indicator consists of three indices: structural indicators, configurational in- dicators and spatial-temporal indicators.
11	Statistical χ2-test confirms that the in- dicators are informative.
12	We then calculate the indicators for each match and analyze the reason why the Huskies won or lost.
13	Furthermore, we conduct sen- sitivity analysis to demonstrate that the performance indicator is robust.
14	Based on these indicators, we give suggestions to the Huskies on how to improve teamwork in the future.
15	For three different coaches we give different suggestions.
16	We also find that the Huskies performs differently in home games and away games.
17	Therefore, we offer some advice targeted at home and away games.
18	Finally, we discuss how to generalize the methods to other team activities.
19	We argue that the methods can be extended to sports such as basketball and volleyball, but cannot be used in others like curling and gymnastics.
20	Taking bas- ketball as a concrete example, we point out how to adjust the model according to the features of basketball games.
21	We further explore the possibility of applying the methods on general social activities.
