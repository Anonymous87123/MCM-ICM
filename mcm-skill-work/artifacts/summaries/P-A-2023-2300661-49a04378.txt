1	Global warming is becoming more and more serious, which has also led to increased drought in some areas.
2	It seriously affects the long-term viability of local plant communities.
3	In order to understand the changing patterns of plant communities in arid regions, we propose three models, a Logistic model that takes into account drought and intermediate interactions, a new model that measures changes in biomass and benefits, and a modified model that takes into account pollution and habitat reduction.
4	First, we establish a Logistic model considering drought and interspecific interactions to pre- dict the population changes of four plant species in the Tengger Desert, Northwest China.
5	Through the comparison of plant quantity changes under regular and irregular drought cycles, it is found that different plants have different adaptability to drought environment because of their own char- acteristics.
6	At the same time, we find that heavy rainfall followed by rapid drought processes can seriously threaten plant survival.
7	Second, we use biomass to construct a new model to measure the benefit degree of plant com- munities, and randomly select 2, 3 and 4 species from the four plant species for analysis.
8	On the one hand, we find that as the number of species increases, more and more selected combinations show positive benefits, and the benefits are gradually increasing.On the other hand, we divide the plants into sensitive types and drought-tolerant types, and find that the biomass of sensitive types is more susceptible to fluctuation of drought intensity, while the biomass of drought-tolerant types remain stable under drought, but decrease due to increased competition under sufficient rainfall.
9	Third, we use the Monte Carlo method to generate two new precipitation distributions with greater and lesser drought frequency and severity, and analyzed four plant species using the pre- vious model.
10	We find that plant biomass remain stable in both conditions, but not in the same amount.
11	Fourth, we modify the previous model by introducing pollution degree and habitat reduction index, and analyze the changes in the number of four plants again in combination with the actual situation of factory construction.
12	We find that habitat loss has a bigger side effect than pollution, and the combination of the two keeps plant populations lower over time.
13	Fifth, we conduct sensitivity analysis of some initial parameters of the four plants to explore how these parameters affect the model and simulation experiment.We find that the maximum num- ber of individual plants under ideal conditions has a relatively large effect on the experiment, but it is within the acceptable range.
14	This reflects the robustness of our model.
15	At last, we optimize the solving method of the model.
16	We use the fourth order Runge-Kutta method to solve the model and get more accurate prediction results.
17	We also use the analysis results to make valuable suggestions on how to improve the viability of plant communities under drought conditions and contribute to environmental governance.
