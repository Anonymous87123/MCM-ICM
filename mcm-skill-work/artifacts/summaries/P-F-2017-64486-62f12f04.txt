1	This paper presents a model to maximize both the economic output and job satisfaction for the workforce with sustainability in the scope.
2	We build a self-adaptive system and deﬁne its dynamics.
3	We run simula- tions of the system with real-world census data(PUMS 2015[1]) to ﬁnd out the optimal feasible solution.
4	Based on the solution, we propose strategies to improve the living conditions of human immigrants on Mars.
5	Our model has capabilities to deal with issues of population scalability, social development modality and evolution dynamics.
6	To address the problem of creating an optimal economic-workforce-education system, we decompose it into six sub-problems.
7	First, we constructed a parameter frame that integrates variables related to three aspects: income, edu- cation and social equality.
8	We include basic parameters that exist in the census database, from which we derive metrics that reveal the characteristics of the society.
9	Through an Analytical Hierarchy Process, we identify the critical parameters, among which the Happiness Index is an important comprehensive evaluation of citizens well-being.
10	Second, we formulate criteria to select 10000 immigrants for Population Zero.
11	In comparison to the selected group, we generate a random sample by extracting data from PUMS 2015 database of 1,618,489 U.S. citizens.
12	We study the demographics of both the selected group and the random sample.
13	The selected group shows an obvious advantage in building a successful society.
14	Third, we construct a model to simulate the dynamic evolution of Population Zero.
15	Consisting of vari- ous systems such as job, marriage, child generation, education, salary, salvation and migration system, this model evolves like our real world.
16	We discuss one sample result in details using demographics, economics and Happiness Index.
17	In addition, we use this model to ﬁnd the equilibrium point between two contradictory goals of faster economic development and better social welfare.
18	By implementing a Principle Component Analysis on the demographic data, we deﬁne the key elements of a successful society as high average family income, high minimum wage and low standard deviation of income.
19	We solve the three-objective optimization problem by Elite Genetic Algorithm.
20	We ﬁnd that the optimal minimum wage is $33526 per year, and the optimal parental leave pay is $50200 per year.
21	Next, we merge the models of income, education and social equality into a global model to and test it in different social groups.
22	We identify the subgroups as professional labors and unskilled workers.
23	The we build a non-linear programming model to decide the resources allocation strategy among different subgroups.
24	We ﬁnd that maximizing the parental leave pay will not reduce the minimum wage and average income signiﬁcantly.
25	Finally, we study the effect of immigrant waves from the view of complex network theory.
26	We generate a scale-free complex network with small world property to represent the interpersonal relationship among all individuals including Mars residents and new immigrants.
27	Our model shows strategy of 10000 migrants every ten years are sustainable, and capable of dealing with a large number of refugees from Earth.
