1	Construction of dams in the Colorado Basin provides people with abundant water re- sources.
2	However, with the change of climate and people’s over-exploitation of water resources, the water levels of dams continue to decrease, threatening economic develop- ment and ecological environment in the surrounding areas.
3	For Question 1, firstly, we analyze the principle of hydropower conversion , the cou- pling characteristics of cascade hydropower stations and the line loss of electricity trans- mission.
4	As for Question 1(a): We first get the data of electricity demand in each state, and then calculate line loss.
5	Next, we calculate the amount of water used for power genera- tion.
6	By adding water used for power generation and general water usage, we get the total water consumption.
7	Then, we construct a nonlinear programming model with the minimum total water consumption as the goal, and use simulated annealing algorithm to solve it.
8	As for Question 1(b): Respecting the relationship between and lake water level and total water consumption, we calculate the expression of lake water level with time.
9	Our model predicts that, after 91 days , Lake Powell reaches its dead water level, and the system can no longer operate.
10	As for Question 1 (c): We decide to add water periodically and compare the water consumption under different water addition cycles.
11	The optimal water addition time interval is 2 days and additional water volume is 5.22 × 108 m3 /day .
12	For Question 2, we construct a competitive game model of water resource allocation according to competing interest between general usage and electricity production, as well as between five states.
13	Firstly, we construct profits function of each state based on the principle of economics and the rights of Mexico.
14	Then, according to the game theory, the differential equations of the net profits of each state are obtained, and the Newton iteration method is used to solve the Nash equilibrium point of the game, so as to obtain the water supply and power supply strategy that maximizes the interests of each state.
15	For Question 3, we first introduce water-shortage index SI to characterize degree of the negative impact of water shortage in each states.
16	Next, we divide water resources into several parts.
17	Then according to the idea of dynamic programming, we select the state with the largest water-shortage index to allocate water resources, and iterate the optimal allocation strategy under water shortage conditions.
18	The results of the model show that when there is a serious shortage of water resources, people tend to use limited water resources to meet the demands of general usage.
19	For Question 4, firstly, the water allocation strategy is analyzed when the demands of each state fluctuate over time.
20	It is found that the sensitivity of electricity production to water shortage rate is higher than that of general water usage.
21	Then, we consider the development of renewable energy technologies.
22	It is found that people’s demand for hydroelectricity becomes lower, making the supply of general water greater during the water shortage period.
23	Finally, the water supply strategy under the condition of water- saving and power-saving measures is analyzed.
24	It is found that the hydropower allocated to each state is significantly decreased, and there is a significant low water supply in dry season.
