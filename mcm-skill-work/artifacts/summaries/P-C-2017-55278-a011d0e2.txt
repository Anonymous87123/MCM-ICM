1	The Effects of Self-Driving Cooperating Vehicles on Traffic Capacity of Highways Summary We propose an innovative model based on cellar automata to analyze the effects of self-driving cooperating vehicles on highway traffic capacity.
2	We extend the traditional cellar automata used for traffic analysis to increase the flexibility of our model, so that it can deal with complex dynamics of self-driving vehicles.
3	Based on the extended cellar automata, we first model the car generation process using Poisson distributions.
4	This model generates specified volume of vehicles on a segment of highway.
5	Our car generation model distinguishes the traffic volumes during peak hours and nonpeak hours.
6	Then we model the behaviors of vehicle-following and lane-changing.
7	In this part, we consider the cooperation between self-driving vehicles and interaction between self-driving and human-driven vehicles.
8	Finally, we propose a probability model to analyze the behavior of traffic flow at highway intersections.
9	We verify the feasibility of our model by running various simulations.
10	Our model is tested with different percentages of self-driving cars, different lane numbers and traffic volumes.
11	We find that self-driving cars can stabilize the performance of traffic system when the lane number and traffic volume change.
12	Then we apply our model to the data of four highways in the Greater Seattle area.
13	Our results show that the traffic capacity increases with rising percentage of self-driving cooperating vehicles.
14	The average velocity of traffic flow doubles when the percentage increasing from 10% to 50%.
15	And in most cases an equilibria exists after 60%.
16	We also test the effects of dedicated lanes for self-driving cars.
17	We suggest using 70% as a dividing point for whether to use dedicated lanes or not.
18	When the percentage of self-driving vehicles is higher than 70% percent, dedicated lanes can be helpful.
19	We also compare the performance during peak hours with average performance during a whole day, and find that self-driving cars significantly speed up peak-hour traffic flows.
20	Finally, we analyze the effect of potential error of self-driving system.
21	We find that defects of self-driving system can degrade the benefit to traffic capacity.
22	We also do sensitivity analysis of various parameters, including probability of lane-changing and parameters in the vehicle dynamics, to prove the robustness of our model.
