1	In the time trial, many factors will aﬀect the rider’s performance.
2	To help riders achieve better results, we need to establish a model that can comprehensively consider many factors and provide a reasonable power distribution plan at diﬀerent course positions.
3	First, we established a Rider Ability Evaluation Model and deﬁned the rider’s power proﬁle.
4	A power-time scatterplot is drawn from the relationship between power and the maximum duration at this power level.
5	Then four diﬀerent exercise intensity domains were divided according to the power-time relationship.
6	We selected the 3P-CP Model to ﬁt the scatter plot, and obtained the rider’s power-time curve, thereby obtaining the three important rider ability indicators: Maximum instantaneous power (P max), critical power (CP ) and anaerobic energy reserve (W ′ ).
7	We take multiple sets of data to evaluate the ﬁtting results.
8	After calculation, the ﬁtting degree is stable at about 0.86, indicating good ﬁtting eﬀect.
9	Secondly, we built an External Factor Analysis Model to identify the potential impact of external conditions.
10	We quantitatively evaluated the inﬂuence of weather and environment from two aspects, namely road rolling resistance and wind resistance.
11	After that, we select actual courses for veriﬁcation and the degree of compliance exceeds 90%.
12	Thirdly, Power Overall Planning Model is established.
13	After the course segmentation and dynamics analysis, the average power (AP ) and normalized power (N P ) are proposed, and the constraints of the power of each track are obtained, so that the problem can be transformed into a multivariate nonlinear programming problem with total time t as objective function.
14	We use gradient descent method to gradually approach the local minimum of t and give a power distribution plan for each road section.
15	The deviation between our results and the actual is +-10%, indicating a good agreement with the actual.
16	Next, we apply the Rider Apability Evaluation Model to diﬀerent types of riders and courses, and obtained the riders output power, speed at a speciﬁc position and minimum time to complete the race.
17	Afterwards, we performed sensitivity analysis of the model, analyzing its robustness to weather and environment and rider performance.
18	The failure probabilities of air temperature, air pressure, altitude, and wind resistance are 0.18%, 3.97%, 0.18%, and 4.22% respectively.
19	Air pressure and wind resistance are sensitive factors, while temperature and altitude are non-sensitive factors.
20	We calculate the probability of failure caused by the deviation from power plan, and give the priority order of strategy execution: slope > corner > ﬂat.
21	Finally, we extend the model to a Team Time-Trial Power Distribution Model, and obtain the extended multivariate nonlinear programming problem.
22	Our suggestion is: the ﬁrst 45% of the course are led by two riders with strong explosive strength, the last 15% are led by the rider with the strongest stamina, and the remaining 4 riders take turns leading in the middle.
