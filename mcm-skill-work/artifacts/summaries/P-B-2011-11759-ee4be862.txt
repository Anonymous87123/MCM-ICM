1	We propose and evaluate two models to determine the minimum number of very high frequency (VHF) repeaters necessary to accomodate a given geographic distru- biton of users.
2	By utilizing cluster analysis, each model uniquely designs a network of open and “continuous tone-coded squelch system” (CTCSS) repeaters to simulta- neously accomodate the desired user load.
3	In addition, the models are mindful of connectivity issues and seek to establish the best connectivity for the set of users.
4	Through the comparison of these two models, we seek to establish the minimum num- ber of repeaters required.
5	In the “Bender” Snaking Model, a network is established by creating a “snake” or chain of open repeaters across the area.
6	The model determines the most effective placement for each open repeater and is mindful to maintain channel availability cy placing CTCSS repeaters when necessary.
7	In the Branching Model, a backbone network is established between the two most populous areas and branch networks are subsequentially added to existing network.
8	After the branched network has been completed, CTCSS lines are placed to both mitigate channel saturation and establish dedicated long-distance lines.
9	This model seeks to create the best connectivity for the users in the area with the minimum number of repeaters used.
10	We test our models on two likely area distrubitions: a city/suburban-like user distribution and a rural-like user distribution.
11	We compare the results and propose the minimum number of repeaters necessary for each scenario.
12	By comparing the two models, we are able to decide if the number is realistic and what the benefits of a different network design may correlate to for the users.
13	Finally, we stress-test our models with 10,000 users in the same area and discuss the defects of line-of-sight propagation caused by mountains in the area.
