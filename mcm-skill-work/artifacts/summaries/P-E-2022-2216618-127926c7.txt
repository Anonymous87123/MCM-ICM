1	Summary Current forest management plans prioritize economical proﬁtability with little con- sideration of the forest’s ecological e↵ects.
2	Thus, the managers fail to delibrately utilize their precious and natural resource of forests as the source of carbon sequestration, the process of decreasing the stocks of carbon dioxide from the atmosphere mechanically or by the biosphere.
3	Undoubtedly, this has led to severe climatic changes and environmental unsustainability.
4	Incorporating social, economical, and ecological sectors, the model we present serves as a novel approach to balance various factors involved in forestry.
5	It re-optimizes the pre-existing forest management plans and enhances e ciency, from a global perspective.
6	A sophisticated but limited model in real-life is The Faustmann Model[1].
7	It calcu- lates the present value of the income stream for forest rotation and infers the optimal time to cut the forest but is completely based on the economical portion of our world.
8	We designed the Comprehensive Index of Forest (CIF) to measure the synthetical value of a forest, consisting of three indices: CSI, EI and BDI.
9	The CSI takes the total carbon dioxide sequestrated by a forest and its products into account; the EI values the economical worth provided by the products; and the BDI is an important indicator of the inﬂuence on biodiversity by the harvest.
10	And we use AHP to determine the weights for developed and developing countries.
11	Moreover, considering the topology, we designed AVF to measure the comprehensive quality of the forest more accurately.
12	Next we selected two forests from developed and developing countries as our case studies for the model: Quaking aspen forests: (representative for developed sector) • Developed countries concentrate more on achieving social and ecological goals, so the CSI weights more than the others and the tendency to cut down trees is low. • Surprisingly, we found out that it is actually better to not harvest any trees.
13	Amazon rainforest: (representative for developing sector) • Developing countries rely more on the economical e↵ects that the forest may bring about, and hence has a much higher chance to cut down the trees. • Being di↵erent from its developed counterpart, developing countries must consider harvesting in the management plan to optimize CIF.
14	We also commented about the strengths and weaknesses of our models, and pointed out the aspects for further improvement.
15	And the sensitivity analysis is conducted at the end.
16	We focused on the analysis of the estimation of the average lifespan of products.
17	Finally, we wrote a newspaper article persuading some communities that well-prepared harvesting may actually elevate the overall beneﬁts socially, economically and ecologi- cally, rather than destroying the environment.
