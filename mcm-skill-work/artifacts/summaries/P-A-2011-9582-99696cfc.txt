5	The major assumption is that the resistance includes the friction of snow and air drag.
6	The former is proportional to the normal force and the latter is proportional to the square of velocity.

8	The variables of our model include: coordinate variables x, y, z, normal force N, resistance f, snowboarder’s mass m, mechanical energy lost due to friction of the snow and air drag Elost, vertical air Hf, and the initial angular momentum of the fly L0.
9	In the first part of our basic model, we obtain a differential equation of E lost based on force analysis and Energy Conservation.
10	Then we derive the representation of Elost by solving the equation, which is a functional.
11	Thus we calculate the representation of “Vertical Air” by analyzing the projectile motion.
12	In the second part of the basic model, we derive the representation of the initial angular momentum before the fly, and discuss the factors influencing it.
13	In our extended model, we calculate the “Vertical Air” when taking the snowboarder’s subjective influence into account.
14	We use numerical method to solve the model and compare analytical results with reality and validate our method to be correct and robust.
15	We analyzed the effects of factors such as width, height and gradient angle of half-pipe on “vertical Air” by controlling other factors, and found that wider, steeper course with proper depth and the best path chosen by a skilled snowboarder are preferred to obtain locally larger “vertical air”.
16	Using Gene Algorithm, we globally optimized the course shape to provide largest “vertical air” and total degree of rotation.
17	There is a trade-off between maximum “vertical air” and maximum rotation.
