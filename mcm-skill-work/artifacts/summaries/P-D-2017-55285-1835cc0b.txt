1	Analysis and Optimization to the Process of Airport Security Check Summary Security check is essential to the safety of airline passengers.
2	However, it is also a time consuming process.
3	Passengers have to wait in lines, get ID check, prepare the belong- ings and get screening inspection and sometimes it even takes hours of time to ﬁnish.
4	Besides, the variance of the waiting time in the checkpoints line is also very high, which is difﬁcult for passengers to decide when to reach the airport.
5	Therefore, we are required to ﬁgure out the potential issues and make some modiﬁcations to the current process that can reduce the average waiting time and its variance.
6	We develop the basic model to simulate the current situation of American airports’ security check and study the properties of Pre-Check policy.
7	Though more ‘Pre-Check’ passengers can help alleviate congestion to some degree, the time in the process of wait for screening is the overwhelming majority of the time spent in security check.
8	It occupy over 98% of total time during rush hours.
9	In addition, we also plot the distribution of different total waiting time, and ﬁnd that it is nearly a uniform distribution over several hours.
10	The variance is hence too high for passengers to predict their total waiting time.
11	Based on this conclusion, we mainly build two efﬁcient modiﬁcation models to opti- mize the current situation.
12	The ﬁrst one is ‘multi-passenger linkage model of belong- ings preparation (MPL)’.
13	It shows that if the airport opens up a small zone for several passengers to prepare for screening at the same time, the average time and variance will obviously decrease.
14	The other one is ‘priority-based queuing model (PBQ)’.
15	It suggests airports to set some appropriate time interval limits to provide passengers who is to take off with great convenience without increasing the average time.
16	Besides, it can encour- age people not to arrive at airports so early that aggravate congestion.
17	We also speculate about the TSA’s latest institution for the elderly and children, but it does not make much contribution.
18	At last, we consider the Chinese cultural norms and apply our model to Chinese airports.
19	We construct the cut-in-line model.
20	The result shows that MPL and PBQ models can still keep their high effectiveness.
21	In other word, our models have high stability, high error-tolerant rate and extensive applicability.
