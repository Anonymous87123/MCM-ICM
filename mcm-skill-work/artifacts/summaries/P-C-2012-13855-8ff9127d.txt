1	In this article, we develop an efficient method to deal with the problem of finding out the likelihood of each person involved in a conspiracy.
2	All the possible suspected conspirators work in the same office complex and communicate with each other, thus forming a message network.
3	We design a model and develop corresponding methodology to tackle this model and finally produce a priority list of potential conspirators.
4	We assign each person(i.e. a node in message network) a real number to represent the likelihood of being involved in conspiracy.
5	This number will be updated during the iterative propagation process.
6	We also assign a real number to each topic to indicate how probable this topic is involved in the conspiracy.
7	This number is not fixed and will also be iteratively modified during the propagation.
8	In the article, we formally define these two values and develop their for- mulations so that we could design an algorithm that could calculate these two functions.
9	We do several iterations of the following 2-phase propagation process until convergence.
10	Each iteration consists of the following two phases: Person Phase In this phase, we recalculate the suspiciousness of each node, based on the suspiciousness of its neighbours and messages between it and its neighbours.
11	Topic Phase In this phase, we recalculate the suspiciousness of each topic, based on the suspiciousness of people who talk about this topic.
12	We also exploit an exponential decay between two iterations to make the effect of messages attenuate as the distance increase.
13	The final suspiciousness of each person is used to produce the priority list of conspirators.
14	At last, we do a series of experiments to evaluate our models and anal- yse the results.
15	We also discuss our model’s sensitivity to the choice of the parameters and its scalability to other applications. 1
