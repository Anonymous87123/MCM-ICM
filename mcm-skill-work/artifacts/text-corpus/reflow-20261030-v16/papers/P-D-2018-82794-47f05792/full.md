1 Overview

<!-- b000098 | heading | pages [4] -->
1.1 Background

<!-- b000099 | prose | pages [4] -->
The world is fascinated by reducing the use of fossil fuels, including gasoline for vehicles. Whether motivated by the environment or by the economics, consumers are starting to migrate to electric vehicles. The migration from gasoline and diesel vehicles to electric vehicles is not simple and can’t happen overnight. The location and convenience of charging stations is critical as early adopters and eventually mainstream consumers volunteer to switch. When nations plan this transition, they need to do:

<!-- b000100 | prose | pages [4] -->
• Build a sufficient number of vehicle charging stations in all the rightplaces;

<!-- b000101 | prose | pages [4] -->
• Consider the final network of charging stations

<!-- b000102 | prose | pages [4] -->
• Consider the growth and evolution of the network of charging stations overtime.

<!-- b000103 | prose | pages [4] -->
As nations seek to develop policies that promote the migration towards electric vehicles, they will need to design a plan that works best for their individual country.

<!-- b000104 | heading | pages [4] -->

<!-- b000105 | prose | pages [4] -->
We need to determine the final architecture of the charging network to support the full adoption of all-electric vehicles. And we will identify the key factors that will be important as they plan their timeline for an eventual ban or dramatic reduction of gasoline and diesel vehicles.

5 SI epidemic model based on the evaluation index system

<!-- b000227 | heading | pages [12] -->
5.1 Model Overview and Analysis

<!-- b000228 | prose | pages [12] -->
In life, people decide whether to use traditional vehicles or electric vehicles based on their satisfaction with electric vehicles which is mainly measured by the number and location of charging stations. Thus we established a evaluation index system to measure people’s satisfac- tion.

<!-- b000229 | prose | pages [12] -->
When a person is very satisfied with electric vehicles, he will tell his friends and family around the advantages of electric vehicles, so that people around him have a certain probability of being convinced by him, that is, turning to the use of electric vehicles. According to the above analysis, we have established a SI epidemic model based on the evaluation index system.

<!-- b000230 | heading | pages [12] -->
5.2 Establishment of Evaluation Index System

<!-- b000231 | prose | pages [12] -->
Before establishing evaluation index system, we need to analyse people’s specific demand- s. In the following, we first classify people according to the length of their journey and analyse the demands of different types of people. Then establish the evaluation index system according to people’s demands.

<!-- b000232 | heading | pages [12] -->
5.2.1 Analysis of User’s Demands

<!-- b000233 | prose | pages [12] -->
Based on people’s preferences, people are divided into two types: those who prefer long- distance travel (LT) and those who prefer short excursions (SE).

<!-- b000234 | prose | pages [12] -->
• Long trips often cross several counties and will definitely need supercharging station during the travel, so it’s more important for LT that the number and location of super- charging station along the way.

<!-- b000235 | prose | pages [12, 13] -->
• SE’s basic necessities of life are mostly in their own county. SE is divided into people who won’t forget to charge (NFC) and who will forget to charge (FC). NFC’s daily life does not require supercharging station, they only need a sufficient number of destination charging stations to meet the daily charging demands. FC may require supercharging stations in the city in case forgotten to charge EVs in time, so the number of superchargingstations in the city is more important to FCs than NFCs.
