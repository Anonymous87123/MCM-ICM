Background

Gross Domestic Product (GDP) is commonly used to measure the economic strength and development of a country or region by accounting for the ﬁnal result of produc- tion activities of all resident units in a speciﬁc time frame. However, the pursuit of economic growth can have adverse impacts on the environment, resulting in negative externalities.

Since the 1960s, ecological degradation and global environmental pollution have become major constraints on sustainability of both society and the economy. To ad- dress this issue, green accounting, which incorporates environmental and sustainabil- ity perspectives and factors, has emerged as a way to account for the ecosystem. The concept of Green GDP was introduced to measure the GDP while accounting for these factors.
