Sensitivity with Respect to Number of Self Driving Cars

As self-driving cars continue to captivate the imagination of the nation, it would be worthwhile to know what eﬀect they might have on the traﬃc encountered in toll plazas. An initial sweep was done ranging from 0% to 25% on the symmetric six lane nine toll layout with a medium traﬃc level in order to gauge the impact.

As can be seen from the ﬁgure, there is essentially no impact on the accumulated throughput of the system, i.e. the number of cars that are serviced by the toll plaza and re-enter the normal traﬃc pattern. This same experiment was run on the three lane four tollbooth symmetric layout with medium traﬃc as well, and the results can be seen below.

We see the same pattern emerge once more, and thus we can safely say that the eﬀect of self-driving cars is negligible on the system. It remains to be seen what the eﬀect of these cars would be on the system as they gain a larger market share, but it is unlikely in the near future that more than 25% of cars on the road will drive themselves. Moreover, it is not surprising that the eﬀect should be negligible, since ideally self driving cars would drive like their human counterparts, just more defensively.
