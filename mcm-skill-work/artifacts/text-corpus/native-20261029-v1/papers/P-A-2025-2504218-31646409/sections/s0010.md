4. Assumption: The rolling friction between the soles of pedestrians’ shoes and the stairs involves

a fixed minor slipping, which can thereby be equated to sliding friction. Justification: In scenarios like stair stepping or wheel rolling, surfaces often experience “nominal rolling contact,” but in reality: a) microscopic sliding occurs due to material roughness, causing wear; b) local unloading-reloading happens with each step. After analysis, simplifying foot con- tact as micro-sliding wear may be acceptable in archaeology.
