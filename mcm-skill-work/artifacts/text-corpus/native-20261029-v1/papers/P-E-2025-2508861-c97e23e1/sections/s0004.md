1 Introduction

1.1 Background

The growing global demand for agriculture has driven the large-scale conversion of for- ests into agricultural land and the transformation of complex ecosystems into monoculture farming landscapes. This shift has resulted in challenges such as soil degradation and pest out- breaks, compelling farmers to rely heavily on chemicals (e.g., insecticides and herbicides) to maintain crop yields. However, prolonged chemical use disrupts ecological balance, diminishes native species diversity, and undermines agroecosystem sustainability [1]. To address these challenges, the introduction of beneficial insects (e.g., bats and bees) has been shown to restore ecological balance, improve crop productivity, and enhance soil health [2].
