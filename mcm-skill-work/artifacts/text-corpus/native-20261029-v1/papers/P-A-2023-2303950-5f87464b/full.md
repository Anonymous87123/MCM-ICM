A Methodology to Simulate Drought-Stricken Plant Communities

<!-- b000010 | heading | pages [1] -->
Summary

<!-- b000011 | prose | pages [1] -->
The study of plant communities in arid areas is an indispensable part of environmental protection. In these regions, annual plants are important components of vegetation, by virtue of their unique characteristics. This paper presents a method of simulation for the growth, competition, and facilitation of annual plants, which can be applied to various circumstances.

<!-- b000012 | prose | pages [1] -->
To begin with, we establish the Growth Model of annual plants. At first, we determine the relationship between soil water content and the natural growth rate in Logistic Model. Then, we build the differential equation of soil water content, based on the stochastic rainfall model. Finally, we establish the law of annual plant reproduction. To estimate the statistical parameters in the stochastic rainfall model, we use Normal Equation to get the optimal parameters according to Least Square Method.

<!-- b000013 | prose | pages [1] -->
Based on the Growth Model, we establish the Competition Model of various plants to simulate their root competition. For the competition coefficient in Lotka-Volterra Model, we innovatively determine its relationship with root growth and water-utilizing capacity of plants. Considering the structure of root system, we estimate the parameters in the model reasonably.

<!-- b000014 | prose | pages [1] -->
Based on the Competition Model, we further establish the Facilitation Model of shrub plants to herbaceous plants. Given the water-lifting action of shrub plants, we modify the water absorption rate in the Competition Model. So far, our models have been elaborated and the parameters have been estimated properly.

<!-- b000015 | prose | pages [1] -->
After that, we use the fourth order Runge-Kutta algorithm to give numerical solutions to the differential equations. And the problems in the Requirement have been solved as follows:

⚫ Based on the results from our TOPSIS model, at least eight plant species are required

<!-- b000017 | prose | pages [1] -->
to provide overall benefits for the community. As the number of plant species grows, the scale of the community will decrease, while the community will embrace better stability. ⚫ By improving the water absorption efficiency of herbaceous plants, shrubs can reduce

<!-- b000018 | prose | pages [1] -->
the interspecific competition, make the average biomass of different herbaceous plants close to each other, and improve the stability of biodiversity. ⚫ Under more severe drought conditions provided in Section 5.4, the scale of the

<!-- b000019 | prose | pages [1] -->
community will be decreased by over 67% and the fluctuation of the evolution will be enlarged by over 36%. But these indexes still follow the similar trend in Section 5.2 as the number of species grows regardless of different weather cycles. ⚫ The effects of two typical types of pollutants are compared. The results show that

<!-- b000020 | prose | pages [1] -->
pollutants such as acid rain are more dangerous because it can decrease the scale by 14.6% and increase the fluctuation by 14.4% at the same time. Also, the diminishment of habitat by 50% will cause the system to lose 22.9% of its scale but reduce 8.7% of its fluctuation. ⚫ Based on our model and analysis, our strategies are expressed from two main aspects：

1 Introduction

<!-- b000082 | heading | pages [3] -->
1.1 Background

<!-- b000083 | prose | pages [3] -->
Arid environments are characterized by limited and variable rainfall that supplies resources in pulses. And grasslands, mostly covered by herbages and shrubs, are quite sensitive to the shortage of water. Plenty of observations reveal that the number of different species has essential effects on how a plant community adapts to droughts which may take place at changeable frequencies and wide range of severity. To be specific, plants are better adapted to drought conditions in communities with several species, rather than in those with only one species. Therefore, this phenomenon, which is a consequence of localized biodiversity, is of great research value.

7 Strengths and Weaknesses

<!-- b000920 | heading | pages [24] -->
7.1 Strengths

<!-- b000921 | prose | pages [24] -->
⚫ With full considerations on the complexity of the evolution of annual plant species, our

<!-- b000922 | prose | pages [24] -->
model takes the form of differential equations on the basis of the Logistic Model. Meanwhile, the instantaneous jumps of the plant communities due to the reproduction and death of the plants are also included. Theoretically, our model is able to predict the state of community at any given time.

<!-- b000923 | prose | pages [24] -->
⚫ We consider the impact of water availability on the plant communities in our model using

<!-- b000924 | prose | pages [24] -->
the relationship between the natural growth rate and the soil water content. So, our model is able to simulate the evolution of plant communities against various drought conditions.

<!-- b000925 | prose | pages [24] -->
⚫ The facilitation and competition between different species are modeled based on the

<!-- b000926 | prose | pages [24] -->
previous study of Botany and Lotka-Volterra Model. Therefore, our model is able to simulate the evolution of various communities in which the interactions between species are different.

<!-- b000927 | prose | pages [24] -->
⚫ Using fourth order Runge-Kutta algorithm to calculate the numerical solutions of the

<!-- b000928 | prose | pages [24] -->
differential equations, our model features computational accuracy and strong stability.

<!-- b000929 | prose | pages [24] -->
⚫ TOPSIS is employed to evaluate the evolutions of different plant communities. By virtue

<!-- b000930 | prose | pages [24] -->
of both simplicity and comprehensibility, this method efficiently measures the overall performance of a specific plant community with respect to both the scale and the stability.

<!-- b000931 | heading | pages [24] -->
7.2 Weaknesses

<!-- b000932 | prose | pages [24] -->
⚫ In our model, soil water only comes from rainfall, and decreases only due to the

<!-- b000933 | prose | pages [24] -->
evaporation and consumptions of species. Whereas the actual development of groundwater is much more complicated than that, as the factors such as underground rivers are not negligible.

<!-- b000934 | prose | pages [24] -->
⚫ Various types of droughts are defined with different frequencies and precipitations of

<!-- b000935 | prose | pages [24] -->
rainfall. However, drought varies in many other aspects, such as the local temperature, wind circulation, and so forth. Therefore, more dimensions should be included to better describe the drought.

<!-- b000936 | prose | pages [24] -->
⚫ Although the multiple parameters in our model ensure the flexibility of the model, some

<!-- b000937 | prose | pages [24] -->
of which are difficult to calculate precisely, so we give a proper estimation based on our references. However, we have rigorous derivation and analysis of the key parameters in the model.
