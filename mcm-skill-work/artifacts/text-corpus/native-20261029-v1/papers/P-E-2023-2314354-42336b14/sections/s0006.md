1.1 Problem Background

Light pollution is a growing concern for many communities and ecosystems around the world. It is caused by the excessive or inappropriate use of artificial light, which can negatively affect the environ- ment as well as the health and safety of humans and other organisms. For example, light pollution can disrupt the migratory patterns of birds, causing them to become disoriented and confused. Exposure to artificial light at night can disrupt human circadian rhythms, leading to poor sleep quality and associated health risks, such as obesity and diabetes.

The impact of light pollution varies depending on local development levels, population, biodiversity, geography and climate. To effectively address light pollution, it is important to develop a widely appli- cable measurement model and discuss intervention strategies that can be tailored to different regions.
