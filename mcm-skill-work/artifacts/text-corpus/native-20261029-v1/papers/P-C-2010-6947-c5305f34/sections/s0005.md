We make the following assumptions in this paper: z Moore’s data in 1999 is accurate and random enough to be a representative sample of the North Pacific Central Gyre. z Marine debris poses danger to marine organisms only through ingestion. This ignores the danger brought by entanglement because the ingestion of small size plastic fractions affect large number and diversity of species when compared to the entanglement (Monica F, 2009). z The danger to marine organism is only determined by the amount of pieces it ingests. The more pieces of plastic it ingest, the greater it will harm the plastic eater. The assumption is necessary because the other data, like poison chemical content of the plastic, cannot be obtained in our work. z The amount of plastic the creatures ingest is only determined by two factors: the size of the plastic and the abundance of the plastic. This ignores the other factors may contribute to fish digestion such as color and shape, which are difficult to model accurately and have little effect on fish ingestion. z The eating habits of different marine organism are same. In other words, all the marine creatures food selection are the same and their ingestion are random.
