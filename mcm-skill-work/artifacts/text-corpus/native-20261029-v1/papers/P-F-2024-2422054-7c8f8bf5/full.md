Summary

<!-- b000012 | prose | pages [1] -->
The illegal wildlife trade(IWT) causes signiﬁcant economic and ecological damage

<!-- b000013 | prose | pages [1] -->
annually. To better comprehend the causal factors of IWT and balance ecological con-

<!-- b000014 | prose | pages [1] -->
servation with economic development, we constructed Discrete Dynamic Optimization

<!-- b000015 | prose | pages [1] -->
Models, providing advice to the Tanzanian government on decision-making.

<!-- b000016 | prose | pages [1] -->
First, we analyzed the Tanzanian government’s governing power, economic resources,

<!-- b000017 | title | pages [1] -->
微信号：MATHmodels

<!-- b000018 | prose | pages [1] -->
and environmental interests that enable it to take action to reduce illegal wildlife trade.Then,

<!-- b000019 | prose | pages [1] -->
we developed two discrete optimization models linked at discrete points in time: a model

<!-- b000020 | prose | pages [1] -->
of the behavioral choices of IWT and a model of the government’s policy decisions.

<!-- b000021 | prose | pages [1] -->
The ﬁrst model analyzes the correlation between the duration of illegal activities

<!-- b000022 | prose | pages [1] -->
by wildlife traders and their legal remuneration, which is determined by their proﬁt-

<!-- b000023 | prose | pages [1] -->
maximizing objective. The second model analyzes how the government’s investment in

<!-- b000024 | prose | pages [1] -->
infrastructure development affects IWT and other goals as a decision variable in an opti-

<!-- b000025 | prose | pages [1] -->
mization model.

<!-- b000026 | prose | pages [1] -->
By iterating between Model I and Model II, we identify policy recommendations for

<!-- b000027 | prose | pages [1] -->
the Tanzanian government. Our program has the potential to signiﬁcantly reduce IWT

<!-- b000028 | prose | pages [1] -->
and increase salary levels within 5 years and in the long term.

<!-- b000029 | prose | pages [1] -->
Furthermore, we consider deferring policy effects to address climate change and

<!-- b000030 | prose | pages [1] -->
other transportation systems. This approach helps to complement the unquantiﬁable com-

<!-- b000031 | prose | pages [1] -->
ponents of policy effects.

<!-- b000032 | prose | pages [1] -->
We conducted sensitivity analyses to ensure the robustness of our project’s perfor-

<!-- b000033 | prose | pages [1] -->
mance in terms of wildlife conservation outcomes and economic value. Finally, we pro-

<!-- b000034 | prose | pages [1] -->
vided the Tanzanian government with a memorandum of project-speciﬁc measures.

<!-- b000874 | heading | pages [22] -->
Conclusion

<!-- b000875 | prose | pages [22] -->
This article reviews the current status and future trend of illegal wildlife trade in

<!-- b000876 | prose | pages [22] -->
Tanzania and personalizes a model to Tanzanian characteristics. The model describes

<!-- b000877 | prose | pages [22] -->
and estimates the relationship between income and illegal wildlife trade in terms of the

<!-- b000878 | prose | pages [22] -->
behavioral decisions of IWTers.

<!-- b000879 | prose | pages [22] -->
Subsequently, we extend the micro-model to the overall economic system, recogniz-

<!-- b000880 | prose | pages [22] -->
ing that the Tanzanian government’s decision-making goals are directly affected by our

<!-- b000881 | prose | pages [22] -->
policies, including investments in infrastructure and agricultural subsidies. The dynamic

<!-- b000882 | prose | pages [22] -->
macro-model offer a quantitative reference to the Tanzanian government’s decision-making.

<!-- b000883 | prose | pages [22] -->
Our decision-making results provide the Tanzanian government with prospective ex-

<!-- b000884 | prose | pages [22] -->
pectations through parameter estimation and sensitivity analysis. We believe that the il-

<!-- b000885 | prose | pages [22] -->
legal wildlife trade will continue to decrease with the efforts of all, although it remains

<!-- b000886 | prose | pages [22] -->
difﬁcult at this stage. Protecting wildlife is crucial not only for ethical reasons but also for

<!-- b000887 | prose | pages [22] -->
our own survival.
