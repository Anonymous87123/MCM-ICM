## Templates this paper hands you

**Which section takes which exhibit.** The schedule is one exhibit per conceptual step and
it is the sparest in the lane: the typology section takes the two MIRROR CHARTS that define
the coefficient sets (6, Figures 1 and 2 - same frame, same inside legend, same three-box
annotation rail, only the sign and the words change), and then each case study takes a
borrowed picture plus its own curve: desert = satellite pair + V(A) + Net(A) + stacked
column (Figures 3 to 6), steel = photograph + falling V(A) (7, 8), reserve = suitability
map pair + rising V(A) (9, 10). What to copy: the mirror pair, because two charts that
differ only in sign teach a classification faster than one chart plus a paragraph. What to
copy with care: the per-case picture, because a cited photograph or map printed at 0.15 to
0.20 of a page is bigger than every model result in the paper - if you import a picture to
set the scene, do not let it out-scale the result it sets up.

**When three dimensions appear.** Never. Nothing in this paper is drawn in perspective, as
a surface mesh or isometrically, in a document whose whole subject is a value surface over
land area - and the paper pays for that with annotation. Depth is replaced by three
cheaper devices, all of which are worth copying: (1) a SIGN RAIL, the three boxes down the
right side of Figures 1 and 2 that read the curve for the reader; (2) a CALLOUT ARROW, the
two blue labels of Figure 4 that print the threshold ("Value turns positive at 2091") and
the maximum ("Value reaches maximum at 4500") because the axis prints almost no numbers;
(3) a COORDINATE BOX, Figure 5's three boxed pairs that carry the turning points the ticks
cannot. The thing not to copy is the tick economy that makes all three necessary: a chart
labelled only "0" and "5000" cannot be reasoned about without its annotations, and the
reader never sees the curve's shape independently of the author's reading of it.

**Table type against data scale.** There is no table in this paper - ten exhibits, all
figures, no grid anywhere. That is itself the reusable lesson: a paper whose contribution
is a FUNCTION rather than a data set prints no grid, because every number it owns is a
coefficient in an equation, and equations are set in type rather than drawn. So when a
census has nothing to put in the table block, the honest reading is not "the table search
failed" but "this document has no tabular data by construction". The pairs still worth
recording are the two multipanel exhibits, which are the only place the paper uses a grid
of any kind: two photographs at 1x2 in Figure 3 and two colour-mapped panels at 1x2 in
Figure 9, both with per-panel captions or titles, neither sharing an axis - which is how
this lane's `shared_axes` field gets a clean false on a genuinely matched pair.

**Caption template.** `Figure <n>: <label>`, colon, capitalised noun phrase, no terminal
period, six to eighteen words, and print it BELOW the art - all ten here do. No finite verb,
no unit, no condition, no conclusion, in a paper that argues hard inside the body. Copy
that discipline and the caption stays a name rather than a claim. One new failure to guard
against: the text layer can SUBSTITUTE a word rather than truncate it - Figure 7's caption
reads "Shanghui" in the layer and "Shanghai" on the page - so a caption that passes the
substring check can still be wrong about a proper noun, and the crop is the only authority.

**Context-linking sentence template.** The positive pattern is a verb-first pointer that
puts the exhibit in the grammatical subject position: "From Figure 4 above we can see
that...", "From Figure 5, we can see that...", "As shown in Figure 8, ...". All three are
same-page, and each is followed immediately by a reading that states a direction rather
than a value ("the total value of this project decreases with increasing land-use area").
Two cheaper substitutes appear and both are worth avoiding: deixis by appearance ("Based on
the cost-benefit stacked column chart above") and citation alone ("according to a
prediction research ... conducted in 2017,"). The failure mode to avoid outright is the
wrong number: this paper's only pointer to Figure 3 sits in the panda section, where the
exhibit under discussion is Figure 9, so the reader is sent to the desert photograph. A
cross-reference must be checked against the exhibit that the sentence is actually
discussing, not just against the count of pointer sentences.
