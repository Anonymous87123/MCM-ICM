6 Sensitivity Analysis

In a model such as this one where there is a large number of parameters to consider, it is crucial to perform a sensitivity analysis on the parameters to determine how robust the model is.

6.1 Sensitivity with Respect to Patience

Patience is one of the more important parameter of this model, since it drives the merging mechanism for cars, which in turn drives the overall traﬃc pattern that is seen in the system. To test its aﬀect on the system, the number of merges occurred up to that tick were recorded and plotted against time. All three of the experiments were the same standard three lane four toll booth symmetric system, just with varying amounts of patience.

From the ﬁgure, it can be seen that the model is rather sensitive to lowering the patience, yet the eﬀect is less drastic as the patience rises. This is due to the fact that if the maximum patience is lower, then cars are much more likely to try to leave their lane, but that after a threshold, the number of times that a car has to brake to desire to switch lanes is not reached by most of the cars at any point while they travel through the toll plaza.

The data from a higher lane simulation corroborates this result. Thus one can be sure that this is a trend in the data, not merely an artifact of a relatively small funnel for a small L, small B system.

6.2 Sensitivity with Respect to Number of Self Driving Cars

As self-driving cars continue to captivate the imagination of the nation, it would be worthwhile to know what eﬀect they might have on the traﬃc encountered in toll plazas. An initial sweep was done ranging from 0% to 25% on the symmetric six lane nine toll layout with a medium traﬃc level in order to gauge the impact.

As can be seen from the ﬁgure, there is essentially no impact on the accumulated throughput of the system, i.e. the number of cars that are serviced by the toll plaza and re-enter the normal traﬃc pattern. We see the same pattern emerge once more, and thus we can safely say that the eﬀect of self-driving cars is negligible on the system. It remains to be seen what the eﬀect of these cars would be on the system as they gain a larger market share, but it is unlikely in the near future that more than 25% of cars on the road will drive themselves. Moreover, it is not surprising that the eﬀect should be negligible, since ideally self driving cars would drive like their human counterparts, just more defensively.

6.3 Sensitivity to Number of Cars in the System

In order to determine the eﬀect of number of cars in the system, an eﬃciency was deﬁned for the system, such that efficiency = cars out of system this tick . If this is summed over all of the ticks, one gets an accumulated cars in system at start eﬃciency, a sort of measure of how well the system is performing over time.

From this, we see that there seems to be a fairly consistent drop oﬀin accumulated eﬃciency for every step up in the number of cars. Thus we can feel conﬁdent that our model is not artiﬁcially hampering itself based in relation to the number of cars in the system, and our method of normalizing over the populations is valid in our metric deﬁnition. To further conﬁrm this result, we would want to run more trials with diﬀerent numbers of starting cars and determine if the the correlation between the end accumulated eﬃciency of the systems is linear.
