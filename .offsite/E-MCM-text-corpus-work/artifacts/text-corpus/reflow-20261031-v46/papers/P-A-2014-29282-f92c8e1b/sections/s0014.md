3.2.3 A Numerical Test And Conclusion

We use a numerical method to simulate the iterating and matrix updating pro- cesses mentioned above, so as to test our model results.

According to our above analysis, we can conﬁdently conclude that if all the cars in the freeway obey the keep-right-except-to-pass rule, they will all travel in the right lane at a relatively low speed in the long term, while at the same time, the left lane is comparatively empty. So, the traﬃc ﬂow, one of our evaluation criteria, under the “keep right” rule is small. In order to make better use of our freeways, a more ﬂexible rule is needed which enable drivers to drive in the left lane under certain conditions.

This conclusion only applies to a “light traﬃc” situation, where cars can overtake and then return to the right lane easily. When the traﬃc is heavy, all is a diﬀerent story. Unfortunately, because all cars will travel at the same low speed, traﬃc in the right lane is doomed to become heavy in the long run. This means that after a certain time point, a car changing lane to the left for overtaking will ﬁnd nowhere to return! We will look into the “keep right” rule’s performance in heavy traﬃc in the next subsection.
