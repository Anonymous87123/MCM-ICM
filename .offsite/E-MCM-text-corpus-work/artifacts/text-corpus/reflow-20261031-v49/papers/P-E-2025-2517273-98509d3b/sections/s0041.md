4.3 Effects of Anthropogenic Introduction of Bats and Sparrows on

Ecosystems

By artificially introducing bats and sparrows, the predation relationship in the ecosystem was adjusted, the number of aphids was effectively suppressed, and crop growth was protected Figure 7a. However, the mutual competition between predators also led to the decline of the ladybird population, but the food chain in the whole ecosystem formed a stable fluctuation, showing the dynamic balance between predators and prey, combined with Figs. 7b and 7d. Smooth reduction of soil P content Although the P demand for crop growth and soil still exists, the rate of soil P consumption levelled off as the ecosystem stabilized, as shown in Figure 7c. Compared with the previous dramatic fluctuations, this steady decline may indicate that the P resources in the soil have been relatively evenly distributed, and the competition and cooperation between species in the farmland ecosystem have gradually formed.
