8.1 Strengths

• Simplicity: Our measures are based on easily understood principles and there are simple ways to compute them. In addition, we make minimum assumptions on individual characteristics: only α and β are required for inference.

• Parameters: Most of our strategies are non-parametric, and the parameters of the churn model have nice properties, allowing for simple but effective parameter es- timation, reducing the need of tuning to a minimum.

• Coverage: Our model and measures are capable of simulating various scenarios associated with different churn rates, recruiting and/or promoting strategies.

• Flexibility: Our model can be easily incorporated with other assumptions. For example, if we assume that an individual accumulates dissatisfaction even without external inﬂuence, our model can be modiﬁed to cover this assumption simply by increasing the β-to-α ratio every time period.

• Appealing simulation results: Simulation results of our model are very appealing.

Not only do them effectively reﬂect the current situation, but they present insight- ful predictions for the effect of changes on company situation. In the case of Task 4, we discovered that higher churn rates leads to lower productivity-cost ratio.

• Heuristics for HR: HR can gain considerable heuristics from our paper, e.g. how to change recruiting strategies to sustain a required number of positions, and how to reduce churn rates by providing incentives for those who are likely to churn.
