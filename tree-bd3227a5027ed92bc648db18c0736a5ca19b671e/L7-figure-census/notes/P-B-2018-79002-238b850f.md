# P-B-2018-79002-238b850f — distilled rules

- Caption side is decided by exhibit kind, not by section: every one of the twenty figures prints its caption under the artwork and every one of the seventeen tables prints its caption over the grid. The paper never mixes the two conventions [caption.position].
- Numbering is the bare two-word form 'Figure N' / 'Table N' with no period, no colon and no bold run, and it reaches fifteen of the twenty figures and fifteen of the seventeen tables; the seven that carry no number are the two headerless continuations and the five appendix screenshots, four of which are labelled '(1)' to '(5)' instead [caption.numbering].
- Captions are noun phrases, never sentences: the longest runs fifteen words and still contains no verb with a subject, and the two-part ones park the reading key in a second caption line instead of a legend [caption.words].
- No caption states a numerical result, a condition or a parameter value except Figure 15, whose second line carries the two growth rates that distinguish its panels; everywhere else the numbers live in the body text or in the table [caption.conditions].
- Units are handled in three different places for the same kind of quantity: sometimes in the caption ('Unit: million'), sometimes inside a header cell ('C-p ratio (unit: million)'), and in the appendix sometimes in the header of a pasted screenshot [table.units_in_header].
- The house rule style is a hybrid, not booktabs and not a box: three horizontals (top, under-header, bottom) plus exactly one vertical rule that divides the stub from the data columns. Sixteen of the seventeen grids are built this way; the single exception is the second half of Table 4, which keeps the horizontals and drops the divider [table.rule_style].
- No table in the paper uses alternating row shading; the pasted spreadsheet screenshots in the appendix carry faint per-cell rules instead, which is a different device for the same job and arrives only because the image was not typeset [table.zebra].
- No merged cell and no diagonal header appears anywhere: where a label needs to span (types of languages, the six continents, the sixteen codes) the authors either let the cell wrap onto several lines or repeat the row label [table.merged_cells].
- Decimal alignment is never used even though Table 6 prints four- and five-decimal values in the same column; numbers are centred or right-aligned by cell, so digits do not line up [table.decimal_aligned].
- A table that crosses a page break loses all three of its identity markers: Table 4 and Table 6 both continue as headerless, captionless halves and Table 4 even drops the vertical rule, so the second half is only recognisable by column count [table.continued].
- Every exhibit in the body is a pasted raster image, not a redrawn vector graphic: the maps, pies, donuts and the paired bar chart all carry Excel or MATLAB screenshot styling, and the only vector artwork is the neighbourhood network [geometry.family].
- Legend placement follows artwork type: maps and the office-siting figures keep the key inside the frame, standalone pies and donuts push it outside to the right, and the composite pies abandon the legend altogether and label each slice inline [geometry.legend].
- No figure is three-dimensional and none carries a continuous colourbar: the two choropleths use discrete class swatches with the unit spelled on the legend instead [geometry.colorbar].
- Composite exhibits are quantities of six when they are geographic: Figures 9, 10, 11 and 16 each place exactly six pies at continent positions on a map, so the number of panels is the number of continents and the layout is cartographic rather than a grid [geometry.panel_count].
- Cross-references and captions disagree throughout: the body cites Figures 3, 14, 17 and 18 and Table 7 and Table 9, none of which the paper ever prints, and in each case the content it refers to sits one number lower. No exhibit in this paper is ever correctly cited by number, so first_page is null for all twenty figures and all seventeen tables [mention.first_page].
- Lead-in sentences describe the subject and stop there: where an exhibit is announced at all it is announced as content ('the chart below is our result', 'below is the language distribution', 'are shownbelow:'), four times in that as-shown-in form and once parenthetically, and the figure-number-shows construction never occurs anywhere in the paper [mention.lead_pattern].
- The reading sentence always sits after the exhibit and converts it into a claim with a reason ('therefore, we divide Earth language data into six continents'), one paragraph per exhibit and never a bullet list under the artwork [mention.trail_sentence].
- Exhibit type tracks section kind rather than page position: model sections hold the network, the mechanism maps and the pie witnesses of one factor each, result sections hold the three pie composites plus the ranking and parameter tables, and sensitivity sections hold the five identically built three-row grids and the two closing composites [place.section_kind].
- Placement splits by exhibit kind: seventeen of the twenty figures sit in the top or middle third of their page and only three reach the bottom, while thirteen of the seventeen tables sit in the middle or bottom third, so the page reads figure-over-table [place.page_zone].

## Templates this paper hands you

**Which section takes which exhibit.** A mechanism section (3.1.x) takes one small
exhibit per factor, in the order the factors are introduced: a screenshot pie for the
data a factor is measured with, a network for a relation between the sixteen languages,
a donut for a weighting. A result section (3.2.x, 3.6) takes the wide tables and the
composite pies. Part II (4.x) takes two choropleths that differ only in their pins.
Sensitivity (5) takes five copies of one three-row grid and then two composites. The
appendix is not exhibits at all: it is six pasted spreadsheets, one per input series.

**Three-dimensional condition.** It never fires. Twenty of twenty figures are flat and no
figure carries a colourbar; where a map needs a scale it uses eight discrete class
swatches with the unit spelled on the legend, or six pies with inline labels.

**Table type against data scale.** Three rows means the table is a reading, not a data
dump: Tables 12-17 all have three rows and each is followed by exactly one paragraph that
turns its index into a sentence. Sixteen rows means the table is an input dump and it
will not fit: Table 4 and Table 6 both break across pages and lose their header. Two to
six rows means a decision aid (Tables 8, 10, 11) and it stays on one page with its label
above it.

**Caption template.** `Figure N <noun phrase describing the subject>` with an optional
second line that carries the reading key: `( the two language having a line linked means
there will be Neighborhood effect between them )`, `the given Hindi growth rate is
0.8%(right) and 1.2%(left)`. Tables use `Table N <noun phrase>` and push the unit into the
caption when the grid has room for a whole extra clause: `Table 6 The predicted rank by
language size after 50 years, Unit: million`.

**Context-weld template.** Lead: `<Enter/Now/After + what was done in MATLAB>, we get
<the thing the exhibit shows>:` and then the exhibit. Trail: `<Therefore/As the chart
shows/Taking ... into account>, <claim>, because <reason tied to one row or one slice>`.
The trail always names a single cell or slice, never the exhibit as a whole.
