- The model half carries no chart at all - Figures 1-9 are seven flowcharts, one node-link network and one plan view - and all three quantitative drawings (one line chart, one stereo bar block, one flow surface) arrive only in 5.3, after the model has been argued. [geometry.family]
- Every schematic is cited by position, never by number: the prose never prints 'Figure n' or 'Table n' anywhere outside the captions themselves, so no exhibit has a first-mentioned page. [mention.first_page]
- Deictic reference is the paper's whole citation system: 'From the chart' (Figure 10), 'the blue line in the map' (Figure 14), 'in the position of the figure' (Figure 15), and for Figures 3/4/5 the order of the three phases named in the lead sentence. [mention.lead_sentence]
- A figure is placed under the subsection that argues for it and the argument comes first: 7 of the 15 figures sit in section 4 (model construction) and only 5 in 5.3 (results), so the paper illustrates its machinery rather than its output. [place.section_path]
- Three small drawings on one row each keep their own caption underneath (Figures 3/4/5, one 373 x 68 pt band), and the middle one alone is axonometric because it is the only one that changes level. [geometry.three_d]
- Before/after is the standard panel recipe: Figures 6 and 7 are both 1x2 rows split by a single arrow, the left panel showing the crowded state and the right the scheduled one, with 1x2 as the only multi-panel layout used in the paper. [geometry.panel_layout]
- Depth is used as information, not decoration: isometric only where a level change or a route surface is the point (Figures 2, 4, 7, 9) and flat 2-D for the other eleven. [geometry.three_d]
- Only 2 of 25 measured exhibits carry a legend (Figures 9 and 10) and both sit inside the drawing area; the paper never prints an outside legend, and the schematic half is keyed by labels written into the art instead. [geometry.legend]
- Colour bars are not a device of this paper: exactly one exhibit has one, the bottleneck flow surface (Figure 12), placed outside right. [geometry.colorbar]
- Element labels, never data labels: node ids and area numbers are printed on diagrams (Figures 2, 9, 12, 13, 14, 15) but no mark in this paper is annotated with a value. [geometry.data_labels]
- Caption template is 'Figure N' or 'Table N' followed by a bare noun phrase with no colon, no full stop and no sentence - 15 of 15 figure captions and 9 of 9 printed table captions are one sentence-free noun phrase of 5-12 words. [caption.numbering]
- Captions never state a result: not one of the 25 captions carries a unit, a variable, a condition or a conclusion, and the voice is neutral throughout, so the number is always supplied by the prose sentence next to the exhibit. [caption.concludes]
- Caption side is fixed by exhibit class with no exception: all 15 figures print their caption below the art and all 10 measured tables print theirs above the grid. [caption.position]
- Table type follows the data, not a house style: 2-column definition grids for symbol and speed lists (notation table, appendix Table 2), 4-7 column value grids once there are 4-6 rows of numbers (Tables 2, 3, 5, 8, 9, appendix Table 1), and a 3-column grid whose third cell is a whole route sentence when the datum IS the sentence (Table 4). [table.text_cols]
- Units ride in the header block, in one of three ways and never in a fourth: inside the label cell ('Weight(kg)', 'Force (N)', 'Total room area(m2)'), in a units-only row under the column names (appendix Table 2's 'm/s', printed under two of three columns and missing from the third), or attached to each value ('527.5s', '42.5 %'). - Every table uses booktabs logic - top rule, header rule, bottom rule, no verticals - with the outer rules printed double or extra-heavy; nothing in the paper uses a boxed grid or vertical rules. [table.rule_style]
- No table is zebra-striped and no cell is shaded, so a reader looking for row grouping gets it from a grey hairline rule or a merged stub instead of a fill. [table.zebra]
- Vertical merging appears once in the paper, on Table 7's 'Disaster type' stub, and never elsewhere: cells are otherwise rectangular and independent. [table.merged_cells]
- Numbers are centred per column, never decimal-point aligned, even in columns whose entries all carry two decimals (Table 3). [table.decimal_aligned]
- A table wider than the text measure is preferred to a smaller font: Table 3's rules overhang the 415 pt measure by about 12 pt on each side. [table.width]
- Two tables cross a page break and neither repeats its header: Table 9's last route cell overflows the ruled body and finishes as free text at the top of p17, appendix Table 2 breaks inside its own header block and restarts on p24 with the double top rule and the 'm/s' row printed again but the area names dropped. [table.continued]
- The appendix renumbers from Table 1, so the paper carries two Table 1s and two Table 2s; only the section path distinguishes them. [caption.words]
- Ten appendix plates are printed with no caption and no number at all - raster floor plans and network overlays - and are still exhibits, so they are filed with an empty caption rather than dropped. [caption.verbatim]

## Templates this paper hands you

## What this paper is

2019 ICM D, team 1902407: a Louvre evacuation model. It builds a node-link network of the
building, runs an improved Dijkstra over it, adds dynamic scheduling on the same floor and
between floors, adds a disaster factor for fires and stampedes, then validates by closing
areas and exits. 27 A4 pages, 595.2 x 841.9 pt, 73 measured regions, 35 exhibits (25
numbered + 10 uncaptioned appendix plates) and 38 refusals.

## Which section takes which exhibit

- Section 1.3 opens with a **whole-paper flowchart** (Figure 1, 442 x 242 pt, 25 boxes,
  no numbers, no arrow labels). The overview exhibit comes before any model section.
- Section 4 carries **the machinery**: a two-panel network sketch (Figure 2), a 1x3 row of
  phase schematics (Figures 3-5), then one before/after pair per scheduling level (Figures
  6, 7) and a quadrant advice diagram (Figure 8). 7 of the 15 figures live here. No chart.
- Section 5.1-5.2 carries the **data table and the map**: Table 3 as the only measured
  value grid in the body, Figure 9 as the only figure with a legend.
- Section 5.3 carries **all three quantitative drawings**: the line chart of exits (Figure
  10), the stereo bar block of the three movement phases (Figure 11), the bottleneck flow
  surface (Figure 12).
- Section 6.3 carries the **scenario plan** (Figure 13) and the personnel table (Table 9).
- Section 8.2 carries the **proposal plans** (Figures 14, 15) - the only figures in the
  paper that illustrate a recommendation rather than a result.
- Sections 10.2-10.4 carry the **appendix ladder**: two data tables, five single-floor
  raster plans, the bare graph, three network-over-plan states, and one numbered schematic.

## When it goes 3-D

4 of 15 figures use depth, and every one of them uses it for the same job - to show a level
change: Figure 2's left panel is a tilted floor slab with a stair ramp, Figure 4 is the
staircase between two floor planes, Figure 7 is an axonometric two-level scene, Figure 9's
left panel is a stack of five tilted plans. Flat 2-D is the default for everything else,
including Figures 3 and 5 which are the flat neighbours of Figure 4 in the same row.
Figure 11 is the paper's only perspective-bar block (MATLAB `bar3`), and it is superfluous:
the same three numbers are printed in Table 6 directly above it, and the depth axis carries
no scale.

Rule of thumb the paper follows: **a cell holds a sentence only when the sentence is the
datum** (Table 4's route). Everywhere else numbers stay bare and units live in the header.

## Caption template

`Figure N` + noun phrase, **no colon, no full stop**: "Figure 1 summary of our work",
"Figure 4 Staircase movement", "Table 3 Basic data of Louvre". 5-12 words, one sentence,
never a result, never a unit, never a variable. Three consequences worth copying:

1. The caption names the *thing* and the prose sentence supplies the *number*, always.
2. Because the caption carries no claim, a figure can be dropped into a new section without
   rewriting it - and this paper does exactly that with Table 6's numbers repeated in
   Figure 11.
3. A caption that ends in a symbol (Figure 8, "Strategy to reduce T4") loses the symbol in
   the text layer; quoted records must stop at the last readable word.

## Context connectives

The paper never writes "as Figure 7 shows". Its connectives are:

- **deictic + verb**: "From the chart we can see the exit evacuation: ..." (Figure 10),
  "When the channel of the blue line in the map is opened ..." (Figure 14),
  "If the exit can be increased in the position of the figure ..." (Figure 15, a forward
  reference printed a page before the art).
- **an enumerated list item, then the drawing**: "(1) Broaden the channel width ... (2) Open
  the other rooms ..." each followed by its plan.
- **a phase list, then three drawings in the same order**: "We divide the escape time into
  the indoor escape phase, the up and down stairs phase, the entry and exit phase ..." and
  then Figures 3, 4, 5 in exactly that order.
- **a pointer to the appendix instead of the figure**: "The algorithm for scheduling
  strategy in the same floor is shown in the appendix." sits directly above Figure 6 and
  points at the listing, not at the drawing.

Read-back sentences exist for 5 of the 15 figures and are always quantified ("the slowest
route was 52.3% in the room and channel movement", "reducing the congestion of the pyramid
by 24%"). The other ten figures get no read-back - the paper simply moves on.

## Layer defects found in the measured layout

- **Six heading welds.** The layout pass gives Figure 7 section 4.7 (it prints inside 4.6);
  Table 2 and Figure 8 inherit 5.1 (they belong to 4.7 and 4.7.1); Table 3 inherits 5.3.1
  (it belongs to 5.1); Figure 9 inherits 5.3.1 (it belongs to 5.2); Table 7 inherits 6.2
  (it belongs to 6.1.1); Figure 15 inherits 8.4 (it belongs to 8.2). All six were
  re-assigned by the printed-heading rule.
- **The appendix renumbers from Table 1**, so the paper carries two Table 1s and two Table
  2s. Only the section path tells them apart.
- **Table 9 splits p16 -> p17** with the last route cell continuing at the top of p17 and
  no header repeated; note that its measured region stops at the header rule and the body
  runs 125 pt past it, so the bbox under-covers the grid. **Appendix Table 2 splits
  p23 -> p24** with the double top rule and the `m/s` row printed twice but the area names
  dropped - and its p23 fragment (caption, top rule, area names) falls outside every
  measured region, because that page's only region is the footer band.
- **Figure 8 is not one region but eight.** The layout pass measures the central disc and
  the four corner boxes separately (p9 r2-r9), with no region covering the whole diagram;
  the record anchors on the largest block and files the other seven as fragments.
- **The notation heading welds into its own table header**: the text layer reads
  "III. Notations Description S he node of the path ...", so the table has no caption line
  of its own and its first cell has lost a letter ("he node of the path").
- **Figure 8's caption object is unreadable** in the text layer (T-sub-4), and the 4.7.1
  heading has the same loss.

## Declared deficits

Three tables print with captions and full grids but have **no measured region at all**:
Table 1 (p6, a 1-column 16-line algorithm box), Table 6 (p12, the 4 x 4 time analysis) and
Table 10 (p18, the appendix algorithm section). The region pass stopped on the footer band
under Table 1 and found nothing on p18 above it. Appendix Table 2 is the partial case: its
p23 fragment has no region either, but its p24 body does, so the table itself is measured
while its caption and area-name header are not. All four are recorded here as deficits
rather than invented, per the lane rule.
