## When the paper goes three-dimensional, and what the plates do instead

Never. No exhibit carries a perspective frame, a mesh or an isometric projection, and no
exhibit encodes a value in colour: `colorbar` is false on all 22 rows [geometry.three_d]
[geometry.colorbar].

What replaces dimensionality is series count. Every plate is a single panel with a single
layout string '1' and `shared_axes` false, and where the paper needs two views it prints
two numbered exhibits rather than two panels - the three prediction charts are Figures 8,
9 and 10, the two buy-sell plates are Figures 13 and 14, and the three market regimes are
Figure 16 printed three times [geometry.panel_count] [geometry.panel_layout]
[geometry.shared_axes].

Legend practice splits by whether two series share a frame: the three plates that put an
observation next to a prediction carry the key inside the axes (Figures 8, 9, 17), and the
other nineteen carry no key - including the buy-sell plates and the three regime plates,
whose coloured dots are never explained in the caption or the prose [geometry.legend].
