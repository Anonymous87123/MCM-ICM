## Templates this paper hands you

**Which section takes which exhibit.** The schedule is one exhibit type per section and it
never mixes: symbols takes the notation grid (Table 1), the two model sections take the box
trees that decompose cost and benefit (Figures 1 to 3, one tree per tier of the derivation),
the evaluation section takes the criteria-weight grid (Table 2), the solution section takes
the result grid (Table 3), sensitivity takes the only plotted chart (Figure 4), and the
appendix takes everything pasted (map, typeset cost table, scanned revenue table). What to
copy: A TREE INSTEAD OF A PROSE DEFINITION. Figures 1 and 2 turn "cost splits into economic
and environmental, and environmental splits into three replacement costs" into ten and six
boxes respectively, and because the same five environmental leaves then reappear as the five
criteria of Table 2, the reader has already met the weighting vector before the AHP is
introduced. What to copy with care: the appendix dump. Three borrowed exhibits with three
provenances (a web map, a typeset table from a Chinese paper, a scan of a book page that
still carries its own chapter head) is an evidence pile rather than an argument, and none of
the three is referred to by number anywhere in the text.

**When three dimensions appear.** Never - and the paper solves the problem three dimensions
would solve by CASCADING THE TREE instead. A cost surface could have been drawn as a surface
mesh; this paper draws a rooted box tree with right-angle branch rules, one tier per level of
the derivation (C -> C1, C2 -> C12, C21-C23 -> C121-C123), and lets depth-in-the-tree carry
the nesting. What to copy: right-angle rules rather than arrows, every label live text (so
the labels are searchable, quotable and translatable), and a box per named variable so the
figure doubles as a symbol table. What not to copy: the grey fill. Four boxes in Figure 1 and
three leaves in Figure 2 are shaded, the shaded sets do not correspond to any class the
paper names, and no key explains them - a fill with no key is decoration that reads as
meaning.

**Table type against data scale.** Five grids, four of them doing different jobs, and the
rule style turns out to encode PROVENANCE rather than house style: Table 1 and Table 2 are
booktabs (heavy top, heavy header rule, heavy bottom, no verticals) because they were
authored here; Table 3 has exactly one vertical rule and no outer box; and both appendix
tables are fully boxed with every vertical drawn because they were pasted from print. So when
a census sees a grid whose rules disagree with the rest of the paper, check the provenance
before recording it as an inconsistency. Two more reusable observations: (1) split grids in
this paper NEVER repeat their header - Table 1 moves caption and header to the foot of one
page and puts fifteen rows at the top of the next, Table 2 loses its last two rank rows to a
bare boxed strip with no header at all, so the strip is a continuation and not a new table;
(2) the merged header is where the real information sits - the appendix cost table's one
spanned cell divides "cost per kW" into current dollars and constant 1982 dollars, which is
the whole point of the table, and a flat extraction that drops the span loses it.

**Caption template.** Two dialects, both worth knowing: `Figure <n>：<label>` with a FULLWIDTH
colon for figures and `Table<n>: <label>` with an ASCII colon and no space before the number
for tables. Three to fourteen words, noun phrase, no finite verb, no unit, no condition, no
conclusion. Position splits by exhibit type without exception: every figure's caption below,
every table's above. Units are never in the caption - they are in the grid (Table 1 has a
Unit column with "$", "m2", "m3"; Table 3 writes "$4,120million" inside a cell; the appendix
table puts 10k kW and USD/kW in the header strings) - which is the right split: a caption
names the exhibit, the grid carries the measurement. Guard against a break the substring
check cannot see: the appendix exhibits are captioned ON THE PAGE and the text layer returns
NONE of those captions. The layer holds only "Appendices", the three lead-in lines and the
Matlab listing, so the map's title line and the "Tab. 3-2" caption are pixels alone. When a
section's pages are image-dominant, absence of a caption token in the layer is not evidence
of absence on the page.

**Context-linking sentence template.** The positive pattern ends the sentence ON the number
and one tick later reads the exhibit: "To demonstrate our analysis of short run cost, we use
diagram in Figure.1" then the figure; "Figure.4 illustrates a function of the score varying
with the weight." then "We can see that both of them converge to 5 with the growth of a," -
a reading that names an ordinate value, which is stronger than the direction-only readings
this lane usually records. Note the pointer spelling ("Figure.1" with a period, "Table2"
without a space) is stable enough to grep but inconsistent between the two tables, and that
the same exhibit can be spelled "Table2" on one page and "Table.2" on the next. The negative
pattern is the appendix: three bare deixis lead-ins with no numbers ("This is the map of the
power station:", "These are the cost tables:", "These are Matlab codes:"), and the third one
announces CODE, so the two scanned tables that follow it are reached by nothing at all.
Deixis works only when the very next object is the exhibit; here the page order is table
fragment, table, table, then code, and the only sentence names the code.
