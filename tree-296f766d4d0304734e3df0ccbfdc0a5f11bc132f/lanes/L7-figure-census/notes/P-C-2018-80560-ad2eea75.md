## Templates this paper hands you

**Which section takes which exhibit.** The paper runs classification, then profile,
then index, then forecast, then policy, and every stop gets one exhibit type: Data
Processing holds the two classification diagrams (2.3), the profile section holds two
grids sold as figures (3.1's six-column state grid, 3.2's demographic web-table) plus
a map (3.2), the index section holds the symbol grid in 4.2 and two more grids sold as
figures (4.2's cost and influence-rate grids), section 4.3 is where the charts begin
(two line charts over a filled-area pair, then the EROI pair), section 5.1 is one
fifteen-panel regression grid, section 5.2 one prediction grid, section 5.3 four
two-panel line pairs, 5.4 one sketch map, section 6 one sensitivity line and the
Conclusions two uncaptioned target grids. Nothing in the abstract, the assumptions or
the memo carries an exhibit.

**Three-dimensional condition: fires once, and badly.** One row of 23 is 3-D: Figure
4's four exploded perspective pies with drop shadows, and it is also the exhibit with
no readable number on it, because the perspective hides the slice areas and no data
label or percentage is printed. Every other exhibit encodes value as height or
position against a printed axis, and no row carries a colourbar. The rule this paper
supports: perspective buys nothing a reader can use, and the one exhibit that takes it
is the one exhibit whose values cannot be read.

**Table type vs data scale.** Two columns is a denotation list (Table 1, eight
symbols) or a parameter list (the p13 grid, ten parameters) and prints with a full
frame plus a centre vertical because the stub and the definition must be separated.
Five printed columns is the paper's data shape (Figures 7 and 8, both page-21
target grids) and prints horizontals with interior verticals and no outer side
borders. Nine columns by twelve rows is the one wide grid, Table 2, and it is the only
grid that leaves the text block: 496 pt against a 424 pt measure, because the four
states each need a 2025 and a 2050 column and the author widened the grid instead of
splitting it. Two-row header with empty cells appears exactly there, and it is a
failure of the spanner, not a design: the state names are centred in the first of
their two columns and abut the dividing rule.

**Caption template.** `Figure N: <noun phrase naming what is plotted>` and
`Table N: <noun phrase naming the content>`, 4-17 words, one sentence, colon attached
to the number, no unit, no condition, no conclusion. The side is uniform here: every
one of the 25 captioned rows prints BELOW its artwork, tables included - the opposite
of the lane's usual "table above". A box holding two printed figures quotes the two
caption strings as they print, in one band, because that is what the page gives you;
and one caption in the paper exists only as pixels inside a pasted page image, so a
census that trusts the text layer alone loses it.

**Context-weld template.** Pointer clause ending in a colon, then the exhibit, then a
reading sentence that names the number the exhibit produced: `We classify the energy
production database by three aspects - usage, source and sector. The specific
classification is listed as follows.` -> diagram -> next classification. For the
charts the weld is `The following graph is based on the sensitivity test of Arizona
Natural Gas consumption, and the results are shown as follow:` -> plot -> nothing, or
a reading sentence that outruns the art. Two failure modes to avoid: the paper points
at four exhibits with 'these figures' and prints no caption for two of them, and it
repeats one pointer paragraph verbatim on consecutive pages so that the exhibit it
introduces sits 290 pt above the sentence that names it.
