# P-F-2021-2109443-9398701f — distilled rules

- Sixty-one measured regions on twenty-five pages hold twenty-one printed exhibits once every region is named: thirteen figures, eight tables, and no unnumbered apparatus row. The other forty break down as twenty-five page-foot stamps, eight fragments and seven display equations, so the region count is 2.9x the exhibit count - but the surplus here is fragments of three composite figures more than it is mathematics, the opposite balance to the previous paper in this block. [place.doc_order] therefore interleaves a row per panel with a row per exhibit.
- One printed table in this paper is invisible to the layout pass: Table 5, 'the divisions of McKinsey Matrix', prints at the foot of p12 as plain text rows with no rules, no fills and no image, so there is no region to measure. The only region the pass finds on p12 is display equation (8) at y 283.9-311.1, well above the table. A table printed with no drawn geometry at all is the block's first genuinely unmeasurable exhibit, and [table.rule_style] cannot be reported for it.
- Table rules in this paper are drawn as FILLS more often than as strokes: Table 7's 39 drawing items contain no thin stroke and Table 2's 108 items contain only seven, because both replace body rules with light-blue cell banding (79 greys at 0.851, 8 at 0.557, 21 black on Table 2) and Table 7 marks itself with one grey row fill. So [table.zebra] true here means the banding IS the grid, and a classifier that reads only stroke colour would call Table 7 'no-rules' when it prints four horizontal rules.
- Table 8's drawings are also glyphs as much as rules: 54 of its 106 items are thin fills, and Table 4's 76 items break down as 21 thin (19 black, 2 white) plus 55 cell and band fills. The rule count per table is therefore not a rule count at all - [table.rule_style] 'booktabs' here rests on the drawn horizontals at the head and foot of the block, never on the total item count.
- Two table regions clip the header row that their rules belong to: Table 6's bbox starts at y 261.5 with its header printing at y 247, and Table 7's at y 196.4 with its group header above that. So [table.rows] and [table.header_rows] for those two rows come from the page text while the geometry comes from the box, and the two must be read together - counting rows from the bbox alone would drop a header from each.
- Caption side is kind-driven with no exception in twenty-one rows: every figure caption prints BELOW its art and every table caption ABOVE, and the gaps are far wider than in the previous paper - figures clear their region by 2.8 to 9.6 pt, and tables clear theirs by 1.25 pt on six rows but by 19.6 pt on Table 6 and 35.8 pt on Table 7 - so [caption.position] 'above' covers both a tight and a very loose assembly. Figure 9 is the one row whose gap depends on which box is measured: its caption sits at y 448.8, 2.7 pt under the exhibit's bottom panel, but the recorded region covers only the top row, so the gap against that row's own bbox is 149.4 pt.
- Caption punctuation has one break: the paper prints 'Figure N.' and 'Table N.' with a full stop on twenty of the twenty-one exhibits, and 'Table 1: Notations used in this paper' with an ASCII colon on the twenty-first. [caption.numbering] therefore reads 'Table N:' once and 'Table N.' or 'Figure N.' everywhere else, which is a single typographic slip rather than a house style.
- Two captions print with the wrong capitalisation and one is welded to its neighbour's prose: six table captions open lower case ('the HA indicator system', 'the indicators' weights', 'the divisions of McKinsey Matrix', 'the health assessment of selected countries', 'the comparisons between Italy and European countries', 'the relevant test results of equation 10'), and the p21 raw line carries TWO captions - 'Figure 10. The variation of EP and SI with policies' followed on the same line by 'Figure 11. The variation of distances'. So [caption.verbatim] for Figure 11 is a substring of one welded line, not a line of its own.
- Twelve of the twenty-one exhibits are single bitmaps and a thirteenth - Figure 7 - is a vector frame wrapping four of them, while all eight tables are drawn as vector rules and text. The resolution is much lower than in the previous paper: 142 dpi (Figure 8) to 236 dpi (Figure 4), with Figure 5 at 157, and Figure 13 rescaled to 233x182 dpi - a 28 per cent aspect distortion, the worst in the block. Figure 7 is the one exhibit whose region is vector while the chart inside it is raster, so [geometry.family] again comes off the crops and not off the text layer.
- Three exhibits are composite panels and all three are counted differently by the detector: Figure 6 is three separate rasters in a row (three regions), Figure 7 is four rasters inside one vector frame (five regions), and Figure 9 is two rows plus a decorative magnifier (three regions). [geometry.panel_count] reads 3, 4 and 4 for these rows, so a region count and a panel count are three different quantities in this paper and neither equals the exhibit count.
- The magnifier inset on p20 is a drawn picture that plots nothing: a 144x158 px light-blue glass at 159 dpi, the only region in the whole block that is neither chart, table, formula nor furniture. It is excluded as a fragment of Figure 9, but [place.area_frac] on a small region is not on its own evidence of a fragment - the crop is.
- Citation is the thinnest measured: four numbered pointers in the entire paper - Table 1 on its own page, Table 8 on its own page, and Figures 10 and 11 both named on p20 before their art prints on p21 - so seventeen of the twenty-one exhibits are never cited by number. [mention.lead_pages] reads +1 twice and 0 twice, and the paper's other references to exhibits are deictic ('the following figure and table'), never numbered.
- Six tables open their caption in lower case and three name a variable the caption never defines: Figure 13's caption counts weights 'with and without disturbance' without naming the disturbance, Table 9 promises a disturbed prediction while also printing the undisturbed baseline, and Table 8 is named for 'equation 10' which it does not print. So [caption.variables] true marks the five captions that name a plotted variable, and the rest defer the naming to the body.
