1	As a mathematician you’re used to solving problems on your own, so that’s the way I approach cycling. —— Anna Kiesenhofer, the winner of women’s road race in Tokyo 2020 As Anna Kiesenhofer, a Ph.D. in mathematics, said, a proper mathematical model can help cyclists to plan their races better and more efficiently.
2	And that is exactly what we do in this paper.
3	We begin with the analysis on energy supply system of the cyclists.
4	According to basic biological knowledge, we divide the contribution of overall output power into three different energy supply sys- tems: ATP-CP system, glycolysis system and aerobic system.
5	We base our model on ODEs to describe the change of content of various substances in human body during cycling.
6	Based on our energy supply model, we then define the power profiles of the time trial specialists and sprinters, with different genders considered.
7	The comparison between our theoretical power curves and real-world power curves implies the reliability of the energy model we have developed.
8	It is worth noting that power curves here is presented as an intermediate results.
9	We use our energy model for further discussions, which is more essential and accurate.
10	Then in order to evaluate the power needed for maintaining a given speed, we analyze the motion of bicycle-cyclist system.
11	We include different drag resistances, hilly terrain and sharp turnings into our model.
12	A motion equation is obtained here to determine the relation between the acceleration and propulsive force.
13	With the energy model and motion model developed, we discuss the optimal strategies on each characteristic terrains.
14	It is found that the optimal strategy in most cases is to maintain a fixed speed, threshold speed, all along the race.
15	The exception is when on a hilly course or sharply curved road.
16	Slowing down is generally needed for cycling uphill or sharp turnings.
17	Accordingly, we propose a feasible strategy, which is a combination of optimal strategies on each specific terrains.
18	To test our model in real world conditions, we first reconstruct the courses and obtain necessary data by processing the official roadmaps.
19	Then we simulate the actual performance of cyclists of four different types on three courses.
20	The simulation shows that the optimal time on 2021 UCI Champi- onship in Belgium is 53.02 minutes, which is very close to the champion’s 57.78 minutes on this course.
21	We evaluate the sensitivity of the deviations from our strategy, where we find 30% deviation only gives 3.11% difference in time.
22	Then for sensitivity of weather, we focus on the wind directions and strengths.
23	The analysis shows that the wind greatly affects the strategy adopted and expected perfor- mance.
24	Finally, we modify our model to include Team Time Trials, where the drafting effect is considered to reduce the overall aerodynamic resistance.
25	We propose a strategy for TTT that cyclists rotate to take pulls and modify themselves before and in sprint stage.
26	The team strategy can improve the speed by 14.43% compared to individual case.
