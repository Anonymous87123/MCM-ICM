    Drivers determine whether or not to change lanes mainly by estimating the
gap between the two vehicles adjacent to them, and accepting if the gap would
be enough for them to merge in. Critical gap (tc ) is defined here as the gap for
which the number of accepted gaps shorter than it is equal to the number of
rejected gaps longer than it. Thus, an average vehicle would decide to merge
if a gap is larger than the critical gap. We estimate the critical gap as the
summation of the difference traveled between vehicle B and vehicle A during
the lane change, and the safety distance from the following vehicle as in the
previous model. An assumption must be made here about the average time it
takes to change lanes; a study conducted by Toledo and Zohar by observing cars
in a variety of traffic conditions [13] found that this value, which we call τ , was
approximately 4.6±2.3 seconds. Assuming that car A continues to travel the
same speed, u as it changes lanes and using the pythagorean theorem gives:
