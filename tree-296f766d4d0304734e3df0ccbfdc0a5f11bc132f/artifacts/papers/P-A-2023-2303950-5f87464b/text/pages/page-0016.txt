5.2 Evaluation of the Benefit for a Multi-Plants Community
     For a community of plants, there are two aspects to evaluate its performance facing the
irregular weather conditions. One is the weight of all plants, which should be as high as possible.
Another is that extreme fluctuations should be avoided to ensure the stability of the ecosystem.
To conclude, an ideal ecosystem is supposed to reach the balance between its scale and stability.

      If biodiversity actually brings benefits to the plant community, it can be reflected by higher
scale or lower fluctuation. To evaluate the balance between these two aspects, we employ the
method of TOPSIS. TOPSIS (Technique for Order Preference by Similarity to an ideal solution)
is a commonly used method in multi-objective decision analysis. Hence, we employ this model
to evaluate the performance of the evolution of a plant community. If the performance of the
evaluation is better than the case where there is only one plant species, it means the plant
community has benefited from the biodiversity. Therefore, we are able to determine how many
