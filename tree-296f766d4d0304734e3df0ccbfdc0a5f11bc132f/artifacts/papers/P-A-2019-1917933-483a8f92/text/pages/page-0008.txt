3.4.2   Reasonable Bounds on Dragon BMR
Other studies have suggested that metabolic rate does not vary nearly this much consistently. Instead,
most endothermic vertebrates, under which our dragons fall, have a similar rate of from .3–9W/kg.
This translates to 3kcal/kg-92kcal/kg each day. Dragons are likely to be somewhere on this spectrum,
which actually applies to not just endothermic vertebrates but most living creatures. Taking the
entire range using these measures, Balerion could have been eating anywhere from 1.32 to 40.46 cows
per day. Our original estimate falls just below this range. Yet, if he were truly burning 40.46 cows a
day, his power would be 979 kW. A standard toaster needs 1.5 kW. The energy consumption would
explain a dragon’s high body heat, but also make the Balerion be dissipating heat at a ridiculously
high rate, since his surface area only scales upwards by a square factor not a cube factor.

We can also see that the largest known animal, the blue whale, does not follow our model which
begins with a human. A blue whale, averaging 173 tonnes, burns somewhere in the range of 1.5
million kcal per day, or 8.7 kcal/kg per day. [25]
