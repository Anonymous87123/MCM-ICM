         As G and θ increase, the respective f (G) and f (θ) exhibit a rapid
     decrease. Since the bounded integral of f (x, y) produces a POC, G
     and θ have the same effect on POC as they do on f (x, y). In short,
     as G + θ decreases, the probability of finding the lost plane increases.
         Increasing θ will directly increase the search area because the X
     distance is measured in mile-multiples of σx = Gh sin (θ) which is pro-
     portional to a chosen 0 ≤ θ ≤ π2 . The following graphs are generated
     using the constant parameter values found in the Working Example
     (Section 3).




                  Figure 5: P OD(θ) where θ is in radians

         As θ increases, POD rapidly decreases from one to zero. The most
     intriguing part of P OD(θ) is that the graph suggests P OD(0) ≈ 1.
     Also, the P OD is extremely small for any plane that has veered more
     than ninety degrees off the IFP.
         Increasing the glide ratio effects the POD similarly to increasing
     θ.
