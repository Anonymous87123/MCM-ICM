8    Future Work

    • Standardization of data set: It is currently difficult to compare
      geographical profiling method performance to previous research be-
      cause each study uses a different set of serial crime data to determine
      performance results. An effort to compile a large, comprehensive list
      of serial crime data to which all methods would be compared would
      greatly help the development of geographical profiling techniques.

    • Use of other relevant geographical information: In this pa-
      per we only consider the spatiotemporal relationships between crimes.
      Work by O’Leary and Brown et al. develop the notion of a feature
      space that groups crime locations by their relevant geographical fea-
      tures, such as population density or proximity to a major highway
      [12, 11]. The probability that a future crime happens at a particular
      location is then determined by the proximity of the location to previ-
      ous crimes in the feature space. Having more information about what
      connects crime locations could potentially improve our geographical
      profiling methods.
