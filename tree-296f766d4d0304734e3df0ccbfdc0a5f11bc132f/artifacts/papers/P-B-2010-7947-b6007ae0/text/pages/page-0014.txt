       – Standard Deviation Rectangles: These are rectangular areas

                              x + (−c σlon , −c σlat )
                              x + (−c σlon , c σlat )
                              x + (c σlon , c σlat )

         where x is the centroid of the crime locations, and σlon and σlat
         are the standard deviations of the longitudes and latitudes of the
         crime locations [15].
       – Standard Deviation Ellipses: These are elliptical areas, ori-
         ented along the trend line of the data in the least-squares sense.
         A standard deviation ellipse is an ellipse with its center at the
         centroid of the crime locations, rotated clockwise by an angle θ,
         and with axis lengths given by 2cσlon and 2cσlat , where θ, σlon ,
         and σlat are calculated as in [16].

    In both of the above methods, c is a constant that determines the range
    of the area. Common values are c = 1 for the 68th percentile area and
    c = 2 for the 95th percentile area. See Figure 7 for an illustration of
    these percentile areas.
