# Plan for Sequentially Studying the MCM/ICM Papers and Building the Master Skill

## Long-Term Goal

`finally delivering a huge skill`

This phrase is a shorthand for the complete goal in the controlling prompt: a multi-level, fully detailed, actionable, competition-oriented, and evidence-backed MCM/ICM master skill. The paper study is not an end in itself. Every reading, extraction, comparison, deduction, script, reference file, test, and review must contribute to a final, installable system that can guide real competition work. A large pile of summaries would not satisfy the goal. The final skill must convert what is learned into usable decisions, procedures, templates, diagnostics, and quality checks while preserving traceability to the source papers.

## Source Boundary

The only current paper source is the extracted directory:

`F:\MCM\MCM-ICM-master`

The deleted ZIP archive is outside the scope. It will not be treated as a backup, a second corpus, an integrity source, or a study item. All inventories and checks will be regenerated from the files that actually exist under `MCM-ICM-master` at execution time.

The pasted prompt is the controlling requirements document. This plan expands it into an operational research and skill-construction program. The prompt supplies mandatory constraints, but it does not replace independent analysis. Its claims about problem types, judge preferences, writing quality, or visualization quality will be treated as hypotheses or requirements to be tested against the paper evidence rather than repeated mechanically.

## Requirements Provenance and Controlled Exceptions

### Controlling and Supplemental Requirements

The pasted construction prompt remains the source of the four major skill branches, A-F separation, sequential single-paper learning, actionability, and final delivery requirements. A later supplemental execution directive adds the following priority without replacing those branches:

- Record substantive errors so the finished skill can prevent recurrence.
- Do not let error finding become the main product; learn the strengths that made each paper useful or persuasive.
- Give language mining first priority during the current study phase.
- Look especially for recurring sentence and paragraph structures in Abstract/Summary and Introduction, while still recording every available chapter.
- Treat highly repeated, patterned wording as evidence of a reusable rhetorical routine when its function, information order, replaceable slots, variation, and boundary can be identified. Do not discard repetition merely because it is formulaic, and do not copy source wording as a template.
- Interpret the author's "thinking logic" primarily as paper organization: section order, paragraph progression, problem decomposition, model handoffs, claim-support links, result ordering, and the return from computation to the requested decision.
- Perform fact-error checking as a bounded safety and evidence-integrity pass after positive language and organization mining. It must not become the main product or substitute for reconstructing how the paper works.
- After every major artifact block, audit whether language and reusable positive evidence still outweigh weakness-oriented work.

These supplemental requirements must be labeled as later execution guidance rather than quoted as text from the original construction prompt.

### Mandatory Intellectual Priority and Execution Order

The four final skill branches remain present, but they are not equally weighted during paper learning. The governing order for every new paper, every historical migration, every review, and every downstream skill build is:

```text
source identity + full text + visual chapter boundaries
        |
        v
language imitation and chapter saturation
        |
        v
observable article organization and argument progression
        |
        v
formula-level model and important-visual retention
        |
        v
bounded formula/fact warning pass
        |
        v
validate -> freeze bytes -> hash -> append transaction -> rebuild compliance
```

This is a gate order, not a list of topics that may be performed in any convenient sequence. A paper may not enter model-first deep reconstruction while its language pass is still incomplete. It may not enter the weakness or fact-error pass while positive organization artifacts are missing. It may not close because the paper card is historically marked `closed`. It closes under the current protocol only after all ordered gates pass.

The intended final skill architecture is therefore deliberately unequal:

1. **Language imitation is the first and largest evidence dimension.** It captures how the authors actually phrase, sequence, compress, qualify, connect, and repeat moves across real chapters.
2. **Organization logic is second.** It captures how the article is built and how one section, paragraph, model, result, and decision hands off to the next.
3. **Model and visualization retention is mandatory but follows the two writing passes.** Core equations, variables, algorithms, interfaces, and important figures must remain exact enough to reproduce or audit.
4. **Formula and fact checking is a final guardrail.** It prevents the final skill from repeating defects, but it must never become the identity of the paper analysis.

Any instruction, checklist, report, script, agent prompt, or later plan amendment that reverses this order is a blocking protocol regression.

### Non-Negotiable Interpretation and Dual-Axis Gate

The priority statement is operational, not decorative. Future agents must apply the following interpretations exactly:

| User requirement | Required interpretation | Invalid substitute | Required proof |
|---|---|---|---|
| Language style must be imitated and carefully learned. | Recover the paper's reusable rhetorical mechanisms at word, sentence, paragraph, and chapter level, beginning with Summary/Abstract and Introduction and continuing through every substantive chapter. | A vocabulary list, a few polished quotations, generic "academic tone" advice, or a final prose-polish pass after modeling. | Chapter coverage, saturation passes, language units, paragraph chains, original instantiations, and anti-copy tests. |
| Highly repeated and patterned expressions are especially important. | Search for exact repetition, grammatical-frame repetition, and semantic-move repetition; retain occurrence locations, stable function, information order, typed slots, variants, and boundaries. | Deleting a routine as formulaic, counting connectors without rhetorical work, or copying the source frame verbatim. | A pattern record plus at least two original examples in unrelated scenarios. |
| Preserve the authors' thinking logic. | Reconstruct observable article organization: global route, section contracts, paragraph progression, argument chains, model/result handoffs, and return to the requested decision. | Speculation about private psychology, a list of topics, a chronology of formulas, or a catalogue of mistakes. | Positive organization artifacts that remain intelligible after paper-specific facts, names, and numbers are replaced with placeholders. |
| Preserve models and visuals. | After the writing passes, retain the complete source-observed set of core models, central equations, algorithms, validation links, dependency edges, and important visuals. | One representative model, model-name lists, selected equations, a chart count, or omissions justified by language priority. | Exact-set retention and disposition checks against validated model, card, audit, and rendered-page records. |
| Check formula and fact errors last. | Open a bounded warning pass only after positive language, organization, model, and visual artifacts pass. | Reading the paper mainly to attack claims, using weaknesses to populate earlier gates, or letting correction volume dominate the paper record. | `last-pass-warning-only`, `completed_after_positive_passes=true`, precise source anchors, and warning counts remaining a strict minority of positive language. |

Two independent axes must pass. They cannot compensate for each other:

```text
PRIORITY AXIS                                FIDELITY AXIS
language first and strictly largest          every substantive chapter covered
        -> organization second               every core model/equation retained
        -> model/visual third                 every important visual dispositioned
        -> warnings last                      every handoff and source ambiguity recorded
```

- **Priority failure:** model or weakness work begins early, language is not the strictly largest rolling evidence dimension, or organization is reduced to defect diagnosis.
- **Fidelity failure:** language volume is high but a core model, central equation, important visual, chapter, paragraph chain, or decision handoff is missing.
- Passing one axis never cures failure on the other. More language cannot excuse dropped equations or figures; exact equations cannot excuse shallow language learning.
- Counts may not be padded. One language unit must represent one materially distinct rhetorical move or a documented variant with changed function, order, grammar, or boundary. Duplicate quotations, connector-only fragments, contents-page text, and cosmetic paraphrases do not increase language coverage.
- The rolling priority window is exactly the latest five distinct native substantive paper-learning events in transaction chronology. Metadata-only corrections are skipped; a later substantive correction replaces the earlier occurrence of the same paper.
- In that five-paper window, language must be **strictly greater** than every other single evidence dimension, weakness must be a strict minority of language, and model/visual presence must remain nonzero. These rolling smoke tests supplement rather than replace per-paper exact-retention validators.

### Evidence-Tested Exceptions

The following departures from the prompt's literal wording are explicit, bounded, and retained unless evidence overturns them:

| Exception ID | Literal prompt position | Operational rule | Basis |
|---|---|---|---|
| `EX-VIZ-01` | Advanced visual complexity is treated as inherently preferable and basic chart forms are broadly rejected. | Choose the simplest chart that fully represents the analytical question, uncertainty, comparison, and decision. Add complexity only when the data or model requires it. | Decorative complexity can reduce accuracy, readability, accessibility, and judge auditability. |
| `EX-JUDGE-01` | Every rule should explain an implicit judge preference. | Every promoted rule carries `judge_relevance`, whose status is `direct-evidence`, `correlational-inference`, or `unknown`. Never infer causality from award status alone. | Awarded papers show coexistence with success, not the cause of a score. |
| `EX-TYPE-01` | A-F are mapped to one fixed modeling attribute each. | Preserve A-F labels strictly from path and filename, but treat topic and model-family descriptions as evidence-tested taxonomies rather than classification rules. | Real papers within one problem type use multiple and changing model families. |
| `EX-PILOT-01` | Every intellectual read appears to occur only in the final queue order. | Permit the twelve-paper cross-type calibration set solely to test extraction and schemas. Pilot papers keep their real queue positions and count as complete only after full protocol revalidation. | Early schema calibration prevents hundreds of structurally incomplete records. |
| `EX-SHARED-01` | All skill content is labeled A-F and no generalized mixed summary is allowed. | Permit a shared invariant and routing layer only when every entry retains an explicit A-F support matrix and never pools type evidence into an unlabeled universal claim. | Mathematical correctness, evidence honesty, and retrieval routing can be shared without erasing type-specific provenance. |

No other literal departure is implicit. New exceptions require an ID, evidence, scope, and explicit change record.

# Part I - Rough Draft

## Rough Draft of the Approach

1. Freeze a read-only inventory of the extracted corpus and assign every file a stable identifier.
2. Separate candidate Outstanding/Finalist papers from problem statements, result sheets, triage guides, datasets, code, unrelated awards, and supporting material.
3. Classify a paper as A, B, C, D, E, or F only from its filename and parent-folder path. Never infer the class from its subject matter.
4. Build six independent queues, A through F. Within each queue, study papers chronologically and then lexicographically by path so the order is deterministic.
5. Extract each PDF into a searchable representation while preserving page boundaries, figures, tables, formulas, headings, and the original file path.
6. Pilot the reading protocol on a small but varied set, revise the schema, and freeze the first stable protocol before full-scale study.
7. Study exactly one paper at a time in the mandatory order: establish source and chapter boundaries; saturate chapter language beginning with Summary/Abstract and Introduction; reconstruct observable article organization; retain formula-level models and important visuals; then run the bounded formula/fact warning pass.
8. Store claims as evidence-bearing records. Distinguish direct observations, faithful paraphrases, cross-paper inductions, and independent extensions.
9. Immediately after finishing one paper, update only its corresponding A-F knowledge branch, run consistency checks, and record what changed.
10. Periodically consolidate within a single problem type without mixing evidence across types.
11. Convert mature findings into a progressively disclosed skill: a concise dispatcher in `SKILL.md`, detailed one-level references, deterministic scripts, and only genuinely reusable assets.
12. Test the skill on realistic MCM/ICM tasks, including held-out papers, leakage tests, contradiction tests, and six-type routing tests.
13. Continue until every eligible paper has passed the full study protocol, every promoted rule is traceable, and the final skill passes structural and behavioral validation.

## Rough Definition of Done

The project is done only when all of the following are true:

- Every in-scope paper has a manifest row, a deterministic A-F label, a completed paper card, and an incorporation record.
- No paper is silently omitted, duplicated, or classified by content-based guesswork.
- The language, modeling, integrated logic, and visualization branches all contain actionable rules with scenarios, procedures, examples or templates, prohibitions, and evidence.
- Problem A-F knowledge remains independently addressable and does not collapse into a generic mixed summary.
- The final skill is installable, valid, searchable, and usable under realistic context limits.
- The skill can produce and critique competition work, not merely describe what historical teams did.
- Tests demonstrate that the skill routes correctly, cites its internal evidence, handles conflicting practices, avoids unsupported claims, and degrades honestly when evidence is weak.
- The full corpus has been learned sequentially and the last paper has been incorporated without breaking earlier material.

The remainder of this document turns this rough draft into a detailed operating plan.

# Part II - Detailed Plan

## 1. Mission, Deliverables, and Non-Goals

### 1.1 Mission

The mission is to transform the highest-award MCM/ICM paper corpus into a reusable reasoning and production system. The transformation has four linked layers:

1. Corpus layer: know exactly which files exist, what role each file plays, and which papers are eligible.
2. Evidence layer: preserve what each paper actually demonstrates, down to page, section, table, figure, equation, and quoted phrase where useful.
3. Knowledge layer: induce type-specific rules, patterns, counterpatterns, exceptions, and decision criteria across papers.
4. Skill layer: expose the knowledge through an efficient workflow that can solve, write, review, visualize, and validate new MCM/ICM work.

Each layer must remain auditable. A user should be able to move from a recommendation in the final skill back to the normalized rule, from the rule back to its supporting paper observations, and from each observation back to a source file and page location.

### 1.2 Final Deliverables

The ultimate delivery should be a single large skill folder rather than one enormous monolithic prompt. Its exact name can be finalized during implementation, but a working name is `mcm-icm-master-skill`. It should contain:

- `SKILL.md`: a concise dispatcher, core workflow, selection rules, safety rules, and direct links to all relevant references.
- `agents/openai.yaml`: generated interface metadata that accurately reflects the finished skill.
- `references/`: the detailed A-F knowledge base, evidence method, chapter language system, modeling decision system, logic system, visualization system, validation guidance, and source indexes.
- `scripts/`: deterministic corpus inventory, extraction, validation, search, linting, and report-generation utilities where scripts materially improve reliability.
- `assets/`: only reusable output resources actually needed by the skill, such as competition-paper skeletons or chart-style configuration. Assets will not be created merely to make the skill look large.

Intermediate research artifacts will be maintained during construction, but the final installed skill will contain only resources needed for operation. Process diaries, redundant summaries, scratch files, and convenience documentation will not be shipped as skill clutter.

### 1.3 What Will Not Count as Success

The following outcomes are explicitly inadequate:

- A long narrative summarizing papers in chronological order with no operational conversion.
- A list of model names without selection conditions, equations, parameter logic, algorithms, or validation requirements.
- A vocabulary list that ignores chapter, scenario, and problem type.
- A chart gallery that recommends visual complexity without analytical purpose.
- Unsupported statements beginning with phrases such as "judges prefer" when no evidence basis is recorded.
- A common A-F summary that loses type-specific provenance.
- A `SKILL.md` so large that it consumes the context window before the user task begins.
- A skill that can repeat templates but cannot adapt, critique, test, or explain tradeoffs.
- Silent exclusion of hard-to-extract, image-only, malformed, or contradictory papers.

### 1.4 What Already Exists and Must Be Reused

The workspace already contains a functioning evidence and transaction system. Future execution must extend it rather than create parallel formats.

| Existing asset | Current location | Required reuse |
|---|---|---|
| Corpus identity, A-F classification, source hash, eligibility, and page count | `mcm-skill-work/artifacts/corpus/manifest.jsonl` | Treat as the identity source; correct it only through an explicit manifest migration. |
| Page-aware extraction, rendered pages, contact sheets, and extraction quality | `mcm-skill-work/artifacts/papers/<paper_id>/` | Use for source, layout, equation, and visual verification. |
| Historical paper cards and observations | `mcm-skill-work/artifacts/paper-cards/` and `artifacts/observations/` | Preserve prior evidence; enrich rather than replace it with a second summary system. |
| Per-paper schema-v3 language records | `mcm-skill-work/artifacts/language-units/` | Continue chapter saturation, layout measurement, pattern nomination, and disposition rules. |
| Per-paper formula-level model records | `mcm-skill-work/artifacts/model-units/` | Continue exact variable, equation, condition, algorithm, validation, and handoff retention. |
| Language-first learning audits | `mcm-skill-work/artifacts/learning-audits/` | Enforce priority order, chapter saturation, paragraph chains, organization logic, model retention, visuals, and last-pass warnings. |

### 1.5 NOT in Scope

- Reclassifying a paper by topic, method, title meaning, or model family.
- Restarting already validated papers merely to make artifacts look uniform; migration is driven by derived protocol debt.
- Treating every source-paper defect as a new observation when an existing record already captures the failure mechanism.
- Replacing positive language or organization coverage with a large weakness list.
- Copying source sentences, clauses, or distinctive continuous wording into reusable templates.
- Counting AI Use Reports, generated disclosure text, contents pages, watermarks, or third-party figure text as author language evidence.
- Inventing missing formulas, data, settings, or causal claims and attributing them to the source paper.
- Promoting visual complexity for its own sake.
- Building a second transaction ledger, second compliance axis, or incompatible paper-summary format.
- Expanding to a new paper while any `correction-required` item or dynamically derived migration debt remains unresolved.
- Shipping the final huge skill before its A-F evidence, routing, regression, holdout, and installability gates pass.

## 3. Governing Research Principles

### 3.1 Sequential Means Sequential

Automation may inventory files, hash them, extract raw text, detect pages, and prepare thumbnails in batches. Intellectual learning and skill incorporation must remain single-paper and transactional:

`select one paper -> G0 source/page map -> G1-G3 language -> G4 organization -> G5-G6 model/visual retention -> G7 warnings -> G8 validate/freeze/append -> rebuild compliance -> select next paper`

No batch-generated model summary, language frequency list, or embedding cluster may be promoted directly into the skill. Batch tools may surface candidates, but a paper-level review must confirm meaning, context, and usefulness.

### 3.2 Six Independent Knowledge Streams

The project will maintain six study streams: A, B, C, D, E, and F. Each stream has its own:

- Queue and progress ledger.
- Vocabulary and phrase evidence.
- Chapter-structure observations.
- Modeling taxonomy.
- Assumption patterns.
- Verification patterns.
- Visualization patterns.
- Contradiction log.
- Rule maturity states.
- Skill references and tests.

The same technique appearing in two types will be represented in both branches with separate evidence. A shared dispatcher may point to both branches, but a rule will not lose its type label or provenance through premature deduplication.

### 3.3 Evidence Before Authority

Every substantive record will carry an evidence class:

- `O` - direct observation from one paper.
- `P` - close paraphrase of an identifiable source passage or structure.
- `I` - induction supported by multiple paper observations within one type.
- `E` - independent extension or improvement derived from domain reasoning.
- `J` - judge-preference claim supported by an official guide or a sufficiently broad pattern, with the basis stated.
- `C` - conjecture awaiting more evidence.

The final skill may use observations, inductions, and extensions, but it must not disguise an extension as something historical papers proved. Judge-preference language must be especially conservative. A top paper demonstrates what succeeded once; it does not by itself reveal why a judge scored it highly.

### 3.4 Full Retention Without Uncritical Promotion

The prompt demands full retention of valuable content. This will be implemented through two levels:

1. Evidence retention: all potentially valuable observations remain in paper cards and evidence ledgers, including minority practices and conflicting approaches.
2. Operational promotion: only sufficiently clear, actionable, and scoped findings become default skill rules.
3. Final accessibility: every retained valuable finding, including seeds, minority practices, contradictions, and bounded counterexamples, must remain searchable from the installed skill through a non-default evidence reference or generated index. Maturity controls recommendation strength, not whether valuable evidence disappears from final access.

Nothing valuable is silently discarded or stranded only in the construction workspace, but not every observation becomes universal advice. A practice can remain as an alternative, an exception, a warning, or a low-confidence hypothesis. The installed skill should load this non-default layer only when a query requires it, preserving both completeness and context efficiency.

### 3.5 Actionability Contract

Every promoted rule must answer the following fields:

- What is the rule?
- Which problem type or types does it apply to?
- Which chapter or workflow stage does it apply to?
- What triggering conditions make it relevant?
- What conditions make it inappropriate?
- What is the step-by-step procedure?
- What inputs are required?
- What output or decision should result?
- What mathematical, logical, or rhetorical mechanism makes it work?
- What positive example or fillable template illustrates it?
- What negative example or failure mode should be avoided?
- What evidence supports it?
- How strong is the evidence?
- What is its `judge_relevance` status: direct evidence, correlational inference, or unknown?
- What tests verify correct use?

A record that cannot answer these questions remains evidence or a research note rather than an operational rule.

### 3.6 Independent Intelligence Without Fabricated Reasoning

Independent deduction is required, but private chain-of-thought is not a deliverable. The research record will preserve concise, reviewable rationales: evidence, assumptions, transformations, decision criteria, uncertainty, and conclusions. It will not preserve hidden internal monologue. This creates auditability without confusing speculative thought with source-backed knowledge.

### 3.7 Author Logic Means Observable Article Construction

"Author thinking logic" does not authorize speculation about hidden psychology. It means the organization that can be observed and cited:

- The full-paper route from prompt demand to final answer.
- The order in which chapters establish background, scope, assumptions, data, models, checks, results, and decisions.
- Each chapter's input, question, rhetorical job, mathematical job, output, and handoff.
- Each important paragraph's dominant move, claim, support, qualifier, consequence, and link to the next paragraph.
- Each main argument's trace from requirement to subproblem, method, intermediate result, validation, caveat, and decision.
- Repeated organizational routines such as `Task n -> first/then/finally`, `baseline -> extension -> comparison`, `metric definition -> equation -> direction -> interpretation`, and `actor -> action -> reason -> condition`.

The review question is first: "How did the authors make the article work as an article?" It is not first: "Which facts or equations can be attacked?" Weakness, formula-warning, fact-error, and notation records cannot satisfy any organization field.

### 3.8 Language Evidence Is Positive Imitation Evidence

Language evidence must help a future writer reproduce a rhetorical function in original wording. A valid reusable unit records:

- Source page, source chapter, and short evidence excerpt.
- Within-paper occurrences for repeated routines and cross-paper support when available.
- Stable rhetorical function and the information order that performs it.
- Replaceable slots and their semantic types.
- Observed variants, including changes in connector, voice, tense, clause order, or quantitative payload.
- Appropriate scenario, forbidden scenario, and transfer boundary.
- A fillable abstraction that is not a copied source sentence.
- Disposition: supports an existing pattern, opens a candidate, or remains paper-specific and nonreusable for a substantive reason.

Frequency is not sufficient by itself. Repetition becomes valuable when the function, order, slots, variants, and boundary remain stable. Conversely, high repetition is not a reason to discard a pattern as "formulaic"; in competition papers, the formula may be precisely the reusable rhetorical routine being learned.

## 4. Deterministic Study Order

### 4.1 Queue Construction

Create six queues in the order A, B, C, D, E, F. Within each queue:

1. Sort by four-digit year ascending.
2. Sort by normalized relative path ascending.
3. Sort by normalized filename ascending.
4. Use a stable content hash only as a final tie-breaker.

This yields a reproducible sequence. The sequence is grouped by type to reduce cognitive and storage cross-contamination and to make within-type development visible over time. Chronological order within a type reveals changes in modeling practice, computation, visual standards, and paper conventions.

### 4.2 Paper Identifier

Assign an immutable identifier such as:

`P-{TYPE}-{YEAR}-{NORMALIZED_TEAM_OR_FILE_TOKEN}-{HASH8}`

Example shape:

`P-A-2010-6749-a1b2c3d4`

The identifier must not change if a display title is corrected. All paper cards, evidence rows, rule citations, extraction outputs, and progress logs use this ID.

### 4.3 Queue Status Machine

Each eligible paper moves through these states:

1. `discovered`
2. `scope-verified`
3. `integrity-checked`
4. `text-extracted`
5. `layout-inspected`
6. `section-mapped`
7. `deep-read`
8. `paper-card-complete`
9. `type-branch-updated`
10. `post-update-validated`
11. `closed`

Failure states include:

- `needs-ocr`
- `corrupt`
- `classification-conflict`
- `duplicate-candidate`
- `missing-pages-suspected`
- `manual-equation-review`
- `manual-figure-review`

A failed paper is not skipped. It remains at the blocking state, receives a remediation record, and is returned to the queue before that type can be declared complete.

The queue state records historical workflow only. It must never be used as a synonym for compliance with the current research protocol. Maintain a second, independently derived axis:

1. `protocol-absent`
2. `protocol-partial`
3. `protocol-source-checked`
4. `protocol-current-validated`
5. `correction-required`

`closed` is an immutable or historically accurate lifecycle fact. `protocol-current-validated` is a revocable compliance conclusion based on current artifacts and current validators. A paper may therefore be `closed + correction-required`. Reports must show both axes, and only `protocol-current-validated` papers count as complete under the current protocol.

### 4.4 Progress Counters

Report progress by type and state, not merely by files opened. Useful counters include:

- Eligible papers total.
- Closed papers.
- Papers awaiting OCR or repair.
- Paper cards passing schema validation.
- Observations captured.
- Rules proposed, corroborated, contradicted, promoted, or retired.
- Language templates by chapter and type.
- Modeling patterns by family and type.
- Visualization patterns by analytical purpose and type.
- Unresolved contradictions.
- References without source pointers.

## 5. Phase 0 - Corpus Census and Reproducibility Setup

### 5.1 Regenerate the Inventory

At the start of execution, regenerate the inventory from the live extracted directory. Do not reuse planning-time counts as authoritative. Record:

- Relative path.
- Filename and extension.
- Byte length.
- Last-modified timestamp.
- SHA-256 hash.
- PDF page count when applicable.
- PDF metadata when available.
- Text-extractability probe.
- Suspected image-only status.
- Folder-derived year, problem type, and role.

The inventory script must be deterministic and idempotent. Re-running it without source changes should produce no semantic diff.

### 5.2 Detect Duplicates and Near-Duplicates

Use SHA-256 to find exact duplicates. For text-extractable PDFs, also create normalized-text hashes to detect the same paper wrapped in different metadata or container formatting. For possible near-duplicates, compare:

- Page count.
- Normalized title text.
- Team number.
- First-page fingerprint.
- Normalized extracted-text similarity.

Never delete duplicates automatically. Choose one canonical source record, connect duplicate paths to it, and explain why only one copy enters the intellectual queue.

### 5.3 Role Taxonomy

Assign one role to each file:

- `award-paper-primary`
- `award-paper-finalist`
- `problem-statement`
- `results-document`
- `judging-or-triage-guide`
- `problem-dataset`
- `supplemental-code`
- `supplemental-document`
- `other-award-paper`
- `out-of-scope-problem-type`
- `duplicate`
- `unknown-quarantine`

Role classification must be based on filename/folder signals and explicit metadata, not topic inference. Content inspection may help distinguish a paper from a result sheet, but it may not determine A-F.

### 5.4 Establish a Research Workspace

Keep original source files untouched. Use a separate generated workspace with predictable locations for:

- Manifest and progress database.
- Raw extracted text.
- Page-preserving text.
- Rendered page images or thumbnails.
- Section maps.
- Paper cards.
- Evidence ledgers.
- Type-specific rule registries.
- Validation reports.
- Skill staging output.

Generated artifacts must never be placed inside source-year folders. This prevents accidental confusion between corpus material and research output.

### 5.5 Phase 0 Gate

Do not start full paper study until:

- Every existing file has a manifest row.
- Every eligible paper has exactly one A-F label.
- Every excluded file has a reason.
- Every exact duplicate group has a canonical record.
- All classification conflicts are resolved or quarantined.
- The six deterministic study queues can be reproduced from the manifest.

## 6. Phase 1 - PDF Extraction and Representation

### 6.1 Extraction Strategy

Use a layered extraction pipeline rather than trusting a single text dump:

1. Run a metadata and page-count probe.
2. Extract text while preserving page breaks.
3. Extract or reconstruct a layout-aware representation when headings, columns, tables, and captions require it.
4. Render selected pages for visual inspection.
5. Capture figure and table captions with page references.
6. Identify equations that were lost, reordered, or garbled.
7. Flag pages whose extracted text is suspiciously short relative to rendered content.

`pdftotext` and `pdfinfo` are available in the current environment and can serve as the first deterministic layer. OCR tooling is not currently available, so image-only documents require an explicit OCR installation or another approved extraction path during execution. The plan must not pretend OCR has already succeeded.

### 6.2 Page Fidelity

Every extracted unit must preserve a link to the original page. Page fidelity matters because the final evidence system must support statements such as:

`Paper P-C-2024-..., page 8, Section 3.2, Equation 12, Figure 5 caption`

Paragraph-level offsets are helpful, but page and section anchors are mandatory. If printed page numbers differ from PDF indices, store both.

### 6.3 Reading Formulas

Formula extraction is a high-risk area. For every central equation:

- Compare extracted text with the rendered page.
- Record the equation number if present.
- Define every variable and unit used by the paper.
- Capture domain, constraints, initial or boundary conditions, and parameter source.
- Distinguish the authors' base model from their modification.
- Note whether the formula is derived, cited, fitted, or simply asserted.
- Mark transcription uncertainty rather than silently correcting it.

Central equations should be normalized into readable LaTeX or structured notation only after visual verification.

### 6.4 Figures and Tables

For every analytically important figure or table, record:

- Page and caption.
- Visual form.
- Variables and encodings.
- Analytical question answered.
- Data or model stage represented.
- Whether uncertainty is shown.
- Whether comparison baselines are shown.
- Relationship to claims in surrounding text.
- Reproducibility clues, including software or parameters.
- Visual defects or ambiguities.

A decorative figure is not treated as analytical evidence. A visually simple chart may still be studied as a negative pattern or as evidence that purpose matters more than superficial complexity.

### 6.5 Extraction Quality Score

Score each paper on:

- Text completeness.
- Heading fidelity.
- Reading-order fidelity.
- Equation fidelity.
- Table fidelity.
- Caption fidelity.
- Page-anchor fidelity.

No paper proceeds to deep reading with a failing text or page-anchor score. Equation, table, or caption failures can proceed only with a mandatory manual-render review flag.

## 7. Phase 2 - Pilot Calibration

### 7.1 Purpose of the Pilot

The pilot is not a shortcut and does not replace sequential study. It is a controlled calibration used to discover defects in the analysis schema before hundreds of paper cards are created.

### 7.2 Pilot Selection

Select at least twelve pilot papers using path metadata only:

- Two from each A-F type.
- One older and one newer paper in each type where the available years permit.
- A mixture of text-rich and visually dense PDFs.
- At least one paper with difficult equations or tables.

The pilot papers retain their true positions in the full queue. Their finalized paper cards are either revalidated under the frozen protocol or redone; they are not counted as complete merely because they were used for experimentation.

### 7.3 Calibration Questions

Use the pilot to answer:

- Does the section taxonomy cover real paper structures without forcing false headings?
- Can every important claim be tied to a page anchor?
- Are base models and novel modifications reliably separated?
- Is the language schema granular enough to distinguish chapter functions?
- Can visualizations be described by analytical purpose rather than appearance alone?
- Are assumptions connected to consequences and validation?
- Can a reviewer reconstruct the full solution logic from the paper card?
- Are independent deductions clearly separated from source observations?
- How much manual review is required for equations, tables, and multi-column layouts?
- Is the per-paper update process small enough to be reliable but rich enough to satisfy the prompt?

### 7.4 Freeze Protocol Version 1

After the pilot:

1. Revise schemas and controlled vocabularies.
2. Define mandatory and optional fields.
3. Add examples of correct and incorrect records.
4. Version the protocol.
5. Validate or redo all pilot artifacts.
6. Begin the full queue only after the schema passes completeness tests.

Later schema changes are allowed, but they must include a migration procedure for earlier paper cards.

## 8. The Authoritative Single-Paper Learning Transaction

This section supersedes every earlier model-first or weakness-first paper workflow. The sequence is mandatory. A gate may loop internally until complete, but a later gate may not be used to excuse an incomplete earlier gate.

### 8.1 State Flow and Stop Rules

```text
SELECT ONE PAPER
      |
      v
G0 source identity, task context, page integrity, visual chapter map
      |
      v
G1 Summary/Abstract + Introduction language and paragraph chains
      |
      v
G2 every substantive chapter language saturation
      |
      v
G3 repeated-pattern abstraction + imitate-without-copying tests
      |
      v
G4 global organization + section contracts + paragraph/argument chains
      |
      v
G5 formula-level models, algorithms, assumptions, data, validation, handoffs
      |
      v
G6 important visualization retention
      |
      v
G7 last-pass formula/fact/notation warning only
      |
      v
G8 paper card + type-branch incorporation + four validators + full tests
      |
      v
FREEZE BYTES -> HASH -> VALIDATE DRAFT -> APPEND -> REBUILD COMPLIANCE
      |
      +-- fail anywhere: stay on this paper, repair, and rerun
      |
      +-- pass all: select the next deterministic paper
```

Hard stop rules:

- Do not start G5 because equations look easier to extract than language.
- Do not open G7 while any language chapter, paragraph chain, section contract, or main argument chain is incomplete.
- Do not use a weakness record to fill a language, organization, model-retention, or visualization field.
- Do not select the next paper while any validator fails, any current paper artifact remains draft, the compliance report names migration debt, or the current transaction is not durably appended.
- Do not mutate a frozen artifact after hashing. If bytes change after append, create and validate a new corrective transaction.

### 8.2 Gate G0 - Source Identity, Task Context, and Visual Chapter Map

First verify the paper as an object:

- Resolve the immutable paper ID, problem type from path or filename, year, source path, source SHA-256, eligibility, and page count from the manifest.
- Read the corresponding official problem statement when available, but never use problem content to reclassify A-F.
- Record explicit tasks, stakeholders, required outputs, data, constraints, time and spatial scales, ambiguity, and the required communication artifact.
- Inspect every rendered page or a complete set of readable contact sheets.
- Reconcile title, Summary/Abstract, contents, printed headings, appendices, references, memo or letter, and any AI Use Report.
- Build a source-heading map and a normalized chapter-function map. Preserve both; normalization must never erase the paper's printed organization.
- Mark blank, image-only, OCR-corrupt, duplicate, watermarked, missing, or ambiguous pages.
- Locate equations, tables, figures, algorithms, results, validation, and final recommendations only to establish boundaries. Do not begin the deep model or error pass here.

G0 output:

```text
paper identity
+ official-task map
+ complete page-review record
+ source heading -> normalized chapter mapping
+ page ranges for every substantive chapter
+ explicit non-author-language exclusions
```

AI Use Reports, generated disclosure prose, table-of-contents entries, publisher watermarks, copied prompt text, third-party chart text, and bibliography entries are not positive author-language evidence. A references chapter may contribute only citation-framing language actually written by the team.

G0 passes only when every physical page is accounted for and every substantive source section has a normalized destination or an explicit non-substantive exclusion reason.

### 8.3 Gate G1 - Summary/Abstract and Introduction First

The first deep read is language, beginning with Summary/Abstract and Introduction.

For the Summary/Abstract:

- Reconstruct every visual paragraph in order.
- Mark the dominant move of each paragraph: stakes, scope, task routing, method, result, validation, recommendation, limitation, or contribution.
- Record how numbered tasks are introduced and whether they use repeated blocks such as `For Problem n -> method -> result`.
- Record exact quantitative payload: how many numbers, which numbers are selected, and where results replace generic claims.
- Record opening, task-transition, contrast, qualification, result, and closing routines.
- Measure word count, sentence count, paragraph count, sentence-length distribution, rhetorical proportions, and counts of equations, figures, tables, citations, and quantitative results.

For the Introduction:

- Reconstruct every visual paragraph in order.
- Separate background, evidence, unresolved need, task restatement, scope, contribution, and work-plan moves.
- Capture how the authors move from real-world stakes to a solvable model.
- Record the order and granularity of task bullets or stage descriptions.
- Record how the work-plan paragraph previews later chapters and model handoffs.
- Measure the same quantitative layout fields as the Summary/Abstract.

Each visual paragraph must link to at least one positive language unit from the same chapter. A paragraph chain is incomplete if it merely lists topics without the dominant rhetorical move or if it points to a weakness instead of language evidence.

### 8.4 Gate G2 - Chapter-by-Chapter Positive Language Saturation

Continue through every substantive normalized chapter. Typical chapters include:

- Problem restatement and problem analysis.
- Assumptions and justification.
- Notation and nomenclature.
- Data source, cleaning, preprocessing, and feature construction.
- Model establishment, model improvement, and algorithm.
- Results, interpretation, verification, robustness, sensitivity, and error analysis.
- Strengths, limitations, future improvements, conclusion, memo, or stakeholder letter.
- Citation framing in references, when it is team-authored and analytically meaningful.

For each chapter, perform at least two positive-language passes:

1. Pass 1 identifies all materially distinct rhetorical moves.
2. Pass 2 searches specifically for missed variants, transitions, repeated routines, quantitative language, qualifications, and boundary statements.
3. If Pass 2 finds a new reusable move, perform another pass.
4. Saturation is reached only when the final pass finds zero new reusable moves.
5. Record pass count, new moves per pass, open gaps, and the final coverage decision.

A chapter is not saturated because one attractive sentence was captured. It is saturated when every materially distinct reusable rhetorical job has a disposition.

Each language unit must include:

- Paper ID, A-F type, page, printed section, normalized chapter, and granularity.
- Short source excerpt.
- Rhetorical function and move.
- Information order.
- Voice, tense, modality, clause structure, transition behavior, and quantitative role.
- Fillable reusable abstraction.
- Replaceable elements.
- Prohibited generalizations and transfer boundaries.
- Candidate pattern IDs.
- Final disposition.

Positive reusable language units must remain more numerous than weakness records for the paper. A higher standard is preferred: language should be the largest intellectual dimension while formulas and visuals remain fully retained.

Anti-padding and absence rules:

- Merge exact duplicates that perform the same move in the same way, but preserve all occurrence locations on the surviving unit.
- Keep a separate variant only when lexical choice, syntax, voice, information order, quantitative payload, chapter position, or transfer boundary changes materially.
- Do not split one sentence into several units merely to raise the language count; split only when it performs independently reusable rhetorical moves.
- A short or sparse chapter still requires a recorded coverage decision. If it contains no reusable language move, record the inspected pages, the reason, and the paper-specific nonreusable disposition rather than inventing a template.
- A chapter with many repeated sentences is not complete after one sample. Capture the stable frame once, attach all occurrences, then record meaningful variants and nonexamples.
- Language coverage is judged by material rhetorical jobs and saturation evidence, not by raw quotation volume.

### 8.5 Gate G3 - High-Frequency and Patterned Expression Mining

Do not discard an expression because it is repeated or formulaic. Repetition is often the evidence that a competition-writing routine is stable.

For every repeated or patterned routine, record:

| Field | Required content |
|---|---|
| Occurrences | Every source page and paragraph or section location in the paper. |
| Cross-paper support | Independent paper IDs and count when the pattern recurs elsewhere. |
| Stable function | What rhetorical job remains constant. |
| Information order | The ordered semantic moves, not only the connector words. |
| Slots | Replaceable semantic fields such as task, model, metric, number, comparator, result, actor, action, or caveat. |
| Variants | Changes in ordinal, connector, voice, tense, clause order, evidence payload, and chapter placement. |
| Boundary | When the routine is appropriate and when it would mislead or become empty. |
| Disposition | Supports existing, opens candidate, or paper-specific nonreusable with a substantive reason. |

The current schema-v3 compatibility representation is explicit: store labeled `occurrences:`, `stable function:`, `order:`, `slots:`, and `variants:` entries in `reusable_elements`, and store the labeled `boundary:` plus anti-copy prohibition in `prohibited_generalizations`. Do not hide these fields in unlabeled prose. A future schema may promote them to first-class properties only with a versioned migration that revalidates every affected historical paper before queue expansion resumes.

Priority examples include, but are not limited to:

- `For Problem n -> data/method -> result -> implication`.
- `Firstly/Secondly/Finally -> stage -> output -> handoff`.
- `Step n -> operation -> intermediate -> next step`.
- `Where [symbol] represents [object]`.
- `As shown in Figure/Table -> pattern -> number -> meaning`.
- `Before/after -> encoding -> decision consequence`.
- `Indicator n -> definition -> direction -> formula`.
- `For [actor] -> action -> modeled reason -> condition`.
- `However -> limitation or exception -> bounded conclusion`.

A repeated connector without stable semantic work is not a reusable pattern. A pattern with stable work but different surface wording is valuable even when exact phrase frequency is low.

Use this harvesting sequence for every substantive chapter:

1. **Surface scan:** locate repeated strings, ordinal openings, section openers, equation-introduction frames, figure/table references, qualifiers, and closing moves.
2. **Syntactic scan:** locate recurring grammatical frames even when vocabulary changes, such as passive definition, conditional recommendation, comparison-result, or limitation-concession structures.
3. **Semantic scan:** locate the same information-order routine expressed with different words, such as `task -> method -> result -> implication`.
4. **Occurrence reconciliation:** attach every confirmed occurrence and reject false matches that share words but perform different jobs.
5. **Variant analysis:** separate stable core from lexical, grammatical, order, voice, tense, and payload variants.
6. **Abstraction:** replace paper-specific content with typed slots while preserving function and useful order.
7. **Boundary and anti-copy test:** name when the routine fails, produce two unrelated original examples, and reject source-clause copying.
8. **Disposition:** link to an existing pattern, open a candidate, or record a substantive paper-specific nonreusable reason.

The executor must search all three repetition levels. Exact-string frequency alone misses rhetorical routines; semantic similarity alone can hallucinate a pattern without occurrence evidence.

### 8.6 Imitate Without Copying Acceptance Test

Every promoted or candidate template must pass all of the following:

1. **Function preservation:** the abstraction performs the same rhetorical job as the source.
2. **Order preservation:** the semantic information appears in the same useful order unless a documented variant changes it.
3. **Slot completeness:** every paper-specific element has a typed replaceable slot.
4. **Two original instantiations:** create two new examples using different scenarios and vocabulary; neither may be a paraphrase of the source case.
5. **Boundary test:** state at least one appropriate and one forbidden use.
6. **No source-clause copy:** do not reproduce a full source clause, distinctive sentence frame with its original entities, or a source-unique run of eight or more consecutive content words.
7. **Traceability:** keep only the short source excerpt needed to prove the pattern.
8. **Semantic honesty:** do not turn correlation into causation, possibility into certainty, or a paper-specific result into a general fact.

The accepted reusable object is the rhetorical mechanism, not the historical sentence.

### 8.7 Gate G4 - Reconstruct the Authors' Observable Article Organization

Only after language saturation reconstruct the paper's organization at four levels.

#### Global organization chain

Record the paper's actual path, for example:

```text
context -> task decomposition -> assumptions/data -> baseline
-> extensions -> results -> checks -> decision -> limitations -> audience artifact
```

Do not force every paper into one universal chain. Preserve unusual but functional orders.

#### Section contract

Create one contract for every substantive section:

- Printed heading and normalized chapter.
- Source pages.
- Input inherited from prior sections.
- Question or task answered.
- Rhetorical job.
- Mathematical or analytical job.
- Output produced.
- Handoff target.
- Transition cue that makes the handoff legible.
- Positive reusable organizational pattern.
- Any bounded discontinuity, recorded only after the positive contract exists.

Under the current schemas, place the source-anchored narrative contract in the paper card's positive `writing_logic` record and place the normalized move sequence plus positive pattern in the audit's `section_progressions`. The `moves` array must explicitly encode `input`, `rhetorical job`, `analytical job`, `output`, and `handoff`; a topic list is not sufficient. Promoting these fields to a new audit schema requires a full migration and empty-debt gate.

#### Paragraph progression

For every Summary/Abstract and Introduction paragraph, and for important paragraphs elsewhere, record:

`dominant move -> claim -> support -> qualifier -> consequence -> next-link`

This reveals how readable argument flow is constructed. It is not an invitation to infer private intention.

The current audit schema stores the dominant move and linked language-unit IDs; the linked language units and the paper-card writing-logic record must together preserve claim, support, qualifier, consequence, and next-link. If any element cannot be resolved through those links, the paragraph chain remains incomplete.

#### Main argument chain

For each main deliverable, trace:

```text
official requirement
-> paper subproblem
-> data/assumption
-> method or model
-> intermediate output
-> result
-> validation or sensitivity
-> caveat
-> final answer or stakeholder decision
```

Every decision claim must have source pages and an observable support path. Weakness records may annotate a chain after it is reconstructed; they cannot replace the chain.

#### Organization skeleton test

After the four positive organization artifacts are drafted, run a deliberate skeleton test:

1. Replace paper-specific entities, parameter values, factual claims, and model names with neutral placeholders.
2. Retain only headings, paragraph moves, logical relations, inputs, outputs, and handoffs.
3. Ask whether a future writer could still see why each section exists, what it receives, what it produces, and why the next section follows.
4. Verify that every official task reaches a final answer or stakeholder action through an unbroken positive path.
5. Verify that no step depends on a weakness label such as "incorrect," "unsupported," or "missing" to explain the intended organization.

If the skeleton collapses when facts and equations are removed, the record describes content rather than article organization and G4 remains open.

This skeleton test does not create an unversioned audit field. Under the current schema, its proof is the jointly resolvable content of `global_progression`, `section_progressions`, Summary/Introduction paragraph chains, paper-card positive `writing_logic`, and `main_argument_chains`, with the pass/fail conclusion recorded in the transaction validator attestation or review note. If a future schema promotes `organization_skeleton_test` to a first-class property, that schema change must rebuild migration debt and revalidate every affected closed paper before queue expansion.

### 8.8 Organization Acceptance Gate

G4 passes only when:

- A positive global organization chain exists.
- Every substantive chapter has a section contract or a documented reason it is non-substantive.
- Summary/Abstract and Introduction paragraph chains exactly match their visually reconciled paragraph counts.
- At least one main argument chain covers every required deliverable.
- Model-to-model and result-to-decision handoffs are explicit.
- The final conclusion, memo, or letter traces back to earlier evidence.
- Weakness substitution is explicitly false.

The question answered by this gate is: "Could a future writer reproduce how this paper organizes a complex solution, even if all paper-specific facts and equations were replaced?"

### 8.9 Gate G5 - Formula-Level Model, Data, Assumption, and Algorithm Retention

After the two writing gates pass, reconstruct every core analytical component. For each model unit, record:

- Family, purpose, base model or modification, and source pages.
- Inputs, outputs, state variables, decision variables, parameters, definitions, units, and source status.
- Governing equations in visually checked normalized LaTeX.
- Objective functions and constraints.
- Initial, boundary, equilibrium, event, or terminal conditions, including explicit absence.
- Data source, observation unit, coverage, cleaning, transformation, and leakage risk.
- Parameter estimation or calibration method.
- Solver, pseudocode, initialization, hyperparameters, randomness, seeds, convergence or stopping rule, and software.
- Assumptions required by the mechanism and the consequence of violating them.
- Identifiability, feasibility, numerical stability, and computational cost.
- Output handed to the next model or decision stage.
- Validation, sensitivity, and robustness references.
- Strength, limitation, and missing reproduction detail.

Separate conventional base form from paper modification:

| Base mechanism | Paper modification | Claimed benefit | Evidence of benefit | Interface | Transfer boundary |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Every displayed central equation must be visually checked against the rendered page. If an equation is unreadable or ambiguous, retain the ambiguity and lower transcription confidence. Never silently repair the source. A corrected version may be stored only as an independent extension.

Every model-to-model dependency edge must state the handed-off quantity, units or scale, transformation, and meaning. Model variety without a coherent interface is not a strength.

### 8.10 Gate G5 Verification and Result Translation

For each model, identify what the paper actually uses to check it:

- Unit, dimension, invariant, positivity, capacity, or conservation check.
- Boundary or limiting case.
- Calibration or fit.
- Holdout, cross-validation, baseline, or external comparator.
- Residual, uncertainty, sensitivity, robustness, scenario, ablation, or stability analysis.
- Solver convergence or repeated-run stability.
- Plausibility or stakeholder feasibility check.

Record what each check can establish and what it cannot. Then trace each major result through:

`model output -> visual/table -> interpretation -> requested answer -> stakeholder consequence -> caveat`

A high fit value is not automatically predictive validity. A sensitivity sweep is not automatically robustness. A network metric is not automatically a causal or policy outcome.

### 8.11 Gate G6 - Important Visualization Retention

For every analytically important visual, record:

- Source page, figure or table number, and caption.
- Analytical purpose.
- Source model or data.
- Chart or diagram form.
- Encodings, axes, units, scales, normalization, color, annotation, layout, and uncertainty.
- Exact claim the visual supports.
- Reproducible construction procedure or missing information.
- A-F applicability and forbidden scenarios.
- Accessibility and interpretability concerns.
- Whether the visual is an important positive record, a bounded alternative, or a diagnostic counterexample.

Organize visualization knowledge by purpose before form:

- Explain mechanism or workflow.
- Show spatial or temporal structure.
- Compare alternatives or counterfactuals.
- Reveal uncertainty or sensitivity.
- Diagnose fit, error, residual, or stability.
- Show network, flow, hierarchy, or model handoff.
- Communicate multi-objective tradeoffs or stakeholder decisions.

A simple chart may be the correct form. An advanced chart is promoted only when the data or model requires its additional dimensions.

### 8.12 Gate G7 - Last-Pass Formula, Fact, Notation, and Claim Warnings

This pass begins only after G1-G6 pass.

Before G7 opens, the executor may record source ambiguity needed for faithful transcription, but must not:

- run an open-ended fact hunt;
- expand a weakness ledger;
- use error density as a quality score;
- search for external contradictions merely to attack the paper;
- rewrite earlier positive artifacts around defects;
- count a warning as language, organization, model-retention, or visual evidence.

External verification is permitted in G7 only when it resolves a precise source claim, equation, datum, unit, or recommendation and the verification basis is recorded. The default question remains "what can the final skill safely learn?", not "how many things can be proved wrong?"

Its purpose is to protect the final skill from reproducing source defects. Consolidate warnings by failure mechanism:

- Equation/prose mismatch.
- Undefined variable, unit, denominator, direction, or sign.
- Conservation, positivity, feasibility, identifiability, or boundary failure.
- Unsupported parameter, data transfer, threshold, or causal claim.
- Validation that cannot establish the stated conclusion.
- Result arithmetic, table, or figure inconsistency.
- Recommendation that does not trace to modeled evidence.
- Page-limit, required-deliverable, or provenance issue.

Every warning must reference a known model and equation when formula-specific, or a precise page and claim when fact-specific. The audit mode is always `last-pass-warning-only`, and `completed_after_positive_passes` must be true.

Warnings remain a strict minority of the paper's intellectual product. During migration, add no new weakness observation unless it corrects source fidelity or resolves a blocking inconsistency.

### 8.13 Gate G8 - Paper Card and Immediate Type-Branch Incorporation

Complete the canonical paper card with:

- Identity, provenance, source quality, task reconstruction, and section map.
- Positive contribution map.
- Language units, chapter layouts, saturation results, and pattern dispositions.
- Positive global organization, section contracts, paragraph chains, and main arguments.
- Assumptions, data, models, algorithms, validation, results, and dependency edges.
- Important visual records.
- Bounded judge-relevance hypotheses.
- Existing weaknesses and last-pass warnings.
- Independent extensions labeled as extensions.
- Candidate rule impacts and unresolved questions.
- Exact canonical paths, versions, counts, and review statuses for language and model artifacts.

Immediately update only the corresponding A-F type branch:

1. Attach every language unit to an existing pattern, new candidate, or substantive nonreusable disposition.
2. Add or update organization patterns.
3. Add model mechanisms, interfaces, selection conditions, and transfer limits.
4. Add important visualization records.
5. Add last-pass diagnostics.
6. Record contradictions and minority practices.
7. Update support counts only for genuinely independent evidence.
8. Run cross-type leakage checks.

No batch-generated candidate becomes a type rule without this single-paper incorporation.

Before freezing, complete a dual-axis scorecard:

| Axis | Mandatory result |
|---|---|
| Gate order | G1-G3 completed before G4; G4 before G5-G6; G7 last. |
| Language breadth | Every substantive chapter has a coverage disposition and final zero-new-move saturation evidence. |
| Pattern depth | Repeated routines include occurrences, function, order, slots, variants, boundary, and anti-copy result. |
| Organization | Global chain, every section contract, required paragraph chains, main arguments, and skeleton test pass. |
| Model fidelity | Retained model and equation IDs exactly match the validated model record; handoffs are complete. |
| Visual fidelity | Dispositions exactly cover the paper card's important visuals. |
| Warning boundary | Warning pass is last, source-anchored, non-substitutive, and a strict minority of positive language. |
| Priority balance | Positive language exceeds weakness for the paper; the latest-five rolling audit remains strictly language-largest. |

No aggregate score is allowed. Every row is pass/fail, and one failed row keeps the current paper open.

The scorecard is a closure view over existing validated fields, not a parallel artifact format. Its rows must resolve to the language record, learning audit, model record, paper card, rolling drift report, and transaction attestations. Adding a new stored scorecard object later requires schema versioning, validator changes, and a derived migration-debt rebuild.

### 8.14 Validation, Freeze, Hash, and Transaction Protocol

Run, in order:

1. `validate_language_units.py`
2. `validate_model_units.py`
3. `validate_learning_audit.py`
4. `validate_paper_artifacts.py`
5. `python -m unittest discover -s tests -v`
6. Language-priority rolling audit when the transaction enters its configured window.

Run the validators from `mcm-skill-work` against the final staged bytes. Set one current paper ID and its manifest-derived type; do not discover paths ad hoc after validation begins:

2. Compute SHA-256 from the frozen bytes.
3. Build the transaction draft with before/after state, artifact hashes, counts, type-branch diff, validator attestations, and unresolved items. 8. Rerun the rolling audit against live history and verify the paper is `protocol-current-validated` with an empty migration set.
9. Verify the transaction history hash chain.
10. Verify every hash named by the final event corresponds to the current frozen artifact or is explicitly superseded by a later corrective event.

### 8.15 One-Paper, One-Pass Closure Contract

"One pass" means one complete gated transaction before moving to another paper, not one unreviewed model response.

A paper closes only when all are true:

- Source identity and all pages are checked.
- Every substantive chapter has positive language coverage and final zero-new-move saturation.
- Summary/Abstract and Introduction paragraph chains match visual layout.
- Repeated patterns have occurrences, function, order, slots, variants, and boundaries.
- Imitation templates pass two-original-example and anti-copy tests.
- Positive global, section, paragraph, and argument organization artifacts are complete.
- All core models, equations, algorithms, handoffs, and important visuals are retained.
- Formula/fact warnings are last-pass only.
- Positive reusable language exceeds weakness records.
- Four per-paper validators and the full regression suite pass.
- Frozen hashes, transaction append, derived compliance, and history-chain validation pass.
- No migration debt or correction-required state remains for the paper.

Failure loops remain inside the current paper. The next paper is never used as a reason to defer a missing field.

### 8.16 Parallel-Agent and Shared-State Rules Inside a Paper Cycle

Only one paper may be active in intellectual learning and artifact-writing work. Parallel work is permitted only for the current paper when tasks are independent, the source/chapter map is already fixed, and write boundaries are explicit; future-paper preparation may be mechanical or read-only but cannot create language, model, audit, card, observation, or type-branch evidence.

Safe examples:

- One designated writer edits the current paper while independent agents perform read-only source, priority, or recovery checks against frozen inputs.
- Mechanical extraction or contact-sheet preparation may run for a future paper, but those outputs do not count as learned evidence and the paper cannot enter G1 before the current transaction closes.
- One read-only agent audits language priority while another read-only agent audits transaction recovery.
- Independent agents review a frozen `plan.md` or frozen paper transaction.

Unsafe examples:

- Two agents edit the same paper artifact.
- Different agents write learning artifacts for different papers before the current paper completes G8.
- Any subagent appends `history.jsonl`, rebuilds the shared compliance report, or edits `plan.md` while another writer is active.
- A model agent and language agent independently redefine the same chapter map.
- A reviewer silently modifies frozen artifacts after the root agent has computed hashes.

The root or designated transaction owner serializes shared-state operations: final validation, byte freeze, hash calculation, transaction append, compliance rebuild, and plan integration. Provider failure, timeout, or agent unavailability must degrade to root execution without weakening gates.
## 9. Evidence and Rule Maturation System

### 9.1 Atomic Observation Schema

An observation should be small enough to support one meaningful claim. Store:

- Observation ID.
- Paper ID.
- Problem type.
- Year.
- Source page and section.
- Evidence class.
- Short source excerpt or structural evidence.
- Faithful interpretation.
- Dimension: language, modeling, logic, visualization, verification, data, or other.
- Applicability conditions.
- Counterevidence.
- Confidence.
- Candidate rule links.

### 9.2 Rule Lifecycle

Rules progress through:

1. `seed`: observed in one paper.
2. `candidate`: clear enough to test against more papers.
3. `corroborated`: supported independently within the same type.
4. `bounded`: conditions, exceptions, and counterexamples are known.
5. `operationalized`: procedure, examples, prohibitions, and tests exist.
6. `promoted`: included in the skill's active references.
7. `challenged`: later evidence weakens or conflicts with it.
8. `revised`: scope or wording changed.
9. `retired`: retained historically but no longer recommended.

Promotion is not based on raw frequency. A rare but essential correctness rule can be promoted with strong direct reasoning. A frequent cosmetic habit may remain descriptive.

### 9.3 Corroboration Thresholds

Use flexible but explicit thresholds:

- One paper can establish a case pattern, never a universal pattern.
- Two independent papers can establish a provisional recurring pattern.
- Three or more papers across different years provide stronger type-level support.
- A direct official guide may justify a rule even when paper frequency is low.
- A mathematical correctness requirement can be promoted from first principles, but it must be labeled as an extension or general standard rather than a corpus induction.

Every promoted rule states which path led to promotion.

### 9.4 Contradiction Handling

When papers disagree:

1. Verify that the observations refer to comparable tasks and stages.
2. Check extraction and interpretation errors.
3. Identify differing assumptions, data regimes, objectives, or deliverables.
4. Split an overly broad rule into conditional branches when appropriate.
5. Preserve genuine alternatives if neither dominates.
6. Record the contradiction rather than choosing the newer or more complex approach automatically.

The final skill should often produce decision tables rather than a single command.

### 9.5 Confidence Language

Use controlled labels:

- `high`: directly supported, repeatedly corroborated, clearly scoped, and uncontradicted.
- `medium`: plausible and supported but limited by sample, transfer conditions, or exceptions.
- `low`: useful hypothesis or extension requiring user judgment and testing.

Avoid numerical confidence values unless a reproducible calibration method is introduced.

## 10. First Major Branch - High-Award Language Style

Sections 10-13 retain the controlling prompt's branch numbering for requirements traceability. They do **not** define paper-learning execution order or final dispatcher weight. The authoritative order remains language -> organization -> model/visual retention -> warning pass. In particular, Section 12 is executed before Section 11 during ordinary paper learning even though the original prompt numbered modeling as the second major branch.

### 10.1 Branch Architecture

Create separate A-F language references. Within each type, organize evidence by chapter function rather than by a single undifferentiated phrase list:

- Title and summary sheet or abstract.
- Introduction and context.
- Problem restatement.
- Problem analysis and decomposition.
- Assumptions and justification.
- Notation and symbol definition.
- Data and preprocessing.
- Model establishment.
- Model improvement.
- Algorithm and solution process.
- Results and interpretation.
- Sensitivity analysis.
- Robustness verification.
- Error analysis.
- Strengths.
- Limitations.
- Future improvement directions.
- Recommendations or memoranda.
- Conclusion.
- References and citation language.
- Appendix and reproducibility notes.

When a source paper combines these functions under one heading, preserve the source heading but tag each distinct rhetorical move separately. When it provides separate sections, do not collapse them into one language category merely for storage convenience.

### 10.2 Language Mining Dimensions

For each chapter and type, examine:

- Sentence purpose.
- Typical information order.
- Voice and agency.
- Tense and modality.
- Clause structure.
- Quantitative specificity.
- Transition mechanics.
- Hedging and confidence.
- Terminology consistency.
- Paragraph length and density.
- Equation-to-prose integration.
- Figure/table references.
- Citation integration.
- Avoidance of unsupported superlatives.

### 10.2.1 Quantitative Chapter-Layout Record

For every available chapter, and mandatorily for Abstract/Summary and Introduction, record a reproducible layout profile rather than only a qualitative note:

- Page span and fraction of the technical paper.
- Word, sentence, and paragraph counts.
- Median and range of sentence length.
- Paragraph function sequence, with each paragraph assigned one dominant rhetorical move.
- Proportion devoted to background, task definition, prior work, research gap, method roadmap, results, validation, limitations, and decision meaning, using only categories present in that chapter.
- Number and placement of equations, figures, tables, citations, and quantitative results referenced in prose.
- Opening, transition, and closing move types.
- Extraction method, counting rule, and any ambiguity caused by columns, bullets, captions, or OCR.

Aggregate these records within one problem type by era and chapter. Report sample size, minimum, maximum, median, and interquartile range before recommending any quantitative convention. Observed ranges are evidence, not mandatory quotas; rhetorical completeness takes precedence over forcing a paper to match an average count.

For the language-priority study phase, every completed paper must contribute positive reusable language evidence for every available chapter. Coverage is not satisfied by one token example when the chapter contains multiple material rhetorical moves: record each distinct move until an additional pass yields no new reusable move, and store the pass result as `coverage-complete` or explain the unresolved gap. Abstract/Summary and Introduction must additionally receive paragraph-chain reconstruction. Negative diagnostics remain a minority and cannot substitute for positive imitation patterns.

### 10.3 Template Construction

Every reusable template must include:

- Function.
- Type label.
- Chapter label.
- Within-paper occurrence list and cross-paper support count where applicable.
- Stable information order.
- Required information slots.
- Optional slots.
- Observed lexical, grammatical, and order variants.
- At least two original variants where variation is useful.
- Two filled examples from different original scenarios that do not copy or paraphrase a source paper.
- Conditions where the template sounds forced or misleading.
- Common low-quality alternatives.
- Source-pattern references.
- Anti-copy result for full clauses, source-unique continuous wording, and source-specific facts.

For example, an assumption-justification template must force the writer to name the simplification, mechanism, evidence, and consequence. It should not merely produce polished prose around a weak assumption.

### 10.4 Positive and Negative Lexicons

The positive lexicon should be semantic, not ornamental. Group terms by actions such as:

- Define.
- Estimate.
- Calibrate.
- Simulate.
- Optimize.
- Validate.
- Compare.
- Quantify.
- Infer.
- Bound.
- Recommend.

The negative lexicon should explain why alternatives fail: vagueness, informality, false certainty, redundancy, undefined referent, inflated novelty, or ambiguous quantity.

### 10.5 Quantitative Style Rules

Induce rules for:

- Reporting central results in abstracts.
- Choosing significant digits.
- Stating units.
- Naming baselines.
- Describing percentage versus percentage-point change.
- Expressing uncertainty.
- Avoiding unsupported precision.
- Connecting values to decisions.

These rules should be verified against actual paper practice and mathematical correctness.

### 10.6 Language Branch Acceptance Gate

A type's language branch is acceptable when it can:

- Draft an original section from structured facts.
- Rewrite weak prose without changing the mathematical meaning.
- Diagnose vague or inflated sentences.
- Preserve terminology across a complete paper.
- Report evidence-backed chapter length, sentence, paragraph, and rhetorical-proportion ranges with sample size and era boundaries.
- Retrieve all retained positive, minority, and contradictory language practices from the final non-default evidence layer.
- Select different wording for assumptions, methods, results, and limitations.
- Explain why a template applies rather than merely inserting words.
- Reproduce the function and information order of high-frequency routines without reproducing source wording.
- Show occurrence, slots, variants, boundaries, and independent-paper support for every recurring pattern.
- Reject a fluent template that copies a source clause, lacks two original instantiations, or has no forbidden-use boundary.

## 11. Second Major Branch - Innovative Mathematical Modeling

### 11.1 Type-Specific Modeling Maps

The prompt provides initial characterizations of A-F, such as continuous physical modeling, discrete optimization, data analysis, system simulation, environmental evaluation, and social decision-making. Treat these as starting hypotheses. The actual model taxonomy for each type must emerge from the path-classified paper evidence and may include multiple subfamilies or changes over time.

Maintain a separate A-F scoring-focus map. Each proposed focus must name the paper feature, task requirement or official judging source, evidence level, counterexamples, applicable era, and `judge_relevance` status. Award status alone supports correlation, not a causal claim about scoring. A type branch cannot be declared complete without this evidence-graded map.

### 11.2 Model Knowledge Unit

Each reusable model entry includes:

- Decision question it answers.
- Required data.
- Mathematical formulation.
- Assumptions.
- Parameter estimation.
- Algorithm.
- Expected outputs.
- Verification plan.
- Computational burden.
- Interpretability.
- Failure modes.
- Suitable and unsuitable scenarios.
- Type and chapter placement.
- Evidence papers.
- Possible improvements.

### 11.3 Model Selection Decision Trees

Build decision trees from problem properties rather than team fashion. Candidate decision variables include:

- Continuous versus discrete state.
- Deterministic versus stochastic dynamics.
- Prediction versus explanation versus optimization.
- Static versus temporal decision.
- Spatial dependence.
- Network structure.
- Multiple objectives.
- Sparse or noisy data.
- Policy or stakeholder constraints.
- Need for interpretability.
- Availability of ground truth.
- Computational budget.

Each leaf recommends a family, required checks, and fallback options rather than a single guaranteed model.

### 11.4 Hybrid Model Interfaces

For hybrid systems, explicitly document:

- Why each component is needed.
- What representation passes between components.
- Whether units and scales match.
- Error propagation across interfaces.
- Joint versus separate calibration.
- Risk of double-counting variables.
- Whether the hybrid is identifiable.
- Whether a simpler baseline could achieve the same goal.

This prevents model stacking for appearance alone.

### 11.5 Innovative Assumption Design

Create type-specific assumption workflows:

1. Identify the mechanism that must be preserved.
2. List variables and interactions that could be removed.
3. Predict the bias caused by each removal.
4. Seek evidence or domain justification.
5. Translate the assumption into mathematical consequences.
6. Design a sensitivity or scenario test for violation.
7. State the limitation in decision-relevant terms.

### 11.6 Verification by Model Family

Map model families to appropriate checks. Examples of the mapping logic include:

- Differential or dynamical models: dimensions, conservation, boundary behavior, equilibrium, stability, numerical convergence.
- Optimization: feasibility, baseline comparison, optimality gap, parameter sensitivity, scenario robustness.
- Statistical prediction: leakage control, holdout evaluation, residuals, calibration, uncertainty, subgroup behavior.
- Simulation: initialization, stochastic replication, convergence, validation against observations, scenario coverage.
- Network models: topology sensitivity, connectivity assumptions, centrality interpretation, edge uncertainty.
- Multi-criteria decision models: weight sensitivity, normalization dependence, rank reversal, stakeholder interpretation.

The final references will be evidence-populated and A-F scoped.

### 11.7 Modeling Branch Acceptance Gate

The branch passes when a new problem can be converted into:

- A task decomposition.
- A justified shortlist of model families.
- A complete formulation plan.
- An algorithm plan.
- A validation plan.
- A simpler baseline.
- Failure criteria.
- A writing and visualization plan tied to the model.

## 12. Third Major Branch - Integrated Paper Logic

### 12.1 Traceability Backbone

Teach organization before defect diagnosis. The branch must preserve both the article-construction chain and the evidence chain.

Article-construction chain:

`source section input -> rhetorical job -> analytical job -> output -> transition -> next-section input`

Evidence-to-decision chain:

`problem requirement -> assumption -> variable -> model component -> computation -> verification -> result -> interpretation -> recommendation -> limitation`

Every important conclusion in a generated or reviewed paper should be traceable backward through this chain.

This branch describes observable article structure. It must not infer private intention, motivation, or psychology. A fact error can annotate a broken link only after the positive chain has been reconstructed.

### 12.2 Global Structure Patterns

For each A-F type, induce:

- How abstracts allocate attention across subproblems.
- How introductions frame the practical stakes.
- How problem analysis predicts the later model structure.
- Where assumptions and notation are most effective.
- How multiple models are sequenced.
- Where verification is placed.
- How results are integrated across subproblems.
- How limitations and recommendations close the loop.

Do not force one table of contents on all papers. Store alternative structures with selection conditions.

### 12.3 Section-Level Logic Templates

Each template will be a sequence of reasoning moves. For example, a model section may follow:

`objective -> mechanism -> variables -> assumptions -> formulation -> parameterization -> solver -> output -> validation hook`

A results section may follow:

`question -> metric -> baseline -> result -> uncertainty -> mechanism -> practical implication -> limitation`

The precise sequence must be tuned by problem type and evidence.

### 12.4 Transition System

Transitions are modeled as logical relations:

- Prerequisite.
- Consequence.
- Refinement.
- Contrast.
- Exception.
- Scale change.
- Model handoff.
- Validation response.
- Decision translation.

Language templates are then attached to these relations. This keeps transitions meaningful instead of decorative.

### 12.5 Redundancy and Gap Diagnostics

Develop checks for:

- Repeated problem restatement.
- Repeated model explanation.
- Duplicate conclusions.
- Unused variables or figures.
- Missing definitions.
- Missing validation links.
- Missing answer to a requested subproblem.
- Contradictory assumptions.
- Inconsistent units or terminology.
- Conclusions that introduce new analysis.

### 12.6 Logic Branch Acceptance Gate

Given a draft or outline, the skill must be able to produce:

- A positive global organization chain.
- An input/job/output/handoff contract for every substantive section.
- Summary/Abstract and Introduction paragraph-progression maps.
- A dependency graph.
- A missing-link report.
- A reordered outline.
- A claim-to-evidence matrix.
- A redundancy report.
- A revised section logic plan specific to A-F.
- A proof that no weakness or formula-warning record was used as a substitute for positive organization evidence.

- Plagiaristic reuse of source sentences.

## 15. Within-Type Consolidation Cycles

### 15.1 Cadence

After every 10 `protocol-current-validated` papers in a type, or at the end of a year if fewer than 10 remain, pause for a consolidation cycle. This pause does not batch-read papers; it reviews already completed evidence. Historically `closed` papers that are migration debt do not count toward the cadence until revalidated.

### 15.2 Consolidation Tasks

1. Validate that every paper transaction updated the correct type branch.
2. Merge only truly duplicate observations while preserving all source links.
3. Reassess candidate rule support.
4. Search for counterexamples.
5. Split rules with hidden conditions.
6. Update model and visualization taxonomies.
7. Refresh chapter language patterns.
8. Review unresolved contradictions.
9. Run cross-type leakage checks.
10. Regenerate progress and coverage reports.

### 15.3 Temporal Analysis

Within each type, examine how practices change over years:

- Model families.
- Computational sophistication.
- Data availability.
- Validation strength.
- Visual conventions.
- Writing structure.
- Reproducibility.

Do not assume newer is better. Record persistence, emergence, decline, and isolated experiments.

### 15.4 Type Completion Review

After the last paper in a type:

- Audit every eligible paper.
- Resolve all blocking extraction issues.
- Review all promoted rules against counterexamples.
- Test all templates and procedures.
- Ensure every type-specific reference is directly reachable from `SKILL.md`.
- Freeze a versioned type knowledge snapshot.

Only then move the main study focus to the next type, although tooling fixes may be shared.

## 16. Cross-Type Synthesis Without Cross-Type Confusion

### 16.1 Allowed Synthesis

After all six type streams are mature, compare them to build a dispatcher and explicit mapping tables. Allowed outputs include:

- The same rhetorical function implemented differently in A-F.
- Model-family applicability by type.
- Verification differences by type.
- Visualization-purpose differences by type.
- Shared correctness requirements with six independent evidence columns.

### 16.2 Forbidden Synthesis

Do not:

- Pool counts across A-F and call the result universal.
- Strip type labels from examples.
- Use a strong C pattern as a default for A without A evidence or an explicitly labeled extension.
- Merge conflicting type procedures into vague advice.
- Create a generic template that erases different reasoning chains.

### 16.3 Shared Rule Admission

A rule may enter a shared core only if:

1. It is a mathematical, academic, or reproducibility invariant independent of type; or
2. It has independent support in every type to which it claims applicability.

The shared record retains a support matrix by type. Absence of support is shown explicitly.

- The main sequence for solve, write, review, and visualize modes.

The installed skill should include a compact source index that maps internal evidence IDs to paper IDs and source paths without embedding the entire corpus. It should not redistribute the full PDFs unless that is explicitly intended and permitted. Source excerpts should be minimal and purpose-limited; most reusable language should be newly abstracted.

## 18. Skill Construction Sequence

### 18.1 Scaffold Only After Requirements Stabilize

The skill creation workflow will follow the skill-creator requirements:

1. Confirm concrete usage examples.
2. Plan reusable scripts, references, and assets.
3. Ask for or confirm installation location when actual creation begins.
4. Initialize with the official `init_skill.py` script.
5. Implement resources and write `SKILL.md` in imperative form.
6. Generate accurate `agents/openai.yaml` metadata after reading the finished skill.
7. Run `quick_validate.py`.
8. Forward-test on realistic tasks.
9. Revise and repeat.

The current task is planning; it does not assume the final installation path or prematurely scaffold the skill.

### 18.2 Incremental Builds

Do not wait until all papers are read to test the representation. Build internal alpha versions:

- Alpha 0: dispatcher plus evidence method and pilot material.
- Alpha A: complete Problem A branch.
- Alpha B: complete A and B branches.
- Continue through Alpha F.
- Beta: all types plus cross-type dispatcher and full validation suite.
- Release candidate: all evidence audits, forward tests, and link checks pass.

Alpha versions are research instruments, not final delivery. Their failures should improve schemas and workflows.

### 18.3 Change Control

Every skill update should record:

- Paper or evidence that triggered it.
- Files changed.
- Rules added, revised, challenged, or retired.
- Tests added or updated.
- Migration required for existing records.
- Validation result.

This makes the sequential accumulation requirement visible and reversible.

### 18.4 Final Skill Priority Architecture

The final huge skill must expose all four major branches without flattening their weight:

```text
SKILL.md dispatcher
  |
  +-- default paper-writing route
  |     1. language style and chapter routines
  |     2. article organization and argument handoffs
  |     3. model/equation and visualization integration
  |     4. final formula/fact warning review
  |
  +-- explicit modeling-only request route
  |     answer from already validated formula-level evidence without starting
  |     or reordering a paper-learning transaction
  |     if paper learning, writing, or review is involved, preserve
  |     language -> organization -> model/visual -> warning order
  |     retain equations, algorithms, handoffs, and visuals without omission
  |
  +-- explicit review route
        review language and organization positively before opening defect diagnostics
```

Reference loading order must follow the same priority for every paper-learning, writing, or review transaction. An exclusively mathematical request may query already validated model evidence directly, but it may not start or reorder a paper-learning transaction. Even in modeling-only response mode, the model record must retain its downstream writing and visual interfaces. In review mode, the skill must not return only an error list when the user asks to learn from or imitate a strong paper.

Behavioral tests must fail a skill build that:

- Routes ordinary paper-writing work directly to models before language.
- Treats language as a final polish layer.
- Defines author logic mainly as correctness checking.
- Drops model equations or visuals in order to inflate language priority.
- Produces a weakness-heavy paper review with no positive organization reconstruction.

## 19. Validation Strategy

### 19.1 Structural Validation

Validate:

- YAML frontmatter and naming rules.
- No unapproved frontmatter fields.
- Reference link integrity.
- Script execution.
- Schema validity.
- Required A-F tags.
- Table-of-contents presence in long references.
- No placeholder or redundant files.
- `SKILL.md` length and navigability.

### 19.2 Evidence Validation

Automated checks should flag:

- A promoted rule with no evidence.
- Evidence without a source paper.
- Missing page anchors.
- Type mismatch between paper and rule.
- A shared rule without a type support matrix.
- Judge-preference language without a basis.
- Extensions attributed to source papers.
- Orphan paper cards not incorporated into a type branch.

Manual audits should sample evidence back to rendered source pages.

### 19.3 Coverage Validation

Maintain coverage matrices:

- Papers by type and year.
- Chapters by type.
- Model families by type.
- Validation methods by type.
- Visualization purposes by type.
- Language rhetorical moves by type and chapter.
- Failure modes by type.

A blank matrix cell is not automatically a defect, but it must be investigated and explained.

### 19.4 Cross-Type Leakage Tests

Construct tests that:

- Query only Problem A and verify no unmarked C advice appears.
- Ask for a D workflow and check all loaded references are D or explicitly shared.
- Present identical keywords under two explicit types and ensure routing follows the given type, not topic stereotypes.
- Check every template and rule retains its type scope.

### 19.5 Contradiction Tests

For known conflicting practices, verify the skill:

- Exposes the differing conditions.
- Does not silently select a rule.
- Recommends a diagnostic question or comparison.
- Preserves evidence for both alternatives.

### 19.6 Reproducibility Tests

Select representative model entries and ask an independent user or agent to reconstruct:

- Inputs.
- Equations.
- Algorithm.
- Parameterization.
- Validation.
- Output visual.

Missing steps indicate that the entry is descriptive rather than actionable.

### 19.7 Language-Priority and Imitation Tests

The test suite must make priority drift observable rather than relying on prose promises.

Test every paper transaction for:

- Exact audit priority markers: `language-imitation`, `organization-logic`, `formula-error-watch`; these markers do not omit the intervening G5-G6 fidelity gates.
- G5 model/equation retention and G6 important-visual disposition complete before G7 opens; final artifact presence alone is not evidence of gate order, so the frozen transaction attestation must record this ordering.
- Positive language coverage for every declared substantive chapter.
- At least two saturation passes and zero new reusable moves in the final pass.
- Exact Summary/Abstract and Introduction paragraph-chain counts.
- Positive reusable language count greater than weakness count.
- Weakness substitution explicitly forbidden for organization coverage.
- Exact model and equation retention even when language remains the largest dimension.
- Exact important-visual disposition coverage.
- AI Use Report and generated disclosure language excluded from author-language counts.

Test every promoted language template for:

- Source evidence resolves to a validated language unit of the same A-F type and chapter.
- Occurrence, function, information order, slots, variants, and boundary are populated.
- Two original instantiations use different scenarios and vocabulary.
- No full source paragraph, source clause, or distinctive eight-content-word run is copied.
- The original examples preserve rhetorical function without preserving source-specific facts.
- Recurring status requires support from at least two independent paper contents, not duplicate files.

Rolling drift audit:

```text
latest N native paper-learning transactions
        |
        +-- language largest dimension? -------- no -> fail
        +-- modeling retained above floor? ----- no -> fail
        +-- important visuals present? --------- no -> fail
        +-- weakness strict minority? ---------- no -> fail
        +-- all yes ----------------------------- pass
```

The rolling window must follow transaction chronology, not filename order. Corrective metadata-only events do not replace the native paper-learning event when selecting the window.

The configured value of `N` is `5`, and the rules are exact:

- Select the latest five **distinct** papers represented by native substantive learning events.
- Skip metadata-only correction events.
- If the same paper later receives a substantive correction with frozen language/model/visual counts, replace its earlier event and move it to the correction event's chronological position.
- Require `language > max(each non-language dimension)`. Equality is drift and must fail.
- Require `weakness < language` both in the rolling aggregate and, through the per-paper audit validator, for each paper.
- Require rolling model and important-visual counts to be nonzero as a drift alarm.
- Separately require per-paper exact model/equation retention and exact important-visual disposition equality. A nonzero rolling count is never proof of completeness.
- Take language/model/visual counts from frozen transaction artifact counts when available; use legacy observation counts only for events that predate those fields and label the basis.

### 19.8 Transaction, Hash, and Recovery Tests

Test the transaction system for:

- First event has the correct genesis state.
- Every later event references the exact preceding event hash.
- Canonical event hashing detects any history mutation.
- Duplicate closure events and invalid correction events are rejected.
- Transaction drafts cannot claim counts that differ from live artifacts.
- Paper-card canonical paths resolve to the exact language and model files.
- Declared versions, counts, chapters, and review statuses match live artifacts.
- Derived compliance detects missing artifacts, duplicates, filename/ID mismatch, semantic audit failures, and path mismatch.
- Artifact-hash verification is a separate hard gate: a post-append byte change requires a later corrective event whose hash matches the corrected file, even when the live semantic compliance report still passes.
- A schema change automatically repopulates migration debt for every affected historical paper.

`current-protocol-compliance.json` is derived from live artifact identity, paths, declarations, and semantic validators; it is not a transaction-hash resolver. History-chain validation protects event immutability, while final-event artifact-hash reconciliation protects frozen bytes. Both gates must pass, and neither result may be reported as proof of the other. Legacy events that name a hash only in narrative text require explicit final-event reconciliation; do not label their entire artifact set structurally hash-bound.

The required recovery simulation is:

```text
valid transaction appended
        -> mutate one declared artifact byte in a disposable fixture
        -> artifact-hash verification fails and blocks queue expansion
        -> live semantic compliance is rerun independently and its result is reported separately
        -> create corrective event
        -> validate and append
        -> artifact-hash verification passes and compliance is current-validated
        -> both historical hashes remain auditable
```

## 20. Forward-Testing Program

### 20.1 Holdout Design

Use rolling temporal holdouts during study:

1. Build a provisional type branch from earlier years.
2. Before incorporating a selected newer paper, treat it as a hidden comparison target.
3. Ask the provisional skill to propose a solution architecture for that official problem.
4. Compare coverage, novelty, validation, writing logic, and visualization plan with the held-out top paper.
5. Record gaps without claiming the historical paper is the only correct solution.
6. Then study and incorporate the held-out paper normally.

This tests generalization without permanently excluding any paper from the final learning set.

### 20.2 Realistic Task Families

Forward tests should include:

- Decompose a new A-F problem.
- Choose and justify a model stack.
- Improve a weak assumption set.
- Design sensitivity and robustness analysis.
- Draft an abstract from actual results.
- Rewrite a model section for rigor.
- Select and specify advanced visualizations.
- Critique a full outline.
- Diagnose logical gaps in a draft.
- Create a reproducibility checklist.

### 20.3 Adversarial Tests

Test failure resistance:

- Ask for a flashy model unsupported by data.
- Ask to classify an ambiguously named corpus paper by topic.
- Ask for a judge-preference claim with no evidence.
- Provide inconsistent units.
- Provide a result with no uncertainty.
- Request an advanced chart that would distort the data.
- Ask the skill to copy source prose.
- Supply an explicit type that conflicts with topic stereotypes.

The skill should refuse the invalid premise, explain the issue concisely, and offer a valid route.

### 20.4 Independent Subagent Validation

When the skill reaches a substantial revision, forward-test with fresh subagents using raw tasks and the skill artifact, not the expected answer or prior diagnosis. Use separate test threads and inspect emitted artifacts. Avoid contamination by removing test outputs before the next independent pass. If a test would consume substantial time or alter live systems, obtain approval first.

### 20.5 Evaluation Rubric

Score outputs on:

- Requirement coverage.
- Type correctness.
- Mathematical coherence.
- Assumption defensibility.
- Model-selection reasoning.
- Validation adequacy.
- Result traceability.
- Writing precision.
- Visualization fit.
- Reproducibility.
- Evidence honesty.
- Practical usefulness under competition constraints.

Use anchored examples for weak, acceptable, and excellent performance rather than vague numeric ratings.

## 22. Handling Difficult Cases

### 22.1 Image-Only or Broken PDFs

- Mark the paper blocked.
- Preserve the source hash and path.
- Attempt approved OCR or alternative extraction.
- Verify OCR against rendered pages, especially formulas.
- Do not infer missing text.
- Close only after required fields can be supported.

### 22.2 Missing Problem Statements

- Analyze the paper's explicit restatement.
- Mark the official task reconstruction as incomplete.
- Avoid claims about unobserved requirements.
- Revisit if an authoritative statement is later found.

### 22.3 Ambiguous Award Status

- Preserve folder and filename signals.
- Consult results documents as context if they explicitly resolve status.
- Do not broaden scope from content quality.
- Keep unresolved papers quarantined rather than silently including them.

### 22.4 Ambiguous A-F Label

- Never use topic knowledge.
- Check path normalization and renamed-folder history if available.
- Use explicit filename or authoritative folder metadata only.
- Quarantine unresolved conflicts.

### 22.5 Incomplete or Poor Top Papers

- Complete the same paper card.
- Preserve useful practices.
- Record omissions and negative lessons.
- Do not lower evidence standards because of award status.

### 22.6 Conflicting Equations or Notation

- Check extraction against the rendered page.
- Preserve the paper's notation separately from normalized notation.
- State the inconsistency.
- Do not silently repair the source.
- Offer a corrected extension only with an `E` label.

## 23. Efficiency Without Mechanical Learning

### 23.1 Safe Automation

Automate:

- File census and hashing.
- PDF metadata extraction.
- Page-aware text extraction.
- Candidate heading detection.
- Schema validation.
- Link and tag linting.
- Progress and coverage reports.
- Search indexes.

### 23.2 Human-or-Agent Judgment Required

Do not automatically promote:

- Claimed innovations.
- Judge preferences.
- Model suitability.
- Assumption quality.
- Logical coherence.
- Visualization effectiveness.
- Language quality.
- Cross-paper universality.

Automated frequency, clustering, or language-model suggestions can propose candidates only.

### 23.3 Context Management

For each paper transaction, load only:

- The current paper and problem statement.
- Its existing type branch.
- The active schemas.
- Directly relevant contradiction records.

Do not load the whole 435-paper knowledge base into every analysis. Use IDs, indexes, search, and progressive disclosure.

### 23.4 Checkpointing

Checkpoint after each committed paper or migration transaction. A checkpoint must include both paper-state axes, a staged artifact manifest, artifact hashes, validator attestations, type-branch diff, language-priority audit, unresolved items, and the last fully committed event ID. If interrupted, resume first from `correction-required`, then from the earliest `closed` paper that is not `protocol-current-validated`, and only then from the first non-closed queue item. Never infer completion from the presence of draft files.

### 23.5 Failure Modes and Required Recovery

| Failure mode | Observable symptom | Required recovery | Forbidden shortcut |
|---|---|---|---|
| Provider or subagent unavailable | 429/503, timeout, empty response, no formal artifact | Retry within a bounded window or return the task to the root; resume from live files | Dropping the paper, lowering coverage, or treating a failed agent as completion |
| Partial subagent write | Some of language/model/audit/card exists | Quarantine the partial state, rerun validators, complete the same paper before append | Inferring completion from file presence |
| Shared-state write conflict | History/report/plan changes during parallel work | Stop writers, select one transaction owner, re-read live files, and serialize integration | Merging two independently appended histories |
| OCR or extraction gap | Blank page text, broken equation, missing heading | Inspect rendered page, record ambiguity, use manual transcription with confidence | Inventing text or equation content |
| AI-language contamination | AI Use Report or copied prompt counted as author language | Remove it from positive counts and rerun saturation/layout audit | Treating disclosure prose as style evidence |
| Language drift | Models or weaknesses dominate a rolling window | Stop queue expansion, re-open affected paper language passes, and rerun drift audit | Adding superficial language units only to change counts |
| False organization coverage | Logic artifacts list only errors or topics | Reconstruct positive global, section, paragraph, and argument chains | Relabeling weaknesses as logic |
| Formula serialization defect | Loaded LaTeX contains unintended double escaping or changed semantics | Visually compare, correct the artifact, revalidate, and append a corrective event if already frozen | Silent byte replacement after append |
| Card path/count mismatch | Derived compliance reports `correction-required` despite validators passing | Correct declarations, revalidate, append corrective transaction, rebuild compliance | Editing the old transaction hash or ignoring the derived report |
| Schema migration debt | Previously closed paper becomes partial or correction-required | Freeze queue expansion and migrate the dynamically derived set in deterministic order | Keeping an optimistic manual debt list |
| Transaction append interrupted | Draft exists but no durable event or report update | Lock history; validate the chain; compare the last durable event's paper, event type, changed fields, frozen hashes/counts, and validator attestations with the draft; if it is the same event, do not append, otherwise revalidate the draft and append once; then rebuild compliance | Re-appending from sequence alone, appending without the exclusive lock, or declaring completion from the draft |
| Post-append artifact change | Current bytes differ from event hash | Create a new corrective event that supersedes the old hash | Rewriting history or hiding the mismatch |

### 23.6 Parallel Lanes and Conflict Boundaries

Within the one active paper transaction, use these lanes:

| Lane | Work | Writes | Depends on |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | Current paper G0-G8 artifact work by the sole designated writer | Only the current paper's staged artifacts | Fixed source/chapter map and schemas |
| B | Read-only language-priority and organization review | None | Frozen current-paper review target |
| C | Read-only transaction and recovery review | None | Frozen draft and history |
| Root integration | Re-read, final validators, freeze, hashes, transaction append, compliance rebuild, and verification | Shared history/report and final plan | Writer stopped and all read-only reviews complete |

Do not launch a second paper-learning lane. Root integration is always sequential and closes the current paper before the next deterministic paper is selected:

`Lane A + read-only B/C -> stop writers -> root re-read -> validate -> freeze -> hash -> append -> rebuild compliance -> verify paper + chain -> select next paper`

The type-branch update belongs to the same current-paper transaction and is serialized by the designated writer or root. Shared type-branch writes never overlap, even when mechanical preprocessing for another paper is running.
